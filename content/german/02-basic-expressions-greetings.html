Basic Expressions & Greetings

Every conversation starts with a greeting — and in German, your very first word choice signals whether you're being formal or casual. This lesson covers hello and goodbye, how to ask "how are you," and the crucial formal/informal divide that shapes German social interaction far more than it does in English.

Grammar

German has two ways to say "you": du (informal, singular) and Sie (formal, singular and plural, always capitalized). Choosing the wrong one isn't just a grammar slip — it's a social signal.

  • Use du with family, friends, children, fellow students, and increasingly with younger colleagues.
  • Use Sie with strangers, in business settings, with anyone older than you in a formal context, and by default with anyone you're not sure about. Germans generally wait to be invited to switch to du.
  • Greetings themselves often shift with formality: Guten Tag is safe and formal-neutral; Hallo is casual; regional greetings like Servus (southern Germany/Austria) or Moin (northern Germany) are casual and local.

Examples

Guten Tag, ich heiße Anna.

Hello, my name is Anna. (formal setting)

Hallo, wie geht's dir?

Hi, how's it going? (informal, note "dir" — covered in Lesson 3 of Part 3)

Wie geht es Ihnen, Herr Schmidt?

How are you, Mr. Schmidt? (formal)

Sehr gut, danke, und Ihnen?

Very well, thanks, and you? (formal reply)

Tschüss, bis morgen!

Bye, see you tomorrow!

Notes

Guten Tag works at almost any time of day and in almost any setting — when in doubt, use it. Saving Hallo and Tschüss for people you already know casually is a safe rule of thumb while you calibrate your ear for German social register.

Practice

  1. Write a short formal exchange: greet someone, ask their name, ask how they are, and say goodbye — all using Sie-forms.
  2. Rewrite that same exchange as an informal conversation between friends using du-forms.
  3. Conjugate heißen for du and for wir without looking at the table.
  4. When would you choose Guten Abend over Guten Tag?
  5. Translate: "Excuse me, what is your name?" (formal)
