Possessive Adjectives

Possessive adjectives — my, your, his, her, our, their — work almost exactly like the indefinite article ein/eine/ein in German, which means if you've already internalized ein-endings, you're most of the way to mastering possessives too. This lesson maps out the full possessive set and shows how their endings shift across all four cases.

Possessive adjectives take endings that agree with the gender, number, and case of the noun that follows them — and those endings follow the exact same pattern as the indefinite article ein, which you already know from Lessons 13–17.

Nominative: no ending before masculine/neuter nouns; add -e before feminine/plural nouns.

  • Das ist mein Bruder. (This is my brother.) / Das ist meine Mutter. (This is my mother.)

Accusative: add -en before masculine nouns (matching ein→einen); add -e before feminine/plural.

  • Sie trifft ihren Bruder. (She meets her brother.) / Sie trifft ihre Schwester. (She meets her sister.)

Dative: add -em before masculine/neuter nouns; add -er before feminine; add -en in the plural.

  • Er spricht mit seinem Bruder. (He talks with his brother.) / Er spricht mit seiner Schwester. (He talks with his sister.)

Genitive: add -es before masculine/neuter nouns; add -er before feminine/plural (rarely used in speech, per Lesson 17).

  • Das ist der Freund unseres Sohnes. (This is our son's friend.)

One spelling quirk: euer (your, informal plural) drops its middle -e- before feminine or plural endings: Ist das eure Mutter? (Is that your mother?) — not "eure" with the base "euer" stem intact.

Examples

Meine Geschwister wohnen in Berlin.

My siblings live in Berlin.

Sie sieht ihr Kind.

She sees her child.

Er spricht mit seinen Eltern.

He talks with his parents.

Ist das eure Mutter?

Is that your (plural informal) mother?

Das ist der Freund unserer Tochter.

This is our daughter's friend.

Notes

Because possessive endings mirror the ein-endings you already learned for the indefinite article, the fastest way to master this lesson is to mentally substitute "ein" for whichever possessive you're using, apply the ein-ending rule you already know, then swap the possessive back in. If you already know ein → einen for masculine accusative, you already know mein → meinen.

Practice

  1. Give the correct form of "sein" (his) before: der Bruder (nominative), die Schwester (accusative), das Kind (dative).
  2. Translate: "He talks to his sister." (dative)
  3. Fill in the blank: "Ist das ___ Mutter?" using euer correctly adjusted for a feminine noun.
  4. Translate: "This is the friend of our son." (genitive)
  5. Explain the shortcut connecting possessive-adjective endings to the indefinite article ein.
