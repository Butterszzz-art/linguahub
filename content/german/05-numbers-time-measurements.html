Numbers, Time & Measurements

Numbers show up everywhere — prices, addresses, phone numbers, the time, dates. This lesson builds 0–100 plus the essential vocabulary for telling time and talking about days, so you can function in almost any transactional situation from day one.

Grammar

German numbers 21–99 are built by joining the ones digit, "and" (und), and the tens digit — in that order, which is the reverse of English:

  • 21 = einundzwanzig (literally "one-and-twenty")
  • 34 = vierunddreißig ("four-and-thirty")
  • 47 = siebenundvierzig ("seven-and-forty")

This "ones before tens" pattern holds all the way up to 99, and it's the reason many German learners initially write numbers backwards when taking dictation — train your ear for it deliberately.

For time, Germans commonly use the 24-hour clock in formal/written contexts (train schedules, TV listings) and a 12-hour clock in casual speech. Es ist ... Uhr (It is ... o'clock) is the core pattern:

  • Es ist drei Uhr. — It's three o'clock.
  • Es ist halb vier. — It's half past three (literally "half four" — German counts toward the next hour, not from the last one, so halb vier means 3:30, not 4:30).

Examples

Ich bin einundzwanzig Jahre alt.

I am twenty-one years old.

Der Zug fährt um vierzehn Uhr.

The train leaves at 14:00 (2:00 PM).

Wie viel Uhr ist es?

What time is it?

Es ist halb neun.

It's 8:30.

Heute ist Montag, morgen ist Dienstag.

Today is Monday, tomorrow is Tuesday.

Notes

The halb (half) construction trips up almost every beginner at least once, because it points forward to the next hour rather than back to the last one, the opposite of how "half past" works in English. Drill it in isolation before mixing it with other time expressions.

Practice

  1. Write out the numbers 21, 34, 58, and 99 in German.
  2. What time is halb sechs in standard English time notation?
  3. Translate: "The train leaves at 15:00."
  4. List the seven days of the week in German, in order, starting with Monday.
  5. Say your own age in a full German sentence using "Ich bin ... Jahre alt."
