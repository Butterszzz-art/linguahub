Asking Questions (W-Questions and Yes/No)

You already know from Part 1 that German statements follow verb-second order. Questions break that pattern in one of two clean, predictable ways — and in fact, German questions are structurally simpler than English ones, because German never needs a helper word like "do" or "does."

Grammar

Yes/no questions: move the finite verb to the very front of the sentence, ahead of the subject. No "do/does" is needed at all.

  • Statement: Peter kommt aus Berlin. (Peter comes from Berlin.)
  • Question: Kommt Peter aus Berlin? (Does Peter come from Berlin?)
  • Statement: Sie wohnen im Hotel. (You live in the hotel.)
  • Question: Wohnen Sie im Hotel? (Do you live in the hotel?)

W-questions (so named because most German question words start with W) put the question word first, then the finite verb immediately second — the same verb-second logic from Part 1, just with a question word occupying position one instead of a subject or time expression.

The core W-question words:

  • wer (who)
  • was (what)
  • wo (where)
  • wohin (where to)
  • woher (where from)
  • wann (when)
  • warum (why)
  • wie (how)
  • wie viel / wie viele (how much / how many)
  • Wo wohnen Sie? (Where do you live?)
  • Was studieren Sie? (What are you studying?)
  • Wann beginnt das Konzert? (When does the concert start?)
  • Woher kommen Sie? (Where do you come from?)

Note the useful distinction between wo, wohin, and woher: wo asks about a static location ("Wo bist du?" — Where are you?), wohin asks about a destination ("Wohin gehst du?" — Where are you going?), and woher asks about an origin ("Woher kommst du?" — Where are you from?).

Examples

Wie heißen Sie?

What is your name? (formal)

Wo wohnst du?

Where do you live? (informal)

Was sind Sie von Beruf?

What is your profession?

Wie viel kostet das?

How much does that cost?

Sprechen Sie Deutsch?

Do you speak German? (yes/no)

Notes

A genuinely nice feature of German for beginners: since there's no equivalent of English "do/does" in questions, you never have to think about an extra helper verb. If you can form the statement, you can form the question just by moving one word.

Practice

  1. Turn the statement "Du sprichst Deutsch" into a yes/no question.
  2. Turn the statement "Sie wohnt in Hamburg" into a W-question asking where she lives.
  3. Choose the correct question word (wo, wohin, or woher) for: "___ kommst du?" (asking where someone is from)
  4. Translate: "What is your profession?" (formal)
  5. Write an original W-question using wann.
