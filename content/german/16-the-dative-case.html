The Dative Case

The dative marks the indirect object — the person or thing that something is given to, done for, or directed toward. Unlike the accusative, the dative changes the article for every gender, not just masculine, so it takes a bit more deliberate memorization. It's also required after a specific list of common verbs and prepositions, independent of whether there's a "real" indirect object in the English sense.

Grammar

Use the dative case for the indirect object — the "recipient" of the action:

  • Der Lehrer gibt der Frau ein Buch. (The teacher gives the woman a book.)

Unlike the accusative, the dative changes the modifier across all genders, and adds an ending to plural nouns themselves:

Dative endings:

ModifierMasculineFeminineNeuterPlural
definitedem Mannder Fraudem Kindden Gästen
indefiniteeinem Manneiner Fraueinem KindGästen
negativekeinem Mannkeiner Fraukeinem Kindkeinen Gästen
possessivemeinem Manndeiner Frauseinem Kindunseren Gästen

The pattern to memorize: masculine and neuter both take -em; feminine takes -er; plural adds -en to the modifier and -en or -n to the noun itself (die Gäste → den Gästen).

A specific set of common verbs always require a dative object, even though English would treat that object as a direct object: helfen (to help), danken (to thank), gehören (to belong to), folgen (to follow).

  • Ich helfe den Gästen. (I help the guests.) — not "helfe die Gäste," even though "the guests" reads like a direct object in English.
  • Ich danke dem Kind. (I'm thanking the child.)

A cluster of common prepositions also always take the dative: aus (from/out of), außer (apart from), bei (at/near), gegenüber (opposite), mit (with/by), nach (after/to), seit (since/for), von (from), zu (to).

  • Ich fahre mit dem Auto. (I go by car.)
  • Er wohnt gegenüber der Kirche. (He lives opposite the church.)
  • Wir fahren zu meiner Freundin. (We're going to my girlfriend's.)

As with the accusative, a sentence can contain multiple datives if both a dative verb and a dative preposition are present:

  • Ich helfe dem Jungen mit seinem Mantel. (I help the boy with his coat.)

Examples

Ich folge dem Mann.

I follow the man.

Das Baby gleicht der Mutter.

The baby resembles the mother.

Wie komme ich zum Bahnhof?

How do I get to the train station? (zum = zu + dem, a common contraction)

Das gehört dem Mann.

That belongs to the man.

Er dankt dem Mitarbeiter.

He thanks the colleague.

Notes

Watch for contracted forms in everyday speech: zu + dem becomes zum, zu + der becomes zur, bei + dem becomes beim, von + dem becomes vom. These contractions are extremely common in spoken and written German, so recognizing them on sight will save you from momentary confusion later.

Practice

  1. Give the dative article for: der Mann, die Frau, das Kind, die Gäste (plural).
  2. Fill in the blank: "Er fährt jeden Tag mit ___ Auto." (dem, masculine dative)
  3. Translate: "I thank you (formal)." using danken + dative
  4. Why does "Ich helfe den Gästen" use the dative, when "help the guests" looks like a direct object in English?
  5. Expand zum and zur into their full two-word forms.
