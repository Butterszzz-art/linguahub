Nouns, Gender & Articles

This is the single most important grammar habit to build early: every German noun has a grammatical gender — masculine, feminine, or neuter — and that gender is grammatical, not biological. Der Tisch (the table) is masculine; die Tür (the door) is feminine; das Fenster (the window) is neuter. None of that maps to anything in the real world — you simply have to learn it. The good news: you learn it once, per word, and it never changes.

Grammar

Every German noun is always capitalized, no matter where it falls in the sentence — this is unique to German among major European languages and is a huge reading aid once you get used to it.

The three genders and their definite articles ("the"):

  • der — masculine (der Mann, the man; der Tisch, the table)
  • die — feminine (die Frau, the woman; die Tür, the door)
  • das — neuter (das Kind, the child; das Fenster, the window)

Indefinite articles ("a/an"):

  • ein — masculine and neuter (ein Mann, ein Kind)
  • eine — feminine (eine Frau)

Some patterns help predict gender, though they're guidelines, not guarantees:

  • Nouns ending in -e are very often feminine (die Lampe, die Schule)
  • Nouns ending in -chen or -lein are always neuter, regardless of meaning — das Mädchen (the girl) is neuter, because -chen makes it a "diminutive," not because of the person's actual gender
  • Nouns ending in -ung, -heit, -keit, -schaft, -tion are always feminine (die Zeitung, die Freiheit)
  • Days, months, and seasons are almost always masculine (der Montag, der Januar, der Sommer)

Since patterns only cover some words, the single best habit you can build is: never learn a noun without its article. Don't write "Tisch = table" in your notes — write "der Tisch = table."

Examples

Der Tisch ist groß.

The table is big.

Die Tür ist offen.

The door is open.

Das Kind spielt.

The child is playing.

Ich habe ein Buch.

I have a book. (ein — neuter, das Buch)

Sie hat eine Katze.

She has a cat. (eine — feminine, die Katze)

Notes

Native speakers genuinely do not think about gender as they speak — it becomes automatic through repetition, not memorized rules. Flashcards that pair each noun with a color (blue for der, red for die, green for das) are a well-tested trick for building that automatic recall faster than rules alone.

Practice

  1. Assign der, die, or das to: Buch, Katze, Zeitung, Mädchen, Sommer, Schule.
  2. Explain why das Mädchen is neuter even though it means "girl."
  3. Write three sentences using der Tisch, die Tür, and das Fenster as subjects.
  4. Give the indefinite article (ein or eine) for: Haus, Frau, Freund.
  5. Which noun ending almost always signals feminine gender: -chen, -ung, or -er?
