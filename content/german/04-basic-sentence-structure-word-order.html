Basic Sentence Structure & Word Order

German word order feels flexible at first glance — you can often move parts of a sentence around — but it is governed by one iron rule: the conjugated verb is always the second element in a normal statement. Master that single rule now, and a huge amount of "German sentences sound backwards" confusion disappears.

Grammar

Verb-second (V2) word order: In a statement, the conjugated verb occupies position two — but "position two" means the second grammatical element, not necessarily the second word.

  • Ich trinke Kaffee. (I drink coffee.) — Ich is element one, trinke is element two.
  • Heute trinke ich Kaffee. (Today I drink coffee.) — Heute is element one, trinke is still element two, and ich moves after the verb.

This is different from English, where you'd say "Today I drink coffee" with the verb in third position. German always snaps the verb back to second position, no matter what you put first.

Yes/no questions flip the verb to the very front:

  • Trinkst du Kaffee? — Do you drink coffee?

W-questions (question words) also put the verb second, right after the question word:

  • Was trinkst du? — What do you drink?

Negation with nicht typically goes near the end of the sentence, after the object but before other verb parts:

  • Ich trinke keinen Kaffee. (I don't drink coffee — using kein, covered in Part 5)
  • Ich verstehe das nicht. — I don't understand that.

Examples

Ich lerne Deutsch.

I am learning German.

Jetzt lerne ich Deutsch.

Now I am learning German. (jetzt takes slot one, verb stays in slot two)

Lernst du Deutsch?

Are you learning German? (verb-first for yes/no question)

Was lernst du?

What are you learning? (question word, then verb)

Ich verstehe die Frage nicht.

I don't understand the question.

Notes

A useful mental trick: think of the conjugated verb as a fixed anchor in position two, and everything else — subject included — as furniture you can rearrange around it depending on what you want to emphasize. What comes first is often the most emphasized information in the sentence.

Practice

  1. Rewrite "Ich trinke Kaffee" starting with "Heute" instead of "Ich," keeping correct word order.
  2. Turn "Du lernst Deutsch" into a yes/no question.
  3. Turn "Du lernst Deutsch" into a question starting with was.
  4. Identify the verb-second element in: "Morgen fahre ich nach Berlin."
  5. Explain, in your own words, why German verb-second order is different from English word order, using one of your own example sentences.
