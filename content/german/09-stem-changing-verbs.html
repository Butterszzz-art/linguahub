Stem-Changing Verbs

A specific group of common German verbs change their stem vowel — but only in the du and er/sie/es forms. This looks unpredictable at first, but it actually falls into three tidy patterns: a→ä, e→i, and e→ie. Learn the patterns, and a huge list of "irregular" verbs suddenly becomes learnable in groups rather than one at a time.

Grammar

The rule that applies to every stem-changing verb: the change appears only in du and er/sie/es. Every other person (ich, wir, ihr, sie/Sie) uses the completely regular stem and ending.

Pattern 1 — a → ä (appears in verbs whose stem vowel is "a"):

  • fahren (to drive) → du fährst, er/sie/es fährt
  • tragen (to carry/wear) → du trägst, er/sie/es trägt
  • waschen (to wash) → du wäschst, er/sie/es wäscht
  • schlafen (to sleep) → du schläfst, er/sie/es schläft

Pattern 2 — e → i:

  • sprechen (to speak) → du sprichst, er/sie/es spricht
  • essen (to eat) → du isst, er/sie/es isst
  • geben (to give) → du gibst, er/sie/es gibt
  • helfen (to help) → du hilfst, er/sie/es hilft
  • nehmen (to take) is this pattern with extra spelling changes: du nimmst, er/sie/es nimmt

Pattern 3 — e → ie (used when the "e" sound is long):

  • sehen (to see) → du siehst, er/sie/es sieht
  • lesen (to read) → du liest, er/sie/es liest
  • empfehlen (to recommend) → du empfiehlst, er/sie/es empfiehlt

There is no shortcut to knowing which verbs belong to which pattern beyond memorization and exposure — but once you know a verb changes, the specific pattern (a→ä, e→i, or e→ie) is fully predictable.

Examples

Sprichst du Deutsch?

Do you speak German?

Sie isst gern Pizza.

She likes eating pizza.

Nimmst du Kaffee oder Tee?

Are you having coffee or tea?

Er sieht Jutta nicht.

He doesn't see Jutta.

Du fährst morgen nach Hause.

You're driving home tomorrow.

Notes

The verb nehmen is worth memorizing on its own: it follows the e→i logic but also drops an "h" and doubles the "m" — du nimmst, er/sie/es nimmt. It's common enough (used for "taking" food, transportation, medicine, and more) that it's worth drilling by itself rather than lumping it in with geben and helfen.

Practice

  1. Sort these verbs by pattern (a→ä, e→i, or e→ie): tragen, geben, lesen, waschen, helfen, sehen.
  2. Conjugate essen for du and er.
  3. Translate: "He carries the bag." (tragen)
  4. Why does nehmen need extra attention beyond the standard e→i rule?
  5. Write a full sentence using fahren correctly conjugated for du.
