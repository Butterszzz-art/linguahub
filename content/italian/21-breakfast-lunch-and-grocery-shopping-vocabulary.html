Breakfast, Lunch, and Grocery Shopping Vocabulary

Building on Lesson 19's meal structure, this lesson fills in concrete food vocabulary — for breakfast pastries, classic pasta shapes, and shopping at specialty food shops like the butcher, fishmonger, and bakery.

Grammar

  • At specialty shops, quantities are usually asked for by weight: un etto (100 grams, Lesson 17), mezzo chilo (half a kilo), un chilo (a kilo). Ask with Vorrei + [quantity] + di + [item]: Vorrei mezzo chilo di prosciutto.
  • Pasta comes in dry, factory-made forms (pasta secca — spaghetti, penne, fusilli, rigatoni) and fresh, often homemade forms (pasta fresca / pasta all'uovo — tagliatelle, fettuccine). "Al dente" (to the tooth) describes pasta cooked just firm enough to have some bite.
  • Common specialty shops: la macelleria (the butcher's), la pescheria (the fishmonger's), la panetteria (the bakery), l'alimentari (the small general grocery). Many Italians still shop at these instead of — or in addition to — il supermercato.

Examples

Vorrei un etto di prosciutto e mezzo chilo di pane.

vohr-RAY oon EHT-toh dee proh-SHOOT-toh eh MEHD-dzoh KEE-loh dee PAH-neh

I'd like 100 grams of prosciutto and half a kilo of bread.

Preferisco la pasta fresca alla pasta secca.

preh-feh-REES-koh lah PAHS-tah FREHS-kah AHL-lah PAHS-tah SEHK-kah

I prefer fresh pasta to dried pasta.

La pasta dev'essere al dente.

lah PAHS-tah deh-VEHS-seh-reh ahl DEHN-teh

The pasta needs to be al dente.

Vado in macelleria a comprare il pollo.

VAH-doh een mah-chehl-leh-REE- ah ah kohm-PRAH-reh eel POHL-loh

I'm going to the butcher's to buy chicken.

Notes

  • "Al dente" isn't just restaurant jargon — it's the standard, expected way pasta is cooked throughout Italy; soft, overcooked pasta is considered a mistake, not a preference.
  • When shopping by weight, un etto (100g) is a genuinely common everyday unit at delis — don't default only to grams or kilos when ordering.
