False Friends and Classic Mistakes to Avoid

This final lesson collects the most common traps for English-speaking learners of Italian: words that look like English but mean something different, and habits worth breaking early. Think of it as a checklist to revisit periodically as you continue studying.

Grammar

  • "False friends" (falsi amici) are words that resemble an English word but carry a different meaning — the opposite of the reliable cognates from Lesson 3. Some of the most common:
    simpatico/a -> does NOT mean "sympathetic"; it means "nice, likeable, pleasant" (someone you enjoy being around)
    sensibile -> does NOT mean "sensible"; it means "sensitive." For "sensible" (reasonable), use ragionevole instead.
    educato/a -> does NOT mean "educated" (in the sense of schooling); it means "well-mannered, polite." For "education" (schooling), use istruzione.
    vecchio/a -> means "old," but is typically reserved for objects, not people; for describing an older PERSON respectfully, use anziano/a instead.
    parenti -> does NOT mean "parents" (that's genitori); parenti means "relatives" broadly (aunts, uncles, cousins, etc.).
  • A grammar habit worth double-checking: the verb "to play" splits into giocare (games/sports) and suonare (instruments) — see Lesson 32 — this remains one of the most persistent errors even at intermediate levels.
  • A cultural/pragmatic note rather than a strict grammar rule: literal, word-for-word translation from English often produces grammatically correct but unnatural-sounding Italian. When in doubt, favor the shorter, more direct phrasing you've seen modeled throughout this course (e.g., Ti piace Roma? rather than a more literal, padded translation of "How do you like Rome?").

Examples

Il mio collega è molto simpatico.

eel MEE-oh kohl-LEH-gah eh MOHL-toh seem-PAH-tee-koh

My colleague is very nice/likeable.

Non è vecchio, è anziano — abbi rispetto!

nohn eh VEHK-kyoh, eh ahn-TSYAH-noh — AHB-bee rees-PEHT-toh

He's not "old," he's elderly — show some respect!

Tutti i miei parenti vengono per Natale.

TOOT-tee ee MYEH-ee pah-REHN-tee VEHN-goh-noh pehr nah-TAH-leh

All my relatives are coming for Christmas.

È una persona molto in gamba.

eh OO-nah pehr-SOH-nah MOHL-toh een GAHM-bah

She's a very sharp, capable person.

Notes

  • Keep a running personal list of false friends as you encounter new ones in reading or conversation — they're highly individual to each learner's native language background, and English speakers hit this particular set (simpatico, sensibile, educato, parenti) more often than most.
  • This lesson closes the course, but real fluency comes from continued exposure — reading, listening, and above all speaking. Revisit earlier lessons' vocabulary in new combinations rather than treating any single lesson as "finished" once read.

Practice

Correct these mismatches: "My parents are visiting" (careful: parenti or genitori?); "She's very sensible" (careful: sensibile or ragionevole?); "He's well-educated" (careful: educato or istruito?).
