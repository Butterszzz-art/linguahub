Consonants and Consonant Combinations

Most Italian consonants sound like their English counterparts, but a handful change pronunciation depending on which vowel follows them. This lesson gives you the two rules (for C and G) that unlock hundreds of words, plus the special consonant clusters GN, GL, and SC.

Grammar

  • C and G each have a "hard" and "soft" sound, and the vowel that follows decides which one you use:
    C + a, o, u, or a consonant -> hard "k" sound: casa (KAH-zah), colpa (KOHL-pah), cuore (KWOH-reh)
    C + e, i -> soft "ch" sound (as in "church"): cena (CHEH-nah), cibo (CHEE-boh)
    G + a, o, u, or a consonant -> hard "g" sound: gamba (GAHM-bah), gomma (GOHM-mah)
    G + e, i -> soft "j" sound (as in "jump"): gelato (jeh-LAH-toh), giorno (JOHR-noh)
  • To get a HARD c or g sound before e or i, insert an h: che (keh) (what), spaghetti (spah-GEHT-tee), ghiaccio (GYAHCH-choh) (ice).
  • To get a SOFT ch or j sound before a, o, or u, insert a silent i: ciao (chow), giacca (JAHK-kah) (jacket) — the i is not pronounced, it just signals the soft sound.
  • Special clusters:
    GN -> like "ny" in "canyon": gnocchi (NYOHK-kee), bagno (BAH-nyoh) (bathroom)
    GL (before i) -> like "lli" in "million": famiglia (fah-MEE-lyah) (family), figlio (FEE-lyoh) (son)
    SC + e/i -> "sh": scena (SHEH-nah) (scene), pesce (PEH-sheh) (fish)
    SC + a/o/u/h -> "sk": scuola (SKWOH-lah) (school), scherzo (SKEHR-tsoh) (joke)
  • Double consonants are held longer than single ones, and this genuinely changes meaning: nono (NOH-noh) (ninth) vs. nonno (NOHN-noh) (grandfather); capello (kah-PEHL-loh) (hair) vs. cappello (kahp-PEHL-loh) (hat).
  • R is trilled/tapped at the front of the mouth, not pronounced in the throat like English or French r.
  • H is always silent on its own — its only job is to switch c/g between hard and soft.

Examples

Buongiorno! Come va la famiglia?

bwohn-JOHR-noh! KOH-meh vah lah fah-MEE-lyah?

Good morning! How's the family?

Vado a scuola con mio figlio.

VAH-doh ah SKWOH-lah kohn MEE-oh FEE-lyoh

I'm going to school with my son.

Mangiamo il pesce stasera.

mahn-JAH-moh eel PEH-sheh stah-SEH-rah

We're eating fish tonight.

Notes

  • The most common beginner slip is treating GLI like the English "gl" in "glass" — it should sound closer to the "lli" in "million." Practice famiglia and figlio slowly.
  • Doubled consonants are not optional flourishes — capello/cappello and pena/penna (pen) are genuinely different words, so lean into the double sound rather than rushing past it.

Practice

Sort these words by whether their C or G is hard or soft, then say them aloud: cena, cane, gente, gatto, ciao, gara, cibo, gonna.
