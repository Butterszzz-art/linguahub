Handling Medical Emergencies

Nobody wants to need this vocabulary, but knowing it in advance removes a lot of stress if you ever do. This lesson covers describing symptoms, body parts, and getting help at a doctor's office or pharmacy.

Grammar

  • Basic body part vocabulary uses the regular gender patterns from Lesson 4, but several very common body parts have IRREGULAR plurals worth memorizing directly: il braccio -> le braccia (arms), il dito -> le dita (fingers), il ginocchio -> le ginocchia (knees), l'orecchio -> le orecchie (ears), la mano -> le mani (hands, irregular because mano is feminine despite its -o ending).
  • To say something hurts, Italian has two common constructions:
    fare male (to hurt) + indirect pronoun: Mi fa male la gamba. (My leg hurts.) Use fa for one thing hurting, fanno for multiple: Mi fanno male gli occhi. (My eyes hurt.)
    avere mal di + [body part, no article]: Ho mal di testa. (I have a headache.) Ho mal di stomaco. (I have a stomachache.)
  • To say you feel sick or unwell, use the reflexive verb sentirsi (Lesson 31): Mi sento male. (I feel sick.) Non mi sento bene. (I don't feel well.)
  • Asking for help: Ho bisogno di un medico. (I need a doctor.) Dov'è il pronto soccorso? (Where's the emergency room?) Chiamate un'ambulanza! (Call an ambulance! — voi imperative, Lesson 36, since you may be addressing anyone nearby)

Examples

Mi fa male la gamba da ieri.

mee fah MAH-leh lah GAHM-bah dah YEH-ree

My leg has hurt since yesterday.

Ho mal di testa e mal di stomaco.

oh mahl dee TEHS-tah eh mahl dee STOH-mah-koh

I have a headache and a stomachache.

Non mi sento bene, ho bisogno di un medico.

nohn mee SEHN-toh BEH-neh, oh bee-ZOH-nyoh dee oon MEH-dee-koh

I don't feel well, I need a doctor.

Chiamate un'ambulanza, per favore!

kyah-MAH-teh oo-nahm-boo-LAHN-tsah, pehr fah-VOH-reh

Call an ambulance, please!

Notes

  • The irregular plurals for body parts (braccia, dita, ginocchia, orecchie) are genuinely exceptions to normal noun rules — they were originally neuter nouns in Latin, which is why they behave differently; simply memorize them as a set.
  • Fare male vs. avere mal di both work for describing pain, but avere mal di is generally reserved for a fixed set of common ailments (testa, stomaco, schiena, denti, gola), while fare male is more flexible and can describe any body part.

Practice

Describe three different symptoms using both constructions (fare male and avere mal di), then write a short sentence asking for a doctor who speaks English.
