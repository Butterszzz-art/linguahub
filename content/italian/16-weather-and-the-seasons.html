Weather and the Seasons

Talking about weather is universal small talk, and Italian leans heavily on the verb fare (to do/make) for weather expressions, echoing the fare idioms you'll expand on later. This lesson covers the four seasons and how to describe conditions.

Grammar

  • Many weather expressions use fare (to do/make), used impersonally in the third person: fa bel tempo (the weather is nice, lit. "it makes nice weather"), fa caldo (it's hot), fa freddo (it's cold), fa brutto tempo (the weather is bad).
  • Some weather verbs stand alone (no fare needed): piove (it's raining, from piovere, to rain), nevica (it's snowing, from nevicare, to snow), tira vento (it's windy).
  • To ask about the weather: Che tempo fa? (What's the weather like?) or Com'è il tempo oggi? (How's the weather today?)
  • The four seasons: la primavera (spring), l'estate (summer), l'autunno (fall/autumn), l'inverno (winter). Use in + season (no article) to say "in [season]": in primavera, in estate, in autunno, in inverno — though d'estate and d'inverno are also common contracted forms.

Examples

Che tempo fa oggi? Fa molto caldo.

keh TEHM-poh fah OHJ-jee? fah MOHL-toh KAHL-doh

What's the weather like today? It's very hot.

D'inverno nevica spesso in montagna.

deen-VEHR-noh NEH-vee-kah SPEHS-soh een mohn-TAH-nyah

In winter it often snows in the mountains.

Piove, porta l'ombrello!

PYOH-veh, POHR-tah lohm-BREHL-loh

It's raining, bring an umbrella!

Le piace Milano? Sì, ma non il clima; fa molto freddo in inverno.

leh PYAH-cheh mee-LAH-noh? see, mah nohn eel KLEE-mah; fah MOHL-toh FREHD-doh een een-VEHR-noh

Do you like Milan? Yes, but not the climate; it's very cold in winter.

Notes

  • Don't try to translate "it's raining" word-for-word with essere — piove is a standalone impersonal verb form, not "è pioggia."
  • Weather + fare idioms extend the pattern of avere idioms from Lesson 9 (fare una domanda, fare una passeggiata) — fare is a genuinely multi-purpose verb worth watching for throughout the course.
