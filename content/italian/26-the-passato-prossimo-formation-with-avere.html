The Passato Prossimo — Formation with Avere

This is your first past tense, and the one you'll use most often to talk about completed actions — "I ate," "we studied," "she called." It's a compound tense built from two parts, and this lesson covers the larger group of verbs that form it with avere.

Grammar

  • The passato prossimo ("recent past") corresponds to both the English simple past (I spoke) and present perfect (I have spoken) — Italian doesn't distinguish between them the way English does.
  • It's a compound tense with two parts: a helping verb (either avere or essere, conjugated in the present tense) + the past participle of the main verb.
  • Most verbs use avere as their helping verb. Regular past participles are formed from the infinitive stem:
    -are verbs -> -ato: parlare -> parlato
    -ere verbs -> -uto: vivere -> vivuto (though many -ere verbs are irregular here — see below)
    -ire verbs -> -ito: partire -> partito, capire -> capito
  • Full example with parlare: ho parlato, hai parlato, ha parlato, abbiamo parlato, avete parlato, hanno parlato. (I spoke/have spoken, you spoke..., etc.)
  • Many common -ere verbs have IRREGULAR past participles that must be memorized individually. Some of the most frequent:
    fare (to do/make) -> fatto Ho fatto una passeggiata.
    dire (to say/tell) -> detto Ha detto la verità.
    leggere (to read) -> letto Abbiamo letto il libro.
    scrivere (to write) -> scritto Hanno scritto una lettera.
    prendere (to take) -> preso Ho preso un caffè.
    mettere (to put) -> messo Ha messo la giacca.
    vedere (to see) -> visto Hai visto il film?
    aprire (to open) -> aperto Ho aperto la porta.
  • With avere-verbs, the past participle does NOT change to agree with the subject — it stays in its base -o form regardless of who's speaking (Maria ha parlato, just like Marco ha parlato).

Examples

Ieri ho mangiato un gelato buonissimo.

YEH-ree oh mahn-JAH-toh oon jeh-LAH-toh bwoh-NEES-see-moh

Yesterday I ate a delicious ice cream.

Hai già visto quel film?

AH-ee jah VEES-toh kwehl feelm

Have you already seen that film?

Non ho ancora finito i compiti.

nohn oh AHN-koh-rah fee-NEE-toh ee KOHM-pee-tee

I haven't finished my homework yet.

Abbiamo letto due libri questo mese.

ahb-BYAH-moh LEHT-toh DOO-eh LEE-bree KWEHS-toh MEH-zeh

We've read two books this month.

Notes

  • Because irregular participles are so common among high-frequency verbs (fare, dire, leggere, scrivere, prendere, vedere), memorize them as vocabulary items in their own right rather than assuming a regular -uto ending.
  • Time expressions like ieri, già, ancora, and mai are your strongest clue that a sentence needs the passato prossimo — notice them and let them cue the tense.

Practice

Put these into the passato prossimo: io (mangiare) la pizza; tu (leggere) il giornale; noi (fare) una passeggiata; loro (vedere) un film.
