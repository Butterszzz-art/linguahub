Finding a Place to Stay and the Imperative Mood

This closing lesson of Part 4 covers booking and checking into accommodation, and introduces the imperative mood — the form Italian uses for commands, instructions, and requests, which you'll hear constantly from hotel staff, tour guides, and recipes alike.

Grammar

  • Lodging vocabulary: l'albergo/l'hotel (hotel), una camera singola/ doppia (a single/double room), la prenotazione (reservation), la reception (front desk).
  • The imperative is used to give commands, instructions, and (in its polite Lei form) formal requests. Its endings differ from the present tense mainly in the tu and Lei forms:
    -ARE verbs (parlare): (tu) parla!, (Lei) parli!, (noi) parliamo!, (voi) parlate!, (loro) parlino!
    -ERE verbs (prendere): (tu) prendi!, (Lei) prenda!, (noi) prendiamo!, (voi) prendete!, (loro) prendano!
    -IRE verbs (partire): (tu) parti!, (Lei) parta!, (noi) partiamo!, (voi) partite!, (loro) partano! Notice the tu and Lei forms are the ones that most sharply differ from the present tense — the -are tu form ends in -a (not -i as in the present), and the Lei forms across all conjugations borrow endings from the "opposite" verb family (-are verbs get -i, -ere/-ire verbs get -a).
  • The negative tu imperative uses non + INFINITIVE, not the normal negative construction: Non parlare! (Don't speak!) Non aprire la porta! (Don't open the door!) All other persons simply add non before the normal imperative: Non parli! (Lei), Non parlate! (voi).
  • Several verbs have irregular imperative tu forms worth knowing: andare -> va'/vai!, dare -> da'/dai!, dire -> di'!, fare -> fa'/fai!, stare -> sta'/stai! (all also have a regular alternate form).
  • You'll hear the plural voi imperative constantly on signs and in group instructions (chiamate!, aspettate!), and the formal Lei imperative from hotel staff, waitstaff, and officials (Mi dia un documento, per favore — Give me an ID, please).

Examples

Vorrei una camera doppia per due notti.

vohr-RAY OO-nah KAH-meh-rah DOHP-pyah pehr DOO-eh NOHT-tee

I'd like a double room for two nights.

Mi dia un documento, per favore, e compili questo modulo.

mee DEE-ah oon doh-koo-MEHN-toh, pehr fah-VOH-reh, eh kohm-PEE-lee KWEHS-toh MOH-doo-loh

Give me an ID, please, and fill out this form.

Aspetti un momento, controllo la prenotazione.

ahs-PEHT-tee oon moh-MEHN-toh, kohn-TROHL-loh lah preh-noh-tah-TSYOH-neh

Wait a moment, I'll check the reservation.

Non dimenticare la chiave!

nohn dee-mehn-tee-KAH-reh lah KYAH-veh

Don't forget the key!

Notes

  • Because Lei imperatives borrow the "opposite family" ending (-are verbs take -i, -ere/-ire verbs take -a), they can momentarily look like a different verb entirely — Parli! (Lei, from parlare) looks like a tu present-tense form of a different verb, so context matters.
  • The negative tu command switching to the infinitive (Non parlare!, not "Non parli" for tu) is a genuinely irregular quirk worth memorizing as a fixed rule rather than reasoning through each time.
