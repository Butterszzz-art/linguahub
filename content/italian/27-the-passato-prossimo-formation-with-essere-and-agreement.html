The Passato Prossimo — Formation with Essere and Agreement

A smaller but very frequent group of verbs — mostly verbs of motion and state-change — form the passato prossimo with essere instead of avere. This changes one important rule: with essere, the past participle DOES agree with the subject, like an adjective.

Grammar

  • Verbs that take essere as their helper are mostly verbs of motion, staying, or change of state. The most common ones to memorize: andare (to go), venire (to come), partire (to leave), arrivare (to arrive), entrare (to enter), uscire (to go out), tornare (to return), stare (to stay/be), nascere (to be born), morire (to die), diventare (to become), rimanere (to remain). Reflexive verbs (Lesson 31) also always take essere.
  • With essere, the past participle behaves like an adjective and must agree in gender and number with the SUBJECT:
    Marco è andato al mare. (Marco went to the beach. — masc. sg.)
    Maria è andata al mare. (Maria went to the beach. — fem. sg.)
    I ragazzi sono andati al mare. (The boys/mixed group went. — masc. pl.)
    Le ragazze sono andate al mare. (The girls went. — fem. pl.)
  • A handy way to remember which group a verb belongs to: many essere-verbs can be arranged into the classic "House of essere" mnemonic — verbs of movement in and out (entrare/uscire), up and down (salire/scendere), arriving and leaving (arrivare/partire), staying and becoming (stare/ diventare/rimanere), being born and dying (nascere/morire), going and coming (andare/venire), being and seeming (essere/sembrare).
  • Some irregular essere-verb participles: essere -> stato (yes, essere itself uses stato, which doubles as the participle for stare too); nascere -> nato; morire -> morto; venire -> venuto; rimanere -> rimasto.

Examples

Sono arrivato stamattina.

SOH-noh ahr-ree-VAH-toh stah-maht-TEE-nah

I arrived this morning. (male speaker)

A che ora sei tornata ieri sera?

ah keh OH-rah sey tohr-NAH-tah YEH-ree SEH-rah

What time did you get back last night? (asking a female)

Siamo usciti insieme sabato.

SYAH-moh oo-SHEE-tee een-SYEH-meh SAH-bah- toh

We went out together on Saturday. (mixed/male group)

Dove sei nata? Sono nata a Napoli.

DOH-veh sey NAH-tah? SOH-noh NAH-tah ah NAH-poh-lee

Where were you born? I was born in Naples. (female speaker)

Notes

  • The single most useful shortcut: if a verb describes moving somewhere, staying somewhere, or changing state (arriving, leaving, being born, dying, becoming), it's very likely an essere-verb — check that instinct against the list above as you build vocabulary.
  • Don't forget the agreement once you've picked essere — this is the most common error at this stage, since avere-verbs (Lesson 26) never changed the participle, and it's tempting to keep that habit here too.
