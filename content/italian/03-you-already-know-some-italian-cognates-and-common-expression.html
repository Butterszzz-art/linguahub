You Already Know Some Italian — Cognates and Common Expressions

Before diving into grammar, it helps to realize how much Italian you already recognize. Italian and English share a huge number of cognates — words with a common origin that look and often sound alike — plus a set of everyday expressions that are useful from your very first conversation.

Grammar

  • A cognate is a word that shares an origin with — and usually a visible resemblance to — a word in another language. Italian and English share thousands of them because English borrowed heavily from Latin (Italian's direct parent) via French.
  • Watch for reliable patterns: English words ending in -tion often match Italian words ending in -zione (informazione = information; nazione = nation); English -ty often matches Italian -tà (università = university; città = city); English -ble often matches Italian -bile (incredibile = incredible; possibile = possible).
  • Not every similar-looking word is a true cognate — some are "false friends" that look alike but mean different things (see Lesson 44 for a full list). For now, trust the patterns above; they're reliable.
  • Popular, everyday expressions are worth memorizing as fixed chunks even before you understand every grammatical piece inside them.

Examples

lah-eh-roh-POHR-toh eh MOHL-toh eem-pohr-TAHN-teh pehr lah cheet-TAH

The airport is very important for the city.

Notes

  • Lean on cognates to read signs, menus, and simple text right away — your passive vocabulary is much bigger than your active one at this stage.
  • Reserve full trust for false friends until Lesson 44; a few common Italian words (simpatico, educazione, sensibile) look like English words but carry different meanings.

Practice

Guess the English meaning of these Italian words before checking: nazione, possibile, comunicazione, terribile, fantastico. Then use "D'accordo!" and "Va bene!" in a short made-up exchange.
