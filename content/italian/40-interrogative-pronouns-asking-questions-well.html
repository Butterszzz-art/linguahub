Interrogative Pronouns — Asking Questions Well

You've been using individual question words throughout this course (dove, quando, come); this lesson pulls them together systematically and adds a few more, including the tricky che vs. quale distinction.

Grammar

  • Core interrogative words: chi? (who?), che cosa?/cosa?/che? (what? — all three are interchangeable, che cosa being the fullest form), dove? (where?), quando? (when?), come? (how?), perché? (why?), quanto/a/i/e? (how much/many? — agrees with the noun like an adjective), quale/i? (which?).
  • Che vs. quale: che (or che cosa) asks an open-ended "what," with no implied set of options: Che cosa fai? (What are you doing?) Quale asks "which one" from an implied or stated set of choices, and agrees in number: Quale vuoi, questo o quello? (Which do you want, this one or that one?) Quali scarpe preferisci? (Which shoes do you prefer?)
  • Quanto agrees fully like a Type 1 adjective when it modifies a noun: Quanto tempo? (How much time?) Quanti anni? (How many years?) Quante persone? (How many people?)
  • Questions in Italian often don't need a special word-order inversion the way English does — rising intonation alone can turn a statement into a yes/no question: Parli italiano? (Do you speak Italian?) is identical in word order to the statement "Parli italiano" (You speak Italian), distinguished only by intonation (or the question mark in writing).

Examples

Chi ha chiamato? Non lo so.

kee ah kyah-MAH-toh? nohn loh soh

Who called? I don't know.

Quale vestito preferisci, quello rosso o quello blu?

KWAH-leh vehs-TEE-toh preh-feh-REE-shee, KWEHL-loh ROHS-soh oh KWEHL-loh bloo

Which dress do you prefer, the red one or the blue one?

Quante persone vengono alla festa?

KWAHN-teh pehr-SOH-neh VEHN-goh-noh AHL-lah FEHS-tah

How many people are coming to the party?

Perché non vieni con noi?

pehr-KEH nohn VYEH-nee kohn noy

Why aren't you coming with us?

Notes

  • If you're tempted to use "che" when there's an implied set of specific options (a menu, a list of colors, a choice between two things), reach for quale instead — this is the most common che/quale mix-up for learners.
  • Written Italian marks questions with a question mark just like English, but spoken Italian relies heavily on intonation alone for yes/no questions — no auxiliary verb inversion is needed the way English uses "do/does."

Practice

Write five questions, one for each of: chi, quale, quanto, perché, and come, on any topic you like.
