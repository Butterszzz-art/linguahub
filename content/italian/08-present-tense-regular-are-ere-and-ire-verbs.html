Present Tense — Regular -are, -ere, and -ire Verbs

Italian verbs fall into three families based on their infinitive ending: -are, -ere, and -ire. Master the pattern for one verb in each family and you can conjugate hundreds of regular verbs the same way.

Grammar

  • To conjugate a regular verb, drop the infinitive ending (-are, -ere, or -ire) and add the ending that matches the subject.
  • The present tense in Italian covers what English expresses with three different constructions: parlo can mean "I speak," "I do speak," or "I am speaking," depending on context.
  • -ARE verbs (like parlare, to speak): io parlo, tu parli, lui/lei parla, noi parliamo, voi parlate, loro parlano
  • -ERE verbs (like vivere, to live): io vivo, tu vivi, lui/lei vive, noi viviamo, voi vivete, loro vivono
  • -IRE verbs, group 1 (like partire, to leave): io parto, tu parti, lui/lei parte, noi partiamo, voi partite, loro partono
  • -IRE verbs, group 2 — "isc" verbs (like capire, to understand): these insert -isc- between the stem and ending in every form except noi and voi: io capisco, tu capisci, lui/lei capisce, noi capiamo, voi capite, loro capiscono. Other common isc verbs: finire (to finish), preferire (to prefer), pulire (to clean). There's no visual way to predict which -ire verbs take -isc-; you learn it verb by verb.

Examples

Parlo un po' d'italiano.

PAHR-loh oon poh dee-tah-LYAH-noh

I speak a little Italian.

Studiamo insieme ogni sera.

stoo-DYAH-moh een-SYEH-meh OH-nyee SEH-rah

We study together every evening.

Non capisco bene, ma capisco di più ogni giorno.

nohn kah-PEES-koh BEH- neh, mah kah-PEES-koh dee pyoo OH-nyee JOHR-noh

I don't understand well, but I understand more every day.

A che ora parte il treno?

ah keh OH-rah PAHR-teh eel TREH-noh

What time does the train leave?

Notes

  • There's no way to tell from the infinitive alone whether an -ire verb needs -isc-; when learning a new -ire verb, note its type immediately.
  • Because subject pronouns are usually dropped (Lesson 7), the verb ending is doing double duty: it tells you both the action and the subject, so getting endings right matters even more in Italian than in English.

Practice

Conjugate mangiare and preferire (an isc verb) fully in the present tense, then use each in one original sentence.
