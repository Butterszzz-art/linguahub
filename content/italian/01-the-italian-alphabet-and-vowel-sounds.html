The Italian Alphabet and Vowel Sounds

Italian spelling is far more consistent than English spelling — once you learn the rules in this lesson, you can pronounce almost any Italian word correctly on sight, even one you've never seen before. This lesson covers the alphabet and the five vowel sounds that are the backbone of Italian pronunciation.

Grammar

  • The Italian alphabet (l'alfabeto) has only 21 native letters. J, K, W, X, and Y exist only in foreign loanwords (jeans, whisky, taxi, yogurt).
  • Every Italian vowel has exactly ONE pure sound — it never glides the way English vowels do (compare English "go" [gow-oo] to Italian "vado," where the o stays a clean, single "oh").
  • The five vowel sounds:
    a -> "ah" (as in "father") casa (KAH-zah) (house)
    e -> "eh" (as in "bet") bene (BEH-neh) (well)
    i -> "ee" (as in "machine") vita (VEE-tah) (life)
    o -> "oh" (as in "boat," no glide) dove (DOH-veh) (where)
    u -> "oo" (as in "moon") tu (too) (you)
  • Double vowels are both pronounced separately: mai (MAH-ee) (never).
  • Word stress usually falls on the second-to-last syllable (the "penultimate" syllable): parLAre, amiCO. When a word carries a written accent (` or ´) on its final vowel, the stress falls there instead: città (cheet-TAH) (city), perché (pehr-KEH) (why/because).

Examples

Dove abiti?

DOH-veh AH-bee-tee

Where do you live?

Come stai? Bene, grazie.

KOH-meh STAH-ee? BEH-neh, GRAH-tsee-eh

How are you? Fine, thanks.

La città è grande.

lah cheet-TAH eh GRAHN-deh

The city is big.

Il sole e la luna

eel SOH-leh eh lah LOO-nah

The sun and the moon

Notes

  • English speakers often want to add a glide to o and e ("oh-oo," "eh-ee"). Resist it — hold the vowel steady from start to finish.
  • If a word looks unfamiliar and has no written accent, guess stress on the second-to-last syllable first; it's right more often than not.

Practice

Read these words aloud, holding each vowel pure and steady: pane, vino, amico, dopo, cultura. Then try città and università, remembering the accent shifts the stress to the final syllable.
