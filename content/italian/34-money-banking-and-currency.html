Money, Banking, and Currency

Handling money — exchanging currency, using an ATM, understanding your bank statement — requires its own practical vocabulary. This lesson covers banking transactions and Italy's currency, the euro.

Grammar

  • Common banking verbs: cambiare valuta (to exchange currency), prelevare (to withdraw), versare (to deposit), aprire un conto (to open an account), riscuotere un assegno (to cash a check). Most of these are regular -are or -ere verbs following the patterns from Lesson 8.
  • To ask for something politely at a bank counter, the conditional vorrei (Lesson 19) works here too: Vorrei cambiare dei dollari in euro. (I'd like to change some dollars into euros.)
  • Italy's currency is the euro (l'euro) — notably, the word is invariable: un euro, due euro (never "due euri"). Smaller units are i centesimi (cents).
  • Useful questions: Quant'è il cambio? (What's the exchange rate?) Dov'è il bancomat più vicino? (Where's the nearest ATM?) Posso pagare con la carta di credito? (Can I pay by credit card?)

Examples

Vorrei cambiare duecento dollari in euro.

vohr-RAY kahm-BYAH-reh doo-eh-CHEHN-toh DOHL-lah-ree een EH-oo-roh

I'd like to change two hundred dollars into euros.

Dov'è il bancomat più vicino?

doh-VEH eel BAHN-koh-maht pyoo vee-CHEE-noh

Where's the nearest ATM?

Posso pagare con la carta di credito?

POHS-soh pah-GAH-reh kohn lah KAHR-tah dee KREH-dee-toh

Can I pay with a credit card?

Mi dispiace, non ho spiccioli.

mee dees-PYAH-cheh, nohn oh SPEECH-choh-lee

I'm sorry, I don't have any change.

Notes

  • Remember that "euro" never takes a plural -i or -s ending — cento euro, not "cento euri."
  • Cash is still widely used and often preferred in parts of Italy, even though cards are accepted almost everywhere in cities — don't assume a card will always work, especially at smaller shops or markets.

Practice

Role-play exchanging $150 for euros at a bank counter, asking the exchange rate and confirming the amount you'll receive.
