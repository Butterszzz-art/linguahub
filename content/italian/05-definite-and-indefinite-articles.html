Definite and Indefinite Articles

Italian articles ("a/an" and "the") agree with the noun in both gender and number, and — unlike English — the choice also depends on the very first sound of the noun that follows. This lesson lays out both systems clearly.

Grammar

  • Indefinite articles ("a/an," always singular):
    una -> feminine nouns starting with a consonant: una ragazza (a girl)
    un' -> feminine nouns starting with a vowel: un'amica (a friend)
    un -> masculine nouns starting with a vowel or most consonants: un amico, un ragazzo
    uno -> masculine nouns starting with s+consonant (s impura), z, gn, ps, or pn: uno studente, uno zio (an uncle), uno psicologo
  • Definite articles ("the") — these are the trickiest part of Italian grammar to memorize, so give this table real attention:
    Masculine singular: il (before most consonants) / lo (before s+cons., z, gn, ps, pn) / l' (before a vowel)
    Masculine plural: i (before most consonants) / gli (before s+cons., z, gn, ps, pn, and before vowels)
    Feminine singular: la (before consonants) / l' (before vowels)
    Feminine plural: le (for all feminine plurals, regardless of the following sound)
  • Examples showing the pattern: il libro/i libri; lo studente/gli studenti; l'amico/gli amici; la casa/le case; l'amica/le amiche.
  • Italian uses the definite article far more often than English does — notably with general or abstract nouns and languages: L'amore è cieco. (Love is blind.) Mi piace l'italiano. (I like Italian.) — English drops the article in both cases; Italian keeps it.

Examples

Ho un'amica a Roma e uno zio a Milano.

oh oon-ah-MEE-kah ah ROH-mah eh OO-noh DZEE-oh ah mee-LAH-noh

I have a friend in Rome and an uncle in Milan.

Gli studenti studiano l'italiano.

lyee stoo-DEHN-tee STOO-dyah-noh lee-tah-LYAH-noh

The students study Italian.

La casa è grande, le case sono piccole.

lah KAH-zah eh GRAHN-deh, leh KAH-zeh SOH-noh PEEK-koh-leh

The house is big, the houses are small.

Notes

  • Learners often default to "il" for every masculine noun out of habit — train your ear specifically on "lo" and "gli" before s+consonant, z, and gn words (lo studente, gli zii, lo zaino).
  • Remember the article-with-language rule: "Studio l'italiano," not "Studio italiano," when talking about the language in general.
