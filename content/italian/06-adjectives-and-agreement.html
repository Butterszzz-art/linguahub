Adjectives and Agreement

Italian adjectives change their ending to match the gender and number of the noun they describe — a feature with no equivalent in English. This lesson covers the two adjective patterns and where adjectives sit relative to the noun.

Grammar

  • There are two adjective types, based on how many distinct endings they have:
    Type 1 (four endings: -o, -a, -i, -e) — agrees in both gender and number, following the pattern of italiano: il ragazzo italiano (the Italian boy, m. sg.) i ragazzi italiani (the Italian boys, m. pl.) la ragazza italiana (the Italian girl, f. sg.) le ragazze italiane (the Italian girls, f. pl.) Other type-1 adjectives: alto/a (tall), piccolo/a (small), rosso/a (red).
    Type 2 (two endings: -e singular, -i plural) — agrees only in number, same form for masculine and feminine: l'esame facile / gli esami facili (the easy exam(s), m.) la prova facile / le prove facili (the easy test(s), f.) Other type-2 adjectives: grande (big), verde (green), francese (French), intelligente (intelligent).
  • Position: most adjectives FOLLOW the noun (una casa grande, un libro interessante) — the opposite default from English. A short list of very common adjectives can go BEFORE the noun instead, especially bello (beautiful), buono (good), cattivo (bad), grande, and piccolo — placement can even shift the nuance slightly (un grand'uomo, "a great man," vs. un uomo grande, "a big/tall man").
  • Adjectives of nationality always follow the noun and are not capitalized in Italian: la cucina italiana (Italian cuisine), un film francese (a French film).

Examples

Ho una macchina rossa e piccola.

oh OO-nah MAHK-kee-nah ROHS-sah eh PEEK-koh-lah

I have a small red car.

Sono ragazze italiane e intelligenti.

SOH-noh rah-GAHTS-tseh ee-tah-LYAH-neh eh een-tehl-lee-JEHN-tee

They're smart Italian girls.

È un libro molto interessante.

eh oon LEE-broh MOHL-toh een-teh-rehs-SAHN-teh

It's a very interesting book.

Che bella giornata!

keh BEHL-lah johr-NAH-tah

What a beautiful day!

Notes

  • The most frequent beginner error is leaving an adjective in its "default" -o form regardless of the noun's gender — always check the noun first, then match the adjective to it.
  • Don't force every adjective before the noun the way English does; when in doubt, put it after. You'll absorb the short "goes-before" list (bello, buono, grande, piccolo, cattivo, giovane, vecchio) with exposure.

Practice

Make each adjective agree with the noun: una casa (piccolo), due ragazzi (francese), gli esami (difficile), la macchina (rosso).
