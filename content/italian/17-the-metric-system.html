The Metric System

Italy uses the metric system exclusively, so a little vocabulary here goes a long way for shopping, cooking, travel distances, and understanding weather reports.

Grammar

  • Length and distance use metri (meters) and chilometri (kilometers, abbreviated km); short lengths use centimetri (centimeters).
  • Weight uses chili/chilogrammi (kilograms, abbreviated kg) and grammi (grams); un etto is a common everyday unit meaning 100 grams, especially at delis and markets (Lesson 24 covers food shopping in depth).
  • Liquid volume uses litri (liters).
  • Temperature is measured in gradi Celsius (degrees Celsius) — 0°C freezing, and body-comfortable room temperature is around 20-22°C, useful to know when reading an Italian weather forecast.
  • The construction for measurements mirrors English fairly closely: Quanto pesa? (How much does it weigh?) Pesa due chili. (It weighs two kilos.) Quanto è lontano? (How far is it?) È a cinque chilometri. (It's five kilometers away.)

Examples

Vorrei mezzo chilo di prosciutto.

vohr-RAY MEHD-dzoh KEE-loh dee proh-SHOOT-toh

I'd like half a kilo of prosciutto.

La città è a dieci chilometri da qui.

lah cheet-TAH eh ah DYEH-chee kee-LOH-meh-tree dah kwee

The city is ten kilometers from here.

Oggi ci sono trenta gradi.

OHJ-jee chee SOH-noh TREHN-tah GRAH-dee

Today it's thirty degrees (Celsius).

Un litro d'acqua, per favore.

oon LEE-troh DAHK-kwah, pehr fah-VOH-reh

A liter of water, please.

Notes

  • If you're used to Fahrenheit, remember 30°C is a genuinely hot summer day, not a chilly one — roughly 86°F.
  • "Un etto" (100 grams) is worth memorizing specifically, since it's the everyday unit Italians use at the deli counter and produce stand rather than always switching to grams or kilos.

Practice

Convert and say in Italian: your height in meters/centimeters, a comfortable room temperature in Celsius, and the weight of a kilo of apples.
