Getting Acquainted — Where Are You From?

Small talk about origins and language ability is some of the most useful early conversation you'll have in Italian. This lesson covers asking and answering where someone is from, and whether they speak a language.

Grammar

  • To ask where someone is from, use the verb essere (to be) + di (from) or the phrase venire da (to come from):
    Di dove sei? / Di dov'è? (Where are you from? inf./form.)
    Sono di New York. (I'm from New York.)
    Da dove vieni? / Da dove viene? (Where do you come from? inf./form.)
  • Country and nationality names are related but not identical — the country is usually capitalized, the nationality/language adjective is not: l'Italia (Italy) -> italiano (Italian); la Francia (France) -> francese (French); gli Stati Uniti (the United States) -> americano (American).
  • To ask if someone speaks a language, use parlare (Lesson 8): Parla inglese? (Do you speak English? formal) / Parli italiano? (informal)
  • To talk about ability with a language, you can also use capire (understand) and un po' (a little): Capisco un po' d'italiano. (I understand a little Italian.)

Examples

Di dove sei? Sono americana, di Chicago.

dee DOH-veh SEY? SOH-noh ah-meh-ree-KAH-nah, dee shee-KAH-goh

Where are you from? I'm American, from Chicago.

Parla inglese? Sì, ma parlo anche italiano.

PAHR-lah een-GLEH-zeh? see, mah PAHR-loh AHN-keh ee-tah-LYAH-noh

Do you speak English? Yes, but I also speak Italian.

Parlo poco ma capisco di più.

PAHR-loh POH-koh mah kah-PEES-koh dee pyoo

I speak a little but understand more.

Da dove vieni? Vengo dalla Francia.

dah DOH-veh VYEH-nee? VEHN-goh DAHL-lah FRAHN-chah

Where do you come from? I come from France.

Notes

  • Country names take a definite article (l'Italia, la Francia, gli Stati Uniti), but the article usually drops after the preposition "in" for countries: vivo in Italia (not "nell'Italia"), though it's kept for plural or modified country names: negli Stati Uniti.
  • "Parlo poco ma capisco di più" is a genuinely useful, humble phrase for real conversations while you're still learning — Italians respond well to it.

Practice

Answer in full sentences: Di dove sei? Parli italiano? Da dove vengono i tuoi genitori (your parents)?
