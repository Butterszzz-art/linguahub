Telling Time

Telling time in Italian uses "it is" differently depending on whether you're talking about one o'clock or any other hour, and everyday Italy runs on the 24-hour clock for anything official. This lesson covers both systems.

Grammar

  • Two equivalent ways to ask the time: Che ora è? or Che ore sono? (both mean "What time is it?")
  • For exactly 1:00, noon, or midnight, use the SINGULAR verb è:
    È l'una. (It's one o'clock.)
    È mezzogiorno. (It's noon.)
    È mezzanotte. (It's midnight.)
  • For every other hour, use the PLURAL verb sono + le + the number (because you're literally saying "they are the [hours]"):
    Sono le due. (It's two o'clock.)
    Sono le sette. (It's seven o'clock.)
  • Minutes past the hour are added with e (and): sono le tre e dieci (3:10); common fixed expressions: e un quarto (quarter past), e mezzo (half past), e tre quarti (quarter to the next hour, less common than the Italian convention of counting toward the next hour, e.g. le quattro meno un quarto for 3:45).
  • To ask what time something starts, add the preposition a, which contracts with the article: A che ora inizia? (What time does it start?) All'una. (At one.) Alle dieci. (At ten.) A mezzogiorno. (At noon.)
  • Italy uses the 24-hour clock for all official schedules — trains, planes, museum hours, showtimes. 13:00 = l'una del pomeriggio in casual speech, but written/spoken officially as le tredici; 20:00 = le venti.

Examples

Che ore sono? Sono le quattro e mezzo.

keh OH-reh SOH-noh? SOH-noh leh KWAHT-troh eh MEHD-dzoh

What time is it? It's four-thirty.

Il treno parte alle diciassette e trenta.

eel TREH-noh PAHR-teh AHL-leh dee-chahs-SEHT-teh eh TREHN-tah

The train leaves at 17:30 (5:30 p.m.).

A che ora inizia il film? Alle nove di sera.

ah keh OH-rah ee-NEE-tsyah eel feelm? AHL-leh NOH-veh dee SEH-rah

What time does the movie start? At nine p.m.

È mezzanotte, devo andare a dormire.

eh mehd-dzah-NOHT-teh, DEH-voh ahn-DAH-reh ah dohr-MEE-reh

It's midnight, I have to go to sleep.

Notes

  • The è vs. sono switch (singular for 1:00/noon/midnight, plural for everything else) is the single most common time-telling error for learners — pause and check which case you're in before speaking.
  • Even though casual conversation often uses the 12-hour system with "di mattina/di pomeriggio/di sera" for clarity, be ready to recognize 24-hour times on posted schedules (le venti = 8:00 p.m.).

Practice

Say these times aloud in Italian: 1:00, 6:15, 12:00 (noon), 9:45, and 20:30 using the 24-hour convention.
