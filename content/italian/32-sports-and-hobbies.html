Sports and Hobbies

Talking about free time and hobbies uses a specific set of verbs that don't always match English one-to-one — particularly the difference between playing a sport and playing an instrument. This lesson sorts out the vocabulary and structures for talking about what you enjoy doing.

Grammar

  • Italian uses different verbs for different kinds of "playing":
    giocare a + [sport] -> to play a sport: gioco a calcio (I play soccer), gioco a tennis (I play tennis)
    suonare + [instrument] -> to play an instrument: suono il piano (I play piano), suono la chitarra (I play guitar) Never use suonare for a sport, or giocare for an instrument — this is a common false-friend-style trap for English speakers used to one verb ("play") for both.
  • Fare is used for many other athletic/hobby activities, especially ones without a ball: faccio jogging, faccio yoga, fare nuoto (to swim/do swimming), fare una passeggiata (to take a walk, from Lesson 9's fare idioms).
  • To express liking an activity, use piacere (to be pleasing to — Lesson 33's future tense lesson will build on this construction further): Mi piace il calcio. (I like soccer — lit. "soccer is pleasing to me.") Mi piace nuotare. (I like swimming/to swim — with an infinitive following). For plural things you like: Mi piacciono gli sport. (I like sports.)

Examples

Gioco a calcio ogni domenica con gli amici.

JOH-koh ah KAHL-choh OH-nyee doh-MEH-nee-kah kohn lyee ah-MEE-chee

I play soccer with friends every Sunday.

Mia sorella suona il violino molto bene.

MEE-ah soh-REHL-lah SWOH-nah eel vyoh-LEE-noh MOHL-toh BEH-neh

My sister plays the violin very well.

Mi piace fare jogging la mattina.

mee PYAH-cheh FAH-reh JOHG-ging lah maht-TEE-nah

I like to go jogging in the morning.

Che cosa fai nel tempo libero?

keh KOH-zah fahy nehl TEHM-poh LEE-beh-roh

What do you do in your free time?

Notes

  • The giocare/suonare split is worth drilling deliberately — the error of saying "suono a calcio" or "gioco il piano" is common but easy to fix once you've practiced the pattern a few times.
  • Piacere agrees with the THING liked, not with the person who likes it — this reverses the English structure and is worth extra attention when you formally study indirect object pronouns later.
