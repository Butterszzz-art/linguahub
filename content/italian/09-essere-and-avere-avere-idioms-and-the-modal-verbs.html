Essere and Avere, Avere Idioms, and the Modal Verbs

Essere (to be) and avere (to have) are the two most important irregular verbs in Italian — they're used constantly on their own and as helping verbs for compound tenses later on. Avere also drives a set of idiomatic expressions that don't translate word-for-word from English. This lesson closes out Part 1 with these verbs plus the three modal verbs: dovere, volere, and potere.

Grammar

  • Essere (to be) and avere (to have) are both irregular and must be memorized outright — you'll use them in nearly every sentence.
  • Idiomatic uses of avere: Italian expresses many states that English handles with "to be" using avere ("to have") instead — literally "to have hunger," "to have heat," and so on.
  • The three modal (helping) verbs — dovere (must/have to), volere (to want), potere (can/may) — are conjugated themselves, and are followed by another verb in the INFINITIVE (unconjugated) form:
    Devo studiare. (I have to study.)
    Voglio dormire! (I want to sleep!)
    Posso entrare? (May I come in?)

Examples

Ho fame e ho sete!

oh FAH-meh eh oh SEH-teh

I'm hungry and thirsty!

Quanti anni hai? Ho ventotto anni.

KWAHN-tee AHN-nee AH-ee? oh vehn-TOHT-toh AHN-nee

How old are you? I'm twenty-eight.

Devo lavorare, ma voglio uscire.

DEH-voh lah-voh-RAH-reh, mah VOH-lyoh oo-SHEE-reh

I have to work, but I want to go out.

Non posso venire stasera.

nohn POHS-soh veh-NEE-reh stah-SEH-rah

I can't come tonight.

Notes

  • English learners instinctively reach for "essere" to translate "to be hungry/cold/right," etc. — catch yourself and use avere instead for this whole family of expressions.
  • After dovere, volere, or potere, the second verb always stays in the infinitive — never conjugate both verbs (devo studiare, not devo studio).

Practice

Translate using avere idioms: "I'm thirty years old." "We're cold." "She's right." "Do you (informal) need anything?" Then write one sentence each with dovere + infinitive and volere + infinitive.
