The Imperfetto — the Other Past Tense

Alongside the passato prossimo (Lessons 26-27), Italian has a second past tense, the imperfetto, used for descriptions, habits, and ongoing states in the past rather than single completed actions. Choosing correctly between the two is one of the more nuanced parts of intermediate Italian.

Grammar

  • The imperfetto is a simple (one-word) tense, formed by dropping the infinitive ending and adding a consistent set of endings — it's actually more regular than the passato prossimo, with only a few common irregular stems.
  • -ARE endings: -avo, -avi, -ava, -avamo, -avate, -avano (parlare -> parlavo, parlavi, parlava, parlavamo, parlavate, parlavano)
  • -ERE endings: -evo, -evi, -eva, -evamo, -evate, -evano (avere -> avevo, avevi, aveva, avevamo, avevate, avevano)
  • -IRE endings: -ivo, -ivi, -iva, -ivamo, -ivate, -ivano (dormire -> dormivo, dormivi, dormiva, dormivamo, dormivate, dormivano)
  • Two important irregular imperfetto stems: essere -> ero, eri, era, eravamo, eravate, erano; fare -> facevo, facevi, faceva, facevamo, facevate, facevano (fare uses its old Latin stem "face-" rather than the modern infinitive stem).
  • USE the imperfetto for: ongoing or repeated past actions ("I used to..." / "I was ...ing"), descriptions of people, places, weather, age, or time in the past, and background/setting for a story.
  • USE the passato prossimo for: a specific, completed action or event — something that happened once, at a defined point.
  • The classic contrast: Mentre studiavo (imperfetto — ongoing background action), ha chiamato Marco (passato prossimo — the single event that interrupted it). (While I was studying, Marco called.)

Examples

Da bambino giocavo sempre in giardino.

dah bahm-BEE-noh joh-KAH-voh SEHM-preh een jahr-DEE-noh

As a child I always used to play in the garden.

Faceva bel tempo ieri quando siamo usciti.

fah-CHEH-vah behl TEHM-poh YEH-ree KWAHN-doh SYAH-moh oo-SHEE-tee

The weather was nice yesterday when we went out.

Mentre guardavo la TV, ha suonato il telefono.

MEHN-treh gwahr-DAH-voh lah tee-VOO, ah swoh-NAH-toh eel teh-LEH-foh-noh

While I was watching TV, the phone rang.

C'erano molte persone alla festa.

CHEH-rah-noh MOHL-teh pehr-SOH-neh AHL-lah FEHS-tah

There were a lot of people at the party.

Notes

  • A simple test: if you can insert "used to" or "was/were ...ing" in English without changing the meaning, reach for the imperfetto; if the action is a single, complete event, use the passato prossimo instead.
  • The imperfetto is genuinely one of the more regular tenses in Italian — once you know the six endings per verb type, you can apply them almost universally except for essere and fare.

Practice

Describe what you used to do on weekends as a child (three sentences, imperfetto), then write one sentence combining both tenses correctly (an ongoing action interrupted by a completed one).
