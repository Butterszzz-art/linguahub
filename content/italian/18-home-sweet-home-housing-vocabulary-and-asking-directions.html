Home Sweet Home — Housing Vocabulary and Asking Directions

This lesson pairs two practical topics: describing where you live (rooms, housing types, ordinal numbers for floors) and asking for directions with the verbs of motion you'll need to get around.

Grammar

  • Ordinal numbers (first, second, third...) are used constantly for building floors: primo, secondo, terzo, quarto, quinto, sesto, settimo, ottavo, nono, decimo. They behave like type-1 adjectives and agree with the noun: il primo piano (the first floor), la seconda porta (the second door). Note: in Italian buildings, il piano terra (ground floor) is separate from il primo piano (which is one floor UP from ground level, i.e. what Americans call the "second floor").
  • Housing vocabulary: un appartamento (apartment), una casa (house), un monolocale (studio apartment), l'affitto (rent), il palazzo (building).
  • To ask for directions, use dov'è...? (where is...?) for singular, dove sono...? for plural, or Come arrivo a...? (How do I get to...?)
  • The main verbs of motion, all irregular, are essential here: andare (to go), venire (to come). Andare pairs with the preposition a for cities and in for most other places: vado a Roma (I'm going to Rome) but vado in centro (I'm going downtown), vado in banca (I'm going to the bank).
  • Directional vocabulary: a destra (to the right), a sinistra (to the left), sempre dritto (straight ahead), all'angolo (at/on the corner), vicino a (near), lontano da (far from).

Examples

Abito in un appartamento al terzo piano.

AH-bee-toh een oon ahp-pahr-tah-MEHN-toh ahl TEHR-tsoh PYAH-noh

I live in an apartment on the third floor.

Scusi, dov'è la stazione?

SKOO-zee, doh-VEH lah stah-TSYOH-neh

Excuse me, where is the station?

Vada sempre dritto e poi giri a destra.

VAH-dah SEHM-preh DREET-toh eh poy JEE-ree ah DEHS-trah

Go straight ahead and then turn right.

La banca è vicino alla piazza, all'angolo.

lah BAHN-kah eh vee-CHEE-noh AHL-lah PYAHT-tsah, ahl-LAHN-goh-loh

The bank is near the square, on the corner.

Notes

  • Remember the floor-numbering offset: what Americans call the "second floor" is il primo piano in Italy, since il piano terra is its own separate ground level, not "floor one."
  • Andare's preposition depends on the destination type — cities take a, most enclosed or general places take in (in banca, in centro, in Italia), and this is worth memorizing case by case rather than by rule alone.

Practice

Describe your home (how many rooms, which floor) in three sentences, then give directions from your front door to the nearest supermarket.
