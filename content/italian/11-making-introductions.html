Making Introductions

Once you can greet someone, the natural next step is introducing yourself and others. This lesson covers names, the verb chiamarsi (to be called), and polite phrases for meeting someone for the first time.

Grammar

  • To say your name, Italian uses the reflexive verb chiamarsi (literally "to call oneself"): Mi chiamo Anna. (My name is Anna. / I call myself Anna.) You'll cover reflexive verbs fully in Lesson 31, but this common phrase is worth learning now.
    io mi chiamo / tu ti chiami / lui-lei si chiama / noi ci chiamiamo /
    voi vi chiamate / loro si chiamano
  • To ask someone's name: Come ti chiami? (informal) / Come si chiama? (formal)
  • To introduce someone else, use the verb presentare (to present/ introduce): Ti presento Marco. (Let me introduce you to Marco. — lit. "I present Marco to you.") Formally: Le presento il signor Bianchi.
  • Standard responses when meeting someone: piacere (pleasure — the Italian equivalent of "nice to meet you") and molto lieto/lieta (delighted, m./f.).

Examples

Ciao, come ti chiami? Mi chiamo Sofia.

chow, KOH-meh tee KYAH-mee? mee KYAH-moh SOH-fyah

Hi, what's your name? My name is Sofia.

Piacere di conoscerLa.

pyah-CHEH-reh dee koh-NOH-shehr-lah

A pleasure to meet you. (formal)

Ti presento la mia amica Giulia.

tee preh-ZEHN-toh lah MEE-ah ah-MEE-kah JOOL-yah

Let me introduce you to my friend Giulia.

Questo è mio fratello, Luca.

KWEHS-toh eh MEE-oh frah-TEHL-loh, LOO-kah

This is my brother, Luca.

Notes

  • "Piacere" alone is enough as a first response when meeting someone — you don't need a full sentence to be polite.
  • Match the formal or informal introduction phrase to the person you're addressing (Le presento... vs. Ti presento...), the same way you choose between Lei and tu.

Practice

Introduce yourself and then introduce a friend to a new acquaintance, first informally (with a peer) and then formally (with your friend's boss).
