Subject Pronouns and Formal vs. Informal "You"

Italian verb endings already show who's doing the action, so subject pronouns (I, you, he, she...) are often dropped entirely — used only for emphasis or clarity. This lesson also covers one of Italian's biggest cultural adjustments for English speakers: choosing between the informal and formal "you."

Grammar

  • Subject pronouns:
    io (I) - tu (you, informal) - lui (he) - lei (she) - noi (we) -
    voi (you, plural) - loro (they)
  • Because the verb ending already signals the subject (parlo = "I speak," necessarily io), Italians normally drop the pronoun. Use it only:
    For contrast: Tu parli italiano, ma io parlo spagnolo. (You speak Italian, but I speak Spanish.)
    For emphasis: Vieni anche tu? (Are YOU coming too?)
    Standing alone: "Chi è?" "Sono io." ("Who is it?" "It's me.")
  • Formal vs. informal "you": Italian has two ways to say "you" to one person, and choosing correctly matters socially.
    tu -> informal; use with friends, family, children, peers, anyone your own age you're on casual terms with.
    Lei -> formal (capitalized when written to show respect); use with strangers, superiors, shopkeepers, waitstaff, teachers, and anyone you don't know well. Grammatically, Lei behaves like "she" — it takes third-person singular verb forms even when addressing a man.
  • The plural "you" (voi) covers a group regardless of formality in everyday speech.
  • Custom dictates that the older or higher-status person is the one who invites a switch from Lei to tu ("Diamoci del tu" — "let's use tu with each other").

Examples

Ciao, come stai?

chow, KOH-meh STAH-ee

Hi, how are you? (informal)

Buongiorno, signora, come sta?

bwohn-JOHR-noh, see-NYOH-rah, KOH-meh stah

Good morning, ma'am, how are you? (formal)

Tu tifi per il Milan, ma io per la Juventus.

too TEE-fee pehr eel mee-LAHN, mah EE-oh pehr lah yoo-VEHN-toos

You root for Milan, but I root for Juventus.

Vieni anche tu alla festa?

VYEH-nee AHN-keh too AHL-lah FEHS-tah

Are you coming to the party too?

Notes

  • Beginners often over-use subject pronouns out of habit from English — practice dropping io, tu, noi, etc. and letting the verb ending carry the meaning; it will sound much more natural.
  • When unsure whether to use tu or Lei with someone new, default to Lei — it's the safer, more respectful choice, and Italians rarely take offense at being addressed too formally.

Practice

Choose tu or Lei for each situation: ordering coffee from a barista you've never met; asking your little cousin a question; greeting your professor; chatting with your best friend.
