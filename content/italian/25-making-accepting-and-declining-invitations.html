Making, Accepting, and Declining Invitations

This lesson rounds out Part 3's social vocabulary with the phrases for inviting someone to do something, and for accepting or gracefully declining an invitation yourself.

Grammar

  • To invite someone, you can use Ti va di + infinitive (informal, "Do you feel like...?") or Vuoi + infinitive (Do you want to...?): Ti va di uscire stasera? (Do you feel like going out tonight?)
  • To accept enthusiastically: Volentieri! (Gladly!) Con piacere! (With pleasure!) Perché no? (Why not?)
  • To decline politely, softening with mi dispiace (I'm sorry) is standard: Mi dispiace, non posso. (I'm sorry, I can't.) Purtroppo ho un altro impegno. (Unfortunately I have another commitment.)
  • To suggest an alternative time: Possiamo rimandare a un'altra volta? (Can we postpone to another time?) or Che ne dici di domani? (What do you say to tomorrow?)

Examples

Ti va di andare al mare domenica?

tee vah dee ahn-DAH-reh ahl MAH-reh doh-MEH-nee-kah

Do you feel like going to the beach on Sunday?

Volentieri, a che ora?

voh-lehn-TYEH-ree, ah keh OH-rah

Gladly, what time?

Mi dispiace, purtroppo ho già un impegno.

mee dees-PYAH-cheh, poor-TROHP-poh oh jah oon eem-PEH-nyoh

I'm sorry, unfortunately I already have a commitment.

Che ne dici di rimandare a sabato?

keh neh DEE-chee dee ree-mahn-DAH- reh ah SAH-bah-toh

What do you say to postponing to Saturday?

Notes

  • "Magari!" is a very common, enthusiastic one-word response — it means something closer to "I wish!" or "I'd love that!" rather than a literal "maybe."
  • Softening a decline with mi dispiace + purtroppo is the polite Italian norm; a flat "no, I can't" without any cushioning can come across as abrupt.

Practice

Write a short dialogue: propose an outing, have the other person decline once with a reason, then successfully agree on an alternative time.
