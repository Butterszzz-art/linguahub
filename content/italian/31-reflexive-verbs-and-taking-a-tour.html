Reflexive Verbs and Taking a Tour

Reflexive verbs describe actions the subject does to or for themselves — waking up, getting dressed, feeling a certain way — and they're extremely common in everyday Italian. This lesson covers their formation and applies them to talking about sightseeing and daily routines.

Grammar

  • A reflexive verb pairs a normal verb conjugation with a reflexive pronoun that matches the subject: mi, ti, si, ci, vi, si. The pronoun goes directly before the conjugated verb.
  • You've already used one reflexive verb: chiamarsi (Lesson 11). The pattern is the same for all reflexive verbs — for example, alzarsi (to get (oneself) up):
    io mi alzo, tu ti alzi, lui/lei si alza, noi ci alziamo, voi vi
    alzate, loro si alzano
  • Common everyday reflexive verbs: svegliarsi (to wake up), alzarsi (to get up), lavarsi (to wash oneself), vestirsi (to get dressed), sentirsi (to feel — used for physical/emotional states, Lesson 16's "handling emergencies" vocabulary reuses this exact verb), divertirsi (to have fun, enjoy oneself), riposarsi (to rest).
  • In the passato prossimo, ALL reflexive verbs take essere as their helping verb and the past participle agrees with the subject (Lesson 27's agreement rule): Mi sono svegliata alle sette. (I woke up at seven. — female speaker)
  • Reflexive verbs are useful for describing a guided tour or excursion: fermarsi (to stop, pause), fare un giro (to take a ride/tour — not reflexive, but frequently paired with sightseeing), divertirsi (to enjoy oneself).

Examples

Mi sveglio sempre alle sei e mi alzo subito.

mee ZVEH-lyoh SEHM-preh AHL-leh sey eh mee AHL-tsoh SOO-bee-toh

I always wake up at six and get up right away.

Ci siamo divertiti molto durante la visita guidata.

chee SYAH-moh dee-vehr-TEE-tee MOHL-toh doo-RAHN-teh lah VEE-zee-tah gwee-DAH-tah

We had a lot of fun during the guided tour.

Vuoi fare un giro in centro?

VWOI FAH-reh oon JEE-roh een CHEHN-troh

Do you want to take a ride/walk downtown?

Ci fermiamo un momento a fare fotografie.

chee fehr-MYAH-moh oon moh-MEHN-toh ah FAH-reh foh-toh-grah-FEE-eh

Let's stop for a moment to take pictures.

Notes

  • Don't drop the reflexive pronoun out of habit — "Alzo alle sette" is incomplete; the pronoun (mi) is a required part of the verb, not an optional add-on.
  • Remember reflexive verbs always take essere in compound tenses, with full subject agreement on the participle — this connects directly back to Lesson 27.
