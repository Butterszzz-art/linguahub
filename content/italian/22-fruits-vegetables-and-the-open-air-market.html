Fruits, Vegetables, and the Open-Air Market

Open-air markets (il mercato) remain a common way to buy fresh produce in Italy. This lesson focuses on fruit and vegetable vocabulary and the phrases you'll need to shop for them.

Grammar

  • Most fruit and vegetable names follow the regular gender patterns from Lesson 4 (-a/-e feminine, -o/-i masculine), so you can predict most plurals once you know the singular and its gender.
  • To ask if something is fresh or in season: È fresco/a? (Is it fresh?) È di stagione? (Is it in season?)
  • Vendors will often ask Quanto ne vuole? (How much/many would you like?) — answer with a quantity + di: due chili di pomodori (two kilos of tomatoes), tre mele (three apples, using the item directly when it's a countable number rather than weight).

Examples

Vorrei due chili di pomodori maturi.

vohr-RAY DOO-eh KEE-lee dee poh-moh-DOH-ree mah-TOO-ree

I'd like two kilos of ripe tomatoes.

Sono fresche queste fragole?

SOH-noh FREHS-keh KWEHS-teh FRAH-goh-leh

Are these strawberries fresh?

Mi dà anche una cipolla e due carote?

mee dah AHN-keh OO-nah chee-POHL- lah eh DOO-eh kah-ROH-teh

Could you also give me an onion and two carrots?

Il mercato è aperto la mattina.

eel mehr-KAH-toh eh ah-PEHR-toh lah maht-TEE-nah

The market is open in the morning.

Notes

  • Haggling is not customary at Italian food markets, unlike some shopping contexts abroad — prices are fixed, even in open-air settings.
  • Many fruit and vegetable words are near-cognates or easy to remember once you see the pattern (banana, limone/lemon-like, tomato/pomodoro is a rarer exception worth memorizing directly).

Practice

Practice asking for five different fruits or vegetables by specific quantity (some by weight, some by count).
