Sentence Structure and Noun Gender

Italian sentences follow the same basic order as English — subject, verb, object — which makes the biggest adjustment not word order but noun gender, something English doesn't have. Every Italian noun is either masculine or feminine, and that single fact ripples out into articles, adjectives, and pronouns throughout the language.

Grammar

  • Basic word order matches English: Marco parla italiano. (Marco speaks Italian.) Subject-Verb-Object.
  • Every noun has a grammatical gender (masculine or feminine) and a number (singular or plural). Gender is not about biological sex for objects — it's a grammatical category you memorize along with the word.
  • The most reliable pattern:
    Feminine nouns usually end in -a (singular) / -e (plural): una casa -> due case (a house -> two houses)
    Masculine nouns usually end in -o (singular) / -i (plural): un libro -> due libri (a book -> two books)
    Nouns ending in -e can be either gender, and end in -i in the plural either way: un esame (m.) -> due esami; una lezione (f.) -> due lezioni. You memorize the gender of -e nouns individually.
  • Handy shortcut: almost any noun ending in -zione is feminine (nazione, lezione, informazione).
  • Invariable nouns keep the same form in singular and plural. Three types:
    1. Nouns ending in an accented vowel: caffè -> caffè; città -> città
    2. Nouns ending in a consonant (rare, mostly borrowed words): bar -> bar; film -> film
    3. Abbreviated words: bici (bike) -> bici; foto -> foto; auto -> auto

Examples

Marco parla italiano.

MAHR-koh PAHR-lah ee-tah-LYAH-noh

Marco speaks Italian.

Ho un esame difficile.

oh oon eh-ZAH-meh deef-FEE-chee-leh

I have a difficult exam.

Le mie amiche studiano in città.

leh MEE-eh ah-MEE-keh STOO-dyah-noh een cheet-TAH

My friends (f.) study in the city.

Prendo un caffè e vado.

PREHN-doh oon kahf-FEH eh VAH-doh

I'll have a coffee and go.

Notes

  • There is no reliable shortcut for -e nouns: gender must be learned word by word, ideally alongside the article (e.g., learn "il caffè," not just "caffè").
  • Because articles and adjectives must agree with noun gender (Lessons 5 and 6), getting gender right from the start saves a lot of correction later — always learn new nouns with their article attached.

Practice

Decide masculine or feminine, then give the plural: pizza, treno, notte, professore, chiave, museo.
