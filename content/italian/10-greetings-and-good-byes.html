Greetings and Good-byes

Greetings are the first thing you'll actually use in real conversation, and Italian has a clear formal/informal split that echoes the tu/Lei distinction from Lesson 7. This lesson covers hellos, good-byes, and how to reply naturally when someone greets you.

Grammar

  • Greetings shift by time of day and by formality level, just like the verbs used to address people:
    Informal, any time: ciao (hi/bye) — use only with tu-level relationships
    Formal, morning/early afternoon: buongiorno (good morning/good day)
    Formal, evening: buonasera (good evening)
    Before bed: buonanotte (good night) — a farewell, not a greeting
  • Good-byes: arrivederci (informal-to-neutral, "until we see each other again") and arrivederLa (the extra-formal version, addressed to a single person you're speaking to with Lei). A ciao works for informal partings too.
  • Replying to "come stai/sta?" (how are you?): Italians commonly answer with a scale rather than a flat yes/no — bene (well), non c'è male (not bad), così così (so-so), male (badly) — then often return the question with "e tu?" / "e Lei?" (and you?).

Examples

Buongiorno, signora Rossi! Come sta?

bwohn-JOHR-noh, see-NYOH-rah ROHS-see! KOH-meh stah

Good morning, Mrs. Rossi! How are you?

Ciao Marco, come stai? Non c'è male, e tu?

chow MAHR-koh, KOH-meh STAH-ee? nohn cheh MAH-leh, eh too

Hi Marco, how are you? Not bad, and you?

È tardi, buonanotte a tutti!

eh TAHR-dee, bwoh-nah-NOHT-teh ah TOOT-tee

It's late, good night everyone!

Arrivederci, a presto!

ahr-ree-veh-DEHR-chee, ah PREHS-toh

Goodbye, see you soon!

Notes

  • Buonanotte is exclusively a farewell (said when parting for the night), never a greeting — unlike buongiorno/buonasera, which work as both hello and general well-wishes throughout their time of day.
  • Match your greeting's formality to your interlocutor: ciao with a shopkeeper you've just met can come across as too casual; buongiorno is the safer opener with strangers.

Practice

Write the right greeting for each: entering a bakery at 9am; leaving a friend's party at 1am; saying hello to your boss at 4pm; texting a close friend "hi, how are you?"
