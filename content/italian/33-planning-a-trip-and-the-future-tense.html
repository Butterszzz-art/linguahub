Planning a Trip and the Future Tense

This lesson introduces the simple future tense — used for plans, predictions, and promises — in the context of planning a trip: deciding where and when to go, and booking travel.

Grammar

  • The future tense (futuro semplice) is formed by dropping the final -e of the infinitive and adding a consistent set of endings. -ARE verbs additionally change their a to e before adding the ending.
  • Endings for all three verb types: -ò, -ai, -à, -emo, -ete, -anno
    parlare -> parlerò, parlerai, parlerà, parleremo, parlerete, parleranno (note the -are -> -er- shift)
    prendere -> prenderò, prenderai, prenderà, prenderemo, prenderete, prenderanno
    partire -> partirò, partirai, partirà, partiremo, partirete, partiranno
  • Several very common verbs have irregular future STEMS (the endings stay the same, only the stem changes): essere -> sarò, sarai, sarà, saremo, sarete, saranno; avere -> avrò, avrai, avrà, avremo, avrete, avranno; andare -> andrò, andrai, andrà, andremo, andrete, andranno; fare -> farò, farai, farà, faremo, farete, faranno; venire -> verrò, verrai, verrà, verremo, verrete, verranno.
  • The future is also used, distinctively from English, to express probability or a guess about the present: Sarà tardi. (It's probably late. / It must be late.) Avrà trent'anni. (He's probably thirty.)
  • Everyday Italian often uses the present tense for near-future plans instead of the future tense (Parto domani — I'm leaving tomorrow), the same way English can say "I'm leaving tomorrow." Reserve the true future for more distant plans, predictions, or formal contexts.

Examples

L'anno prossimo andremo in Sicilia.

LAHN-noh PROHS-see-moh ahn-DREH-moh een see-CHEE-lyah

Next year we'll go to Sicily.

Prenoterò il volo domani.

preh-noh-teh-ROH eel VOH-loh doh-MAH-nee

I'll book the flight tomorrow.

Che tempo farà in agosto?

keh TEHM-poh fah-RAH een ah-GOHS-toh

What will the weather be like in August?

Sarà una vacanza indimenticabile.

sah-RAH OO-nah vah-KAHN-tsah een-dee-mehn-tee-KAH-bee-leh

It will be an unforgettable vacation.

Notes

  • Don't try to guess future stems from the infinitive for irregular verbs — essere, avere, andare, fare, and venire all need to be memorized directly, the same way their present-tense forms did.
  • Watch for the "probability" use of the future — Sarà in ufficio (He's probably at the office) is not about a future event at all, and this nuance often surprises English speakers.

Practice

Write four sentences about a future trip: where you'll go, when you'll leave, what you'll do there, and a guess (using the future of probability) about the weather.
