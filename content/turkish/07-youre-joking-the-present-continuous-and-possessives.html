You're Joking! — The Present Continuous and Possessives

This lesson introduces the tense you'll use constantly in everyday Turkish conversation: the -iyor present tense, used for what's happening right now, for habitual actions, and even for near-future plans. You'll also learn how to say what belongs to whom (possessives), the endings -li and -siz ("with" and "without"), and the essential verb istemek ("to want").

Grammar

  • The -iyor present tense: this is Turkish's equivalent of the English "-ing" form, though its uses are broader (see below). To build it:
    1. Take the verb stem (e.g. iç-, "drink")
    2. Add -iyor (always -iyor in spelling, though it harmonizes in sound: -ıyor/-iyor/-uyor/-üyor depending on the stem's last vowel — four- way harmony)
    3. Add the personal ending (same family as "to be": -um, -sun, none, -uz, -sunuz, -lar)
    içiyorum - I am drinking (literally "drink-ing I-am")
    geliyorum - I am coming
    satıyorsun - you are selling
    sürüyor - she is driving
    yazıyoruz - we are writing
    koşuyorlar - they are running
  • Negative -iyor forms: insert -mi- (which loses its vowel before -iyor, becoming just -m-) after the stem: geliyorum -> gelmiyorum (I'm not coming). Stress shifts onto the syllable right before -mi- in negative forms — this stress shift is just as important as the ending itself for being understood.
  • Questions with -iyor: split the word into the verb+iyor part and the personal ending, then insert the question particle between them.
    geliyorsunuz -> geliyor musunuz? (are you coming?) Note that the "they" form is the odd one out, putting mı at the very end: koşuyorlar mı? (are they running?)
  • Avoiding vowel clashes in -iyor forms: if a verb stem ends in a vowel, that vowel is dropped before adding -iyor, to avoid two vowels colliding.
    başlamak (to start) -> başlıyorum (I'm starting), not "başlayıyorum"
    beklemek (to wait) -> bekliyorsunuz (you're waiting)
    yemek (to eat) -> yiyor (he's eating) In the negative, though, the stem stays intact, since -mi- starts with a consonant sound in context: Başlamıyorum (I'm not starting).
  • Uses of the -iyor present tense: not just "right now" — also for stating unchanging facts (Sigara kullanmıyorum - I don't smoke), habitual or repeated actions (Sık sık plaja gidiyorum - I often go to the beach), and near-future plans (Bu akşam geliyorum - I'm coming this evening).
  • Possessives — the possessor ending -in: to say something belongs to someone, add -in (or -nin after a vowel) to the possessor, following four-way harmony.
    kızın - the girl's annenin - mother's
  • Possessives — the possessed ending -i/-si: Turkish ALSO marks the thing being possessed, unlike English. Add -i (or -si after a vowel).
    doktorun evi - the doctor's house
    kızların annesi - the girls' mother Exception: su (water) takes -yu instead of -su: Ali'nin suyu (Ali's water).
  • Possessive personal pronouns: benim (my), senin (your), onun (his/her), bizim (our), sizin (your), onların (their) — note ben and biz are slightly irregular, taking -im rather than -in. The possessed noun then takes the matching ending: benim evim (my home), onun evi (his/her home). Because the possessed ending already shows the owner, you can usually drop the pronoun itself — except onların, which is best kept to avoid confusing "his" with "their."
  • Saying what you've got: Turkish uses var/yok with a possessive rather than a separate verb "to have." Kızım var. (I've got a daughter — literally "my daughter exists.") Arabası yok mu? (Doesn't he have a car?)
  • The endings -li and -siz ("with" and "without"): these make adjectives, following four-way (-li) and two-way (-siz) harmony respectively.
    balkonlu - with a balcony şekersiz - without sugar -li also attaches to country and town names to give a nationality or demonym: Kanada -> Kanadalı (Canadian); İstanbul -> İstanbullu (a person from İstanbul). Some nationalities are irregular exceptions: Japonya -> Japon (not "Japonyalı"); Rusya -> Rus.
  • İstemek (to want): use it with a noun (Bir kilo domates istiyorum - I want a kilo of tomatoes) or with an infinitive verb (Gitmek istiyorum - I want to go).

Examples

İçiyor musunuz? Teşekkür ederim, sigara kullanmıyorum.

ee-CHEE-yohr moo-soo-NOOZ? teh-shek-KEWR eh-deh-REEM, see-gah-RAH kool-lah-NUH-yoh-room

Do you smoke? Thank you, I don't smoke.

Kilimlere bakmak istiyorum.

kee-leem-leh-REH bahk-MAHK ees-tee-YOH-room

kuh-ZUHM vahr. oh-dah-nuhn bahn-yoh-SOO vahr muh

Uzun ömürlü süt istiyorum.

oo-ZOON ur-mewr-LEW sewt ees-tee-YOH-room

I want long-life milk.

Notes

When someone offers you something in Turkey, saying teşekkür ederim ("thank you") on its own is generally taken to mean "no thank you" — to accept, use lütfen instead. This can genuinely cause confusion (and very hungry foreigners) at a Turkish dinner table! Also note ben de and şu de — de/da means "also/too," which is different from the endings -de/-da that mean "in/at/on"; context makes clear which is meant.

Practice

  1. Put these verbs into the -iyor present, "I" form: gelmek (to come), yazmak (to write), yemek (to eat, watch the vowel clash).
  2. Make these negative: geliyorum, satıyorsun, yiyor.
  3. Translate using a possessive: "the firm's director" and "my mother."
  4. Say "I don't have a car" and "Do you have milk?" using var/yok.
  5. Turn Kanada and İstanbul into nationality/demonym adjectives using -li.
