When Was This Place Built? — The Passive Voice and Reflexives

This lesson covers the passive voice (used far more often in Turkish than in English, especially on signs), how to say "by" someone, the "was going to happen" past future tense, and reflexive verbs and pronouns ("myself," "yourself"). You'll finish with a set of noun/adjective- building endings useful for expanding your vocabulary independently.

Grammar

  • The passive voice: used when you want to focus on what was done or what something had done to it, rather than on who did it. Build it by adding -il or -in (there are also -n and other variants for certain stem shapes) after the verb stem.
    açmak (to open) -> açılmak (to be opened)
    çekmek (to pull) -> çekilmek (to be pulled)
    bulmak (to find) -> bulunmak (to be found) Rules for which variant to use:
    most verbs: -il stems ending in a vowel: -n
    stems ending in l: -in Compare an active and passive pair: Cüzdanı çaldı. (He stole the purse.) -> Cüzdan çalındı. (The purse was stolen.) Note how the object (cüzdan) drops its -i ending and becomes the SUBJECT of the passive sentence.
  • Saying "by" in a passive sentence — tarafından: place this word after the person who performed the action.
    "Hamlet" Sekspir tarafından yazıldı. - "Hamlet" was written by
    Shakespeare. With personal pronouns, put a possessor ending on the pronoun and a possessed ending in the middle of tarafından: benim tarafımdan (by me). This is only used for nouns/pronouns, not attached directly to ordinary nouns without tarafından. Also note: Turkish generally reserves tarafından passives for inventions, discoveries, or describing a process — for everyday statements, an active sentence (Bu resmi ben çizdim - I drew this picture) is more natural than the passive.
  • The passive on signs: Turkish uses the passive far more than English does, especially in public signage.
    Tamir edilir. - Repairs made. Döviz alınır. - Foreign currency
    accepted.
    Girilmez. - No entry. Sigara içilmez. - Smoking
    forbidden.
  • The past future tense ("was going to," -ecekti): build from the future tense by removing the personal ending and adding the past "to be" ending instead.
    içeceğim -> içecek -> içecektim - I was going to drink
    Sana telefon edecektim, ama numaranı kaybettim. - I was going to
    telephone you, but I lost your number.
  • Reflexives — kendi ("self"): combine with the possessed endings -im, -in, -i, -imiz, -iniz, -leri.
    Kendim seçtim. - I chose it myself.
    Kendine iyi bak. - Look after yourself. For "by myself," double it: Kendi kendime seçtim. (I chose it all by myself.) Kendi can also mean "my own/your own" etc. when combined with a possessed noun: Kendi arabamızı kullandık. (We used our own car.)
  • Reflexive verbs: add -in to a verb stem to build a reflexive form.
    yıkamak (to wash something) -> yıkanmak (to wash yourself)
    giymek (to put clothes on) -> giyinmek (to get dressed) Some reflexive forms take on their own special meaning: dinlemek (to listen) -> dinlenmek (to relax, literally "to listen to yourself"). Watch out — for some verbs the reflexive form looks IDENTICAL to the passive form (e.g. taşınmak can mean either "to move house" or "to be moved"); context makes the intended meaning clear.
  • Vocabulary-building endings: Turkish builds many nouns and adjectives from verb stems using a family of endings. A sample set:
    -im: doğmak (to be born) -> doğum (birth)
    -in: sormak (to ask) -> sorun (problem)
    -gin/-kin: üzmek (to hurt feelings) -> üzgün (sad)
    -gen/-ken: çalışmak (to work) -> çalışkan (hard-working)
    -gi: bilmek (to know) -> bilgi (knowledge)
    -i: sormak (to ask) -> soru (question)

Examples

Bu han 1876 yılında inşa ettirilmiş.

boo hahn been seh-keez YEWZ yet-meesh AHL-tuh yuh-luhn-DAH een-SHAH et-tee-reel-meesh

This building was reportedly built in 1876.

Kapı Ahmet tarafından açıldı.

kah-PUH ahh-MET tah-rah-fuhn-DAHN ah-chuhl-DUH

The door was opened by Ahmet.

Girilmez. Sigara içilmez.

gee-reel-MEZ. see-gah-RAH ee-cheel-MEZ

No entry. Smoking forbidden.

Sana telefon edecektim, ama numaranı kaybettim.

sah-NAH teh-leh-FOHN eh-deh-jek-TEEM, ah-MAH noo-mah-rah-NUH kahy-bet-TEEM

I was going to telephone you, but I lost your number.

Kendine iyi bak!

ken-dee-NEH ee-YEE bahk

Look after yourself!

Notes

When you're out and about in Turkey, watch specifically for passive-voice signage — Girilmez, Kapalıdır (closed), and similar official-sounding notices are everywhere, and recognizing the passive pattern will make public Turkish far more readable, even before you're fluent in conversation.

Practice

  1. Make bulmak (to find) passive.
  2. Translate: "This building was built by Turks," using tarafından.
  3. Translate this sign: "No entry."
  4. Say "I was going to call you" using the past future -ecekti.
  5. Build the reflexive of giymek (to put clothes on) and explain what it means.
