Where Shall We Go? What Can We Do? — Ability, Comparatives, and "Let's"

This lesson covers how to say "can/can't" and "might/might not," how to compare two things, the full set of "let" forms (let me, let's, let him), the useful adjective-forming ending -ki, and a tidy review of when to use each buffer consonant.

Grammar

  • Can/be able to: -ebil- (four-way, e-type harmony) slots in right after the verb stem, inside any tense.
    gidebilirim - I can go yapabilirsin - you can do
    gidebileceğim - I'll be able to go Oynayabildi mi? - Was he able to
    play? Form questions the normal way for whichever tense you're in: gidebilir miyim? (can I go?)
  • Can't/not able: mark with -e- (note: NOT -ebil-, just -e-) right after the stem, in the negative form of whichever tense you need.
    gelemeyeceğim - I won't be able to come
    Yapamadım. - I couldn't do it.
    Olamaz! - It can't be!
  • Might: also uses -ebil-, with the specific meaning (can vs. might) usually clear from context.
    Biraz sonra içebilirim. - I might have a drink a little later. The negative of "might" is different from the negative of "can" — for "might not," simply add -me after the verb stem (not -e-):
    Yapabilirim, yapmayabilirim. - I might do it and I might not. Compare: Sinemaya gidemem (I can't go to the cinema) vs. Sinemaya gitmeyebilirim (I might not go to the cinema) — these mean different things, so don't mix them up.
  • Comparatives: to say "more X than Y," Turkish literally says "more X FROM Y" — the compared-against item takes the -den ending, and daha ("more") appears before the adjective.
    Senden daha akıllıyım. - I'm cleverer than you (literally "from you
    more clever am-I").
    Türkiye, İngiltere'den daha ucuz. - Turkey is cheaper than England. For superlatives ("the most / the ...-est"), use en before the adjective: Türkiye'de en iyi şarkıcı Pınar. (Pınar is the best singer in Turkey.)
  • The "let" forms of verbs:
    -eyim - "let me..." (offering help, making suggestions): Yardım edeyim. - Let me help. Gideyim mi? - Shall I go?
    -elim - "let's..." (making suggestions): Gidelim. - Let's go. Yapalım mı? - Shall we do it?
    -sin - what someone ELSE should do, or a hope that something happens: Eve gitsin. - He should go home. Yağmur yağmasın. - I hope it doesn't rain (literally "let it not rain").
    -sinler - the plural of -sin: Yarın gelsinler. - Tell them to come tomorrow. You'll recognize several fixed phrases built this way: Üstü kalsın. (Keep the change.) Kolay gelsin. (I hope your work goes well.) Afiyet olsun. (Bon appétit.)
  • The ending -ki: attach to a noun already carrying -de to turn it into an adjective meaning "the one which is in/at/on."
    otel + de -> oteldeki bar - the bar in the hotel
    masa + da -> masadaki çiçekler - the flowers on the table -ki never changes for vowel harmony. Used without its noun, it means "the one(s) which is/are in/at/on": oteldeki (the one in the hotel). You can also attach -ki to a possessor to mean "the one which is mine/his/etc.": benimki (mine), onlarınki (theirs).
  • Buffer consonants, reviewed and organized: now that you've met several endings, here's the full pattern for which buffer to use:
    -s- : for possessed endings, and in compound nouns
    -n- : for possessor endings; after a word that already has a possessed ending; with bu/şu/o; with the -ki ending
    -y- : everywhere else a vowel clash needs to be avoided
  • -ce ("according to," and to name a language): add to a nationality or country name to give the related language (Türk -> Türkçe; İsveç -> İsveççe). Add to a personal pronoun to mean "according to...": bence (in my opinion), sizce (in your opinion). Add to an adjective to mean "rather...": iyice (rather good), büyükçe (rather big).

Examples

Sinemaya gidebiliriz. Başka ne yapabiliriz?

see-neh-mah-YAH gee-deh-bee-lee-REEZ. bahsh-KAH neh yah-pah-bee-lee-REEZ

We can go to the cinema. What else can we do?

Bu saatte bilet bulamayız.

boo sah-aht-TEH bee-LET boo-lah-mah-YUHZ

We can't find tickets at this hour.

Yeni Zelanda, Türkiye'den daha küçük.

yeh-NEE zeh-lahn-DAH, tewr-kee-yeh-DEN dah-HAH kew-CHEWK

New Zealand is smaller than Turkey.

Gidelim mi? Evet, gidelim.

gee-deh-LEEM mee? eh-VET, gee-deh-LEEM

Shall we go? Yes, let's go.

Taksim'deki lokanta.

tahk-seem-deh-KEE loh-kahn-TAH

the restaurant in Taksim.

Notes

Comparative sentences reverse English logic — instead of saying "I'm cleverer THAN you," Turkish says "FROM you more clever" — so make sure the -den ending lands on the thing being compared against, not the person doing the comparing. Also, exclamations like üf be! and yok canım! are common and hard to translate literally, but their emotional tone usually comes through clearly from context and delivery.

Practice

  1. Say "I can come" and then "I can't come" for gelmek.
  2. Explain the difference between Sinemaya gidemem and Sinemaya gitmeyebilirim.
  3. Say "Turkey is cheaper than England" using the comparative pattern.
  4. Give the "let's" form of gitmek and the "let him" form of gelmek.
  5. Build oteldeki using -ki, and explain what it means.
