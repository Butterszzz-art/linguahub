Part 3 · Past and Future · Lesson 9

I'll Get Up Early Tomorrow — The Future Tense and Place Words

This lesson covers the "real" future tense (as opposed to using -iyor with future meaning, which you already know from Lesson 7), how to make casual suggestions, the word ile ("with"/"and"), how to say where something is in relation to something else (on top of, behind, between, etc.), and how to tell the time more precisely than just the hour and half-hour.

Grammar

  • The future tense: spot it by -ecek (two-way harmony: -ecek/-acak). Build it the same way as other tenses: stem + -ecek + personal ending.
    geleceğim - I will come satacaksın - you will sell
    yazacağız - we will write bekleyecekler - they will wait Negate by inserting -me- before -ecek, with a buffer -y- always required between them: geleceğim -> gelmeyeceğim (I won't come). Form questions by splitting the verb from its personal ending and inserting mı/mi/mu/mü between them, same pattern as the past tense; the "they" form again puts mı at the very end. You can also use olmak (to become/be) as the future of "to be," var, or yok: Mutluyum -> Mutlu olacağım (I will be happy); Su yok -> Su olmayacak (There won't be any water).
  • The present continuous (-iyor) can ALSO carry future meaning, and Turkish speakers switch freely between it and -ecek — often the two are interchangeable, much like English "I'm coming tomorrow" vs. "I will come tomorrow."
  • Informal suggestions with -sene/-senize: add this ending to a verb stem to mean "why don't you...!" Gelsene! (Do come!) Baksana! (Why don't you look!) Depending on tone of voice, these can be either a friendly nudge or an expression of mild impatience.
  • Ile ("with," and sometimes "and"): can be shortened to the ending -le (two-way harmony).
    Kardeşimle git. - Go with my brother.
    Arabayla gidiyorum. - I'm going by car. Turkish also uses ile in some places English uses "and": Pınar ile Didem geliyorlar (Pınar and Didem are coming). With personal pronouns, -le attaches to the possessive form (except for "they"): Benimle gel. (Come with me.) Note the difference between -le (with/and, from ile) and -li (an adjective-forming ending, e.g. sütlü, "with milk," meyveli, "with fruit") — they look similar but serve different jobs.
  • Prepositions of place: Turkish builds these from nouns meaning the relevant body part or zone, combined with a possessive and the -de/-e/ -den family of endings.
    üst (top) -> -in üstünde (on top of)
    alt (bottom) -> -in altında (under)
    ön (front) -> -in önünde (in front of)
    arka (back) -> -in arkasında (behind)
    yan (side) -> -in yanında (beside)
    orta (middle) -> -in ortasında (in the middle of)
    iç (interior) -> -in içinde (inside)
    dış (exterior) -> -in dışında (outside)
    ara (gap) -> -in arasında (between)
    karşı (opposite) -> -in karşısında (opposite) Examples: Masanın altından geldi. (The cat came from under the table.) PTT'nin karşısında. (Opposite the post office.) Otel ile plajın arasında. (Between the hotel and the beach.)
  • Telling the time accurately: for minutes before the hour, use -e var ("there are ___ to"); for minutes after the hour, use -i geçiyor ("___ is passing"). To express a specific moment ("at five to," "at five past") swap var for kala and geçiyor for geçe.
    Saat üçe on var. - It's ten to three.
    Saat üçü on geçiyor. - It's ten past three.
    Saat bire beş kala. - At five to one.
    Saat biri beş geçe. - At five past one.

Examples

Bu akşam ne yapıyorsun? Bilmiyorum.

boo ahk-SHAHM neh yah-puh-yohr-SOON? beel-mee-yoh-ROOM

What are you doing this evening? I don't know.

Yarın erken kalkacağım.

yah-RUHN ehr-KEN kahl-kah-jah-UHM

I'll get up early tomorrow.

Kardeşimle git. Arabayla gidiyorum.

PTT'nin karşısında. Otel ile plajın arasında.

peh-teh-teh-NEEN kahr-shuh-suhn-DAH. oh-TEL ee-LEH plah-JUHN ah-rah-suhn-DAH

Saat dörde çeyrek var.

sah-AHT dur-deh-CHEYREK vahr

It's a quarter to four.

Notes

In this unit's dialogue, notice how the speakers move fluidly between -iyor and -ecek to talk about the same future evening — this is completely normal and not a mistake in either direction. Also, hadi (come on!) is a word you'll hear constantly in Turkey; try counting how many times you hear it in a single day once you're there.

Practice

  1. Put geleceğim, satacaksın, and bekleyecekler into the negative.
  2. Say "I might come with you" using -sene as an informal suggestion form applied to gel-.
  3. Translate: "The cat is under the table" and "in front of the grocer's."
  4. Give the time for "twenty-five to twelve" and "twenty past six."
  5. Explain the difference between -le (from ile) and -li in your own words, with an example of each.
