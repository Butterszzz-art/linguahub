Wrong Number — Participles: -en vs. -dik vs. -ecek

This lesson completes the set of Turkish participles (verb-based adjectives), teaching you -dik adjectives for the past/present object of an action and -ecek adjectives for the future object, and clarifies exactly how they differ from the -en adjectives you met in Lesson 10. You'll also learn the -ip ending for chaining actions together and how to intensify adjectives using a special prefix pattern.

Grammar

  • -dik adjectives (the "object participle"): describe the OBJECT of an action — the thing being acted upon — where -en (Lesson 10) describes the SUBJECT (the doer). Build with stem + -dik (four-way harmony) + possessed ending.
    (benim) sevdiğim renk - the colour which I like (or liked)
    (onun) sürdüğü araba - the car he drives (or drove) -dik adjectives are flexible about which relationship they express, depending on context:
    plain object (which/that): sevdiğim renk - the colour I like
    location (in/at which): oturduğum ev - the house in which I live
    destination (to which): gittiği okul - the school he goes to
    source (from which): geldiğiniz köy - the village you come from
    companion (with which/whom): evlendiği erkek - the man she married Note that -dik adjectives can refer to either the past or present — the meaning is usually clear from context.
  • The crucial distinction between -en and -dik: use -en for the SUBJECT of the action (who's doing it), and -dik for the OBJECT (who/what it's being done to).
    Beni seven kadın. - The woman who loves me. (she is the subject)
    Sevdiğim kadın. - The woman whom I love. (she is the object)
  • -dik as a noun (dropping the described noun): when it's obvious what the missing noun is, you can drop it and move its ending onto the -dik form itself.
    Aldığım şeyi istiyor musun? -> Aldığımı istiyor musun? - Do you want
    the thing I bought? -> Do you want what I bought? If the missing noun was plural, carry the plural ending over too: Aldığım şeyleri -> Aldıklarımı.
  • -ecek adjectives and nouns: the future counterpart of -dik, used for "which/that I will..." Build with stem + -ecek (two-way harmony) + possessed ending.
    Okuyacağım kitap. - The book which I'm going to read. Like -dik, -ecek forms can also stand alone as nouns: Alacaklarım bunlar. (These are the ones I'm going to buy.) And like -en/-dik, -ecek covers both subject and object roles depending on context: Yapan bu (this is the one who did it) vs. Yapacak bu (this is the one who'll do it); Yaptığı bu (this is the one he did) vs. Yapacağı bu (this is the one he'll do).
  • The -ip ending: lets you chain two actions together with the same subject and matching tense, dropping the repeated tense marker on the first verb — similar to how English can say "I'm going to have a bath and wash my hair" instead of repeating "I'm going to."
    Bu akşam banyo yapacağım ve saçlarımı yıkayacağım. -> Bu akşam banyo
    yapıp saçlarımı yıkayacağım. You CANNOT use -ip if the verb forms differ, or if the two actions have different subjects: Eve döneceğiz ve çalışacağım (We'll go home and I'll work) cannot be shortened this way, because "we" and "I" are different subjects.
  • Intensifying adjectives with a repeated prefix: Turkish has one case where it adds a PREFIX instead of a suffix — to intensify certain adjectives, repeat the adjective's first syllable-initial sound plus a fixed consonant (usually p, m, or s), then attach it to the front of the full word.
    kırmızı (red) -> kıpkırmızı (bright red)
    beyaz (white) -> bembeyaz (white as snow)
    temiz (clean) -> tertemiz (squeaky clean)
    boş (empty) -> bomboş (completely empty) There's no single rule for which consonant to use — it's best learned by encountering each intensified form as a fixed expression.

Examples

Sevdiğim renk mavi.

sev-dee-EEM renk mah-VEE

The colour I like is blue.

Beni seven kadın orada. Sevdiğim kadın burada.

beh-NEE seh-VEN kah-DUHN oh-rah-DAH. sev-dee-EEM kah-DUHN boo-rah-DAH

The woman who loves me is there. The woman I love is here.

Okuyacağım kitap bu.

oh-koo-yah-jah-UHM kee-TAHP boo

This is the book I'm going to read.

Bakkala gidip bir kilo süt al!

bahk-kah-LAH gee-DEEP beer kee-LOH sewt ahl

Go to the grocer's and buy a kilo of milk!

Bomboş bir oda.

bohm-BOHSH beer oh-DAH

A completely empty room.

Notes

The -en/-dik distinction is one of the trickiest points for English speakers, since English "which/who" doesn't mark this subject/object difference at all. A reliable trick: ask yourself who or what is performing the action versus who or what is receiving it — the performer gets -en, the receiver gets -dik.

Practice

  1. Build a -dik adjective from görmek (to see) meaning "the film which we saw."
  2. Explain the difference between Beni seven kadın and Sevdiğim kadın.
  3. Shorten Aldığım şeyi istiyor musun? by dropping the noun şey.
  4. Combine these with -ip: Bakkala git ve bir kilo süt al!
  5. Give the intensified form of temiz (clean) and boş (empty).
