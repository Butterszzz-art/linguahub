No More, Please! — Commands, Direct Objects, and Verb Basics

This lesson takes you into a restaurant, where you'll learn how to give commands (both casual and formal), how to say "at/in/on," "to," and "from," how to mark the direct object of a sentence, how Turkish handles proper nouns and certain consonant changes, and how to find a verb's dictionary form — the foundation you'll need for every verb tense to come.

Grammar

  • Turkish word order: unlike English, the verb comes at the end of a Turkish sentence, and a word that describes another word comes BEFORE it. soğuk bir şişe beyaz şarap literally reads "cold a bottle white wine" but means "a cold bottle of white wine." When speaking casually, don't stress too much about perfect word order — meaning usually comes through regardless.
  • Commands (imperative): Turkish has three levels of formality.
    Informal: just the bare verb stem. Gel! (Come!) Koy! (Put!)
    Formal: add -in (four-way harmony) to the informal form. Gelin!
    Very formal/official (signs, notices): add -iniz. Geliniz! To make a command negative, add -me (two-way harmony) before the formality ending: Gelme! (Don't come!) Gelmeyin! (formal — note the buffer -y- to stop the vowels clashing) Yapmayınız! (very formal).
  • The endings -de (at/in/on), -e (to), and -den (from): these attach to nouns and follow two-way vowel harmony.
    Masada otur. - Sit at the table.
    Bakkala git. - Go to the grocer's.
    Bankadan bakkala git. - Go from the bank to the grocer's. Note the consonant change: -de becomes -te after an unvoiced consonant (ç, f, h, k, p, s, ş, t) — sokakta (in the street), not sokakda.
  • Direct objects and the accusative -i: a sentence's direct object is the thing an action is done TO. If that object is a SPECIFIC, particular thing (the equivalent of English "the"), add the ending -i (four-way harmony; -yi if the noun ends in a vowel).
    Kahve iç. - Have a coffee. (any coffee)
    Kahveyi iç. - Drink the coffee. (a specific coffee) If the object isn't specific, use no ending, or use bir ("a"): Masadan kalem al (Take a pen from the table) vs. Masadan kalemi al (Take the pen from the table).
  • Personal pronouns take these same endings, and several are irregular:
    ben -> beni, bana, bende, benden
    sen -> seni, sana, sende, senden
    o -> onu, ona, onda, ondan (note the buffer -n- with o)
    biz -> bizi, bize, bizde, bizden
    siz -> sizi, size, sizde, sizden
    onlar -> onları, onlara, onlarda, onlardan Note ben+e becomes bana (not "bene"), and sen+e becomes sana — these two are simply irregular and worth memorizing on their own.
  • Proper nouns: when adding an ending to the name of a specific place or person, insert an apostrophe before the ending: Türkiye'de (in Turkey), İstanbul'dan İzmir'e (from İstanbul to İzmir).
  • Consonant changes: two patterns to know.
    d -> t: the -de/-den endings become -te/-ten after an unvoiced
    consonant (maçta, "at the match"; sokakta, "in the street").
    k -> ğ: a word-final k with a vowel before it changes to ğ when you
    add a vowel-starting ending: kazak -> Kazağı giy! (Put the coat on!)
    This does NOT happen if the letter before k isn't a vowel: park stays
    park (Parka git! - Go to the park!).
  • The dictionary form of verbs (the infinitive): Turkish informal commands use the bare stem of a verb; the dictionary form is that stem plus -mek or -mak (two-way harmony). bak (look!) -> bakmak (to look); ver (give!) -> vermek (to give).

Examples

İki kişilik bir masa lütfen.

ee-KEE kee-shee-LEEK beer mah-SAH LEWT-fen

A table for two, please.

Buz getir. Daha koymayın, lütfen.

booz geh-TEER. dah-HAH koy-mah-YUHN, LEWT-fen

Bring some ice. Don't put any more, please.

Masadan kalemi al.

mah-sah-DAHN kah-leh-MEE ahl

Take the pen from the table.

Kadını öp! Kalemi Hakan'a ver!

kah-duh-NUH urp! kah-leh-MEE hah-kah-NAH vehr

Kiss the woman! Give the pen to Hakan!

Sokağa bak!

soh-kah-AH bahk

Look at the street!

Notes

Turkish restaurant custom: waiters often bring a large tray of starters (meze) unprompted and start unloading dishes without being asked — the custom is to interrupt them, pick what you want, and send the rest back (daha koymayın, "don't put any more," is exactly the phrase for this). Also keep in mind that word order matters less for meaning in spoken Turkish than it does in written Turkish — as long as the endings are right, listeners will generally understand you even if the words come in an unusual order.

Practice

  1. Make Yap! (Do!) formal, then very formal.
  2. Fill in -de, -e, or -den appropriately: Bankadan bakkal___ git (go from the bank to the grocer's).
  3. Decide whether these need the -i ending: (a) Kahve iç (any coffee) (b) Kahve___ iç (a specific coffee already mentioned).
  4. What happens to the k in kazak when you add a vowel-starting ending, and why doesn't the same happen to park?
  5. Give the infinitive (dictionary form) of: gel, al, otur.
