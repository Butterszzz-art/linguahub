Survival Turkish — Essential Words and Phrases

Before diving into full sentences and grammar, it helps enormously to have a stock of ready-made words and phrases you can lean on right away — the kind of language that gets you through a shop, a taxi, or a hotel check-in even before you can build your own sentences. This lesson is a practical phrasebook covering the situations you'll run into most often in Turkey.

Grammar

  • Most of this lesson is vocabulary rather than grammar, but a few patterns are worth flagging early:
    - lütfen (please) goes at the end (or occasionally the start) of a request: Bir çay, lütfen. (One tea, please.)
    - Turkish often omits words that English needs, like "a/an" or "is" — var mı? literally means "is there?" with nothing standing in for "a."
    - Yes/no answers frequently repeat the key word rather than using a simple "yes": Ekmek var mı? - Var. (Is there bread? - There is.)
  • A note on politeness: raising your eyebrows and making a single soft "tut" sound is a normal, polite way to say no in Turkey — it isn't rude or dismissive the way it might seem to an English speaker.

Examples

beer chai LEWT-fen

One tea, please.

Ekmek var mı? - Var.

ek-MEK vahr muh - vahr

Kaç para?

kach pah-RAH

Notes

Don't worry about understanding the grammar behind these phrases yet — that comes over the next several lessons. For now, the goal is simply recognition and recall: if you can greet someone, ask if something's available, ask the price, and say you don't understand, you already have enough Turkish to get through a huge range of everyday situations. As you listen to Turkish (audio, TV, conversation), pay attention to these words in particular — they come up constantly.

Practice

  1. How would you ask "is there any bread?" and then say "there isn't any"?
  2. Match each phrase to its meaning: (a) hoşça kalın (b) anlamadım (c) kaç para? — (i) I don't understand (ii) how much? (iii) goodbye.
  3. You want to ask where the post office is. What do you say?
  4. Someone offers you tea you don't want. What is the polite non-verbal way to decline, according to this lesson?
  5. Translate: "Excuse me, is there a toilet here?"
