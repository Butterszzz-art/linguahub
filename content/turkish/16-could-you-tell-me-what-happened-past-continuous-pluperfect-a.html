Could You Tell Me What Happened? — Past Continuous, Pluperfect, and Reported Speech

This lesson equips you to tell a detailed story about the past: what was happening at a given moment, what had already happened before that, and how to report what someone said, using nominalized -dik/-ecek clauses as the objects of verbs like "say," "hear," or "understand." You'll also learn how to build nouns from verbs with -iş and a handful of special expressions using var/yok.

Grammar

  • The past continuous tense ("what was happening"): build it from the present continuous (-iyor) by removing the personal ending and adding the past form of "to be" instead.
    yapıyorsunuz -> yapıyor -> yapıyordunuz - you were doing
    Saat sekizde ne yapıyordun? - What were you doing at eight o'clock?
    Ders çalışıyordum. - I was studying.
  • The pluperfect ("what had happened," -mişti): NOT related in meaning to the reported -miş from Lesson 14 — this is simply the equivalent of English "had done."
    Eve geldiğim zaman sen gitmiştin. - When I came home, you had gone.
    Daha önce hiç yememiştim. - I had never eaten it before.
  • Reporting events with -dik/-ecek nouns: to say "I said that I was fine," Turkish uses the -dik (or, for the future, -ecek) participle as a noun functioning as the object of the reporting verb — literally "fine my-being I-said."
    Hasta olduğumu kim söyledi? - Who said that I was ill?
    Senin bunu söyleyeceğini tahmin ettim. - I guessed you would say
    that.
    Onların boşanacağını sanmam. - I don't reckon they'll get divorced.
  • Combining -dik/-ecek nouns with connecting words: certain words attach after these participles to build more complex connectors (shown below with -diği as the placeholder form):
    -diği gibi - just as -diği halde - although
    -diği için - due to, because -diği kadar - as far as
    -diği zaman / -diğinde - when -diğinden başka - apart from
    Bildiğim kadar iyiler. - As far as I know, they're fine.
    Fakir olduğu halde hayatından memnun. - Although he's poor, he's
    content with his life.
  • The difference between -dik/-ecek nouns and the short infinitive: use -dik/-ecek nouns for a SPECIFIC event that actually happened or will happen; use the short infinitive for the general CONCEPT or manner of doing something.
    Şarkı söylediğiniz doğru mu? - Is it true that you sing? (a specific
    fact about you)
    Şarkı söylemeniz doğru mu? - Is your singing the right thing to do?
    (the concept/appropriateness of singing)
  • Building nouns with -iş: attach to a verb stem to create a related noun, often about an action's occurrence.
    kalkmak (to leave) -> kalkış (departure)
    girmek (to enter) -> giriş (entry)
    uçmak (to fly) -> uçuş (flight)
    almak/vermek (to take/give) -> alışveriş (shopping)
  • Special expressions with var and yok:
    Bir varmış bir yokmuş... - Once upon a time... (uses -miş, tying back
    to Lesson 14's storytelling use)
    Ne var? - What's up? What's the matter?
    Ne var ne yok? - What's the news?
    var etmek - to create, to cause to exist
    yok etmek - to get rid of
    var olmak - to exist
    yok olmak - to disappear

Examples

Ben denizde yüzüyordum.

ben deh-neez-DEH yewz-ew-yohr-DOOM

I was swimming in the sea.

Erkek yok olmuştu.

ehr-KEK yohk ohl-moosh-TOO

The man had disappeared.

Hasta olduğumu kim söyledi?

hahs-TAH ohl-doo-oo-MOO keem sur-leh-DEE

Who said that I was ill?

Fakir olduğu halde mutlu.

fah-KEER ohl-doo-OO hahl-DEH moot-LOO

Although he's poor, he's happy.

Kalkış saat kaçta?

kahl-KUHSH sah-AHT kach-TAH

What time is departure?

Notes

This unit's dialogue (a tourist reporting a theft to a policeman) is a good model for real narrative Turkish: notice how it layers the past continuous ("I was swimming"), the pluperfect ("he had disappeared"), and reported description together in a single connected account. Practicing retelling a simple personal story using these three tools together is one of the most useful exercises at this stage.

Practice

  1. Turn yapıyorsunuz into the past continuous.
  2. Say "I had never eaten it before" using -mişti.
  3. Report this statement as something someone said: "Ali went home" (Ali eve gitti) -> "They said that Ali went home."
  4. Build a noun meaning "flight" from uçmak using -iş.
  5. Translate: "Although he's poor, he's content with his life," using -diği halde.
