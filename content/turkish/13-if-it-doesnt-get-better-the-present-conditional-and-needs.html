If It Doesn't Get Better... — The Present Conditional and Needs

This lesson introduces the present conditional ("if" sentences about possible future events), the versatile word kadar ("as...as," "until"), how to say "since" and "for" when talking about time, how to express needing something, and the vocabulary for parts of the body.

Grammar

  • Present tense "if" forms of "to be": spot them by -se- inserted into the personal endings.
    iyiysem - if I'm well Türkse - if she's Turkish
    iyiysek - if we're fine Türkseler (or Türklerse) - if they're
    Turkish These same endings attach directly onto verbs to create the if-form of the -r present tense: take the -r present form, strip off the personal ending, and add the if-ending instead.
    yaparım -> yaparsam - if I do isterse - if she wants
    açarlar -> açarlarsa - if they open Full example: Telefon etmezsem saat ikide buluşacağız. (If I don't phone, we'll meet at two.) The word eğer ("if") can optionally be placed before the clause for extra clarity, but it's usually not needed since the -se ending already signals "if" on its own. You can also attach -se directly to var/yok: Paramız yoksa gidemeyiz. (If we've got no money, we can't go.)
  • Using the -r present tense in the result clause: after an "if" clause, Turkish typically follows with the -r present tense (not -ecek) for what will happen: Eğer gelirse memnun olurum. (If he comes, I'll be happy.) You'll also hear the -r present used alone as an implied warning, with the "if" part left unspoken: Düşersin! (You'll fall! — meaning "if you're not careful, you'll fall.")
  • Needing something — -e ihtiyaç var: the Turkish equivalent of "I need a holiday" is literally "I have a need to a holiday."
    Tatile ihtiyacım var. - I need a holiday.
    Neye ihtiyacımız var? - What do we need? Note ihtiyaç changes its final ç to c before a vowel-starting ending (ihtiyacım, not "ihtiyaçım") — this is the same voiced/voiceless consonant harmony pattern from Lesson 2.
  • Since — -den beri: for time, "since" is expressed with -den beri.
    Ne zamandan beri? - Since when? Dünden beri. - Since
    yesterday. Note the tense mismatch with English: Turkish says Bu sabahtan beri hastayım (literally "I am ill since this morning") where English says "I've BEEN ill since this morning" — don't try to translate the English perfect tense directly; just use the Turkish present/simple form with -den beri.
  • For (duration in the past): use -den beri or -dir if the action is still ongoing; use nothing extra if the action is already finished.
    Bir haftadan beri burada kalıyorlar. - They've been staying here for
    a week (still ongoing).
    İki gün yemedik. - We didn't eat for two days (finished).
  • Kadar: as "until/as far as/up to," it's used as -e kadar:
    Beşe kadar beklerim. - I'll wait until 5.
    Işıklara kadar gidin. - Go as far as the lights. On its own, kadar also means "as...as": Senin kadar akıllı değil. (He's not as clever as you.) Note that personal pronouns take a possessor ending before kadar (bizim kadar - as much as us), but o, onlar, and ordinary nouns do not (David kadar - as much as David).
  • Parts of the body: a practical vocabulary set, useful well beyond the doctor's office — see VOCABULARY below.

Examples

Eğer gerekirse, günde dört kere alabilir.

eh-EHR geh-reh-keer-SEH, gewn-DEH durt keh-REH ah-lah-bee-LEER

If necessary, he/she can take it four times a day.

Eğer yarın geçmezse, yine gelin.

eh-EHR yah-RUHN gech-mez-SEH, yee-NEH geh-LEEN

If it isn't better tomorrow, come again.

Tatile ihtiyacım var.

tah-tee-LEH eeh-tee-yah-JUHM vahr

I need a holiday.

Bindokuzyüzdoksanbeşten beri burada oturuyorum.

been-doh-kooz-yewz- dohk-sahn-besh-TEN beh-REE boo-rah-DAH oh-too-roo-yoh-ROOM

I've lived here since 1995.

Senin kadar akıllı değil.

seh-NEEN kah-DAHR ah-kuhl-LUH deh-EEL

He's not as clever as you.

Notes

Loanwords like ishal (diarrhoea) and saat (hour) came into Turkish from Arabic and can behave slightly irregularly for vowel harmony, because vowel harmony follows how the word is actually pronounced rather than how it's spelled — so saatler (hours) rather than the "expected" saatlar. As with lazım in the previous lesson, these are best treated as memorized exceptions rather than rule-breakers.

Practice

  1. Build the if-form of gelmek for "if I come" and "if they come."
  2. Translate: "If you want, let's go to the cinema."
  3. Say "I need a car" using -e ihtiyaç var.
  4. Say "since yesterday" and "for two days" (assume the two days are already finished).
  5. Name five parts of the body in Turkish.
