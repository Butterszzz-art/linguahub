You Said It Was Very Quiet! — The -miş Reported Past

This lesson introduces one of Turkish's most distinctive grammatical features: the -miş ending, used to report what someone else said, to signal you're not fully certain about something, to react to something you've just discovered, or to tell a story or joke. You'll also learn a cluster of endings for connecting clauses ("when," "after," "before," "while").

Grammar

  • The -miş ending: means "apparently," "supposedly," or "reportedly." Build it by adding -miş (four-way harmony) to the stem, then the personal ending.
    Ben yapmışım. - I did it, apparently (i.e., so I'm told).
    Bitmiş diyorlar. - They say it's finished.
    Arabamı siz sürmüşsünüz. - Apparently it was you who drove my car. You can also attach -miş to the verb "to be": İngilizmiş (he is/was said to be English), şişmanmışız (they say we are/were fat) — note the buffer -y- needed after a vowel-ending word.
  • Using -miş: this ending is extremely common in spoken Turkish, and its common thread is distancing the speaker from the statement in some way. Specific uses include:
    reporting what someone else said: Otel güzelmiş. - They said the
    hotel was nice.
    checking something you're unsure of: Doğru mu yapmışım? - Have I
    done it right?
    reacting to something you've just noticed: Aa! Çorabım kaçmış! - Oh!
    My stocking's laddered!
    telling stories, jokes, or fairy tales: Bir güzel prenses varmış... -
    There was a beautiful princess... (this is the Turkish equivalent of
    "once upon a time")
  • -miştir/-mişlerdir: these forms carry two special meanings beyond the basic -miş sense.
    "I expect...": used when you're not sure something's happened but
    assume it has. Şimdi oraya gelmiştir. - She must have arrived by
    now.
    the "journalistic" official past: used in announcements and news
    reports as a formal-sounding equivalent of the simple past tense —
    Ankara treni üçüncü perona gelmiştir. (The Ankara train has arrived
    at platform three.)
  • Nouns and adjectives that simply end in -miş as part of their basic form (not the grammatical ending): geçmiş (the past), dolmuş (a "stuffed" shared taxi — literally "having become full"), kızarmış (fried). These are worth recognizing so you don't mistake them for the reported-past ending on a completely different root.
  • Clause-connecting endings: attach directly to verb stems (or, for -dikten sonra and -ken, to specific tense forms) to link two actions.
    -ince - on...-ing, when: İstanbul'a gelince otelimize gittik. - On
    arriving in İstanbul, we went to our hotel.
    -dikten sonra - after...-ing: Yemeğini yedikten sonra uyudu. - She
    fell asleep after eating her meal.
    -ken (added to the -r present "he/she" form) - whilst...-ing:
    Temizlerken kafamı çarptım. - Whilst cleaning I banged my head.
    -den önce (added to the short infinitive) - before...-ing: Alışveriş
    yapmadan önce kütüphaneye gitti. - Before doing the shopping, he
    went to the library.

Examples

Sizce otelin restoranı birinci sınıfmış.

seez-JEH oh-teh-LEEN loh-kahn-tah-SUH bee-reen-JEE suh-nuhf-MUHSH

According to you, the hotel's restaurant was supposed to be first class.

Bir güzel prenses varmış...

beer gew-ZEL pren-SES vahr-MUHSH

There was a beautiful princess... (once upon a time...)

İstanbul'a gelince otelimize gittik.

ees-tahn-boo-LAH geh-leen-JEH oh-teh-lee-mee-ZEH geet-TEEK

On arriving in İstanbul, we went to our hotel.

Yemeğini yedikten sonra uyudu.

yeh-meh-ee-NEE yeh-deek-TEN sohn-RAH oo-yoo-DOO

She fell asleep after eating her meal.

Şimdi oraya gelmiştir.

sheem-DEE oh-rah-YAH gel-meesh-TEER

She must have arrived there by now.

Notes

The -miş tense is sometimes jokingly called the "gossip tense" in descriptions of Turkish, because it lets the speaker report something while quietly signaling "I wasn't there, but..." It's worth actively listening for in real Turkish speech and media — once you notice it, you'll hear it constantly, especially in storytelling and casual conversation about other people's business.

Practice

  1. Turn yapmak into "I did it, apparently" using -miş.
  2. Explain in your own words the difference in feeling between Otel güzeldi (the hotel was nice) and Otel güzelmiş (the hotel was apparently nice / they said the hotel was nice).
  3. Use -dikten sonra to say "After eating, we slept."
  4. Use -ince to say "On arriving home, I called you."
  5. What does the -miştir ending suggest in Şimdi oraya gelmiştir?
