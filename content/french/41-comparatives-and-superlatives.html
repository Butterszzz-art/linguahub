Comparatives and Superlatives

How to say "more/less/as...as" (comparative) and "the most/least" (superlative) in French, including several irregular forms that don't follow the standard pattern.

Grammar

  • Comparative with adjectives/adverbs:
    plus...que - more...than (plus grand que = taller than)
    moins...que - less...than (moins cher que = less expensive than)
    aussi...que - as...as (aussi rapide que = as fast as)
  • Comparative with nouns (quantity): plus de / moins de / autant de + noun + que: Elle a plus d'argent que moi. (She has more money than me.)
  • Comparative with verbs (how much an action happens): verb + plus/moins/ autant + que: Il travaille plus que moi. (He works more than I do.)
  • Superlative: le/la/les + plus/moins + adjective (+ de, if naming the group): C'est la plus grande ville de France. (It's the biggest city in France — note "de," not "dans," after a superlative naming a group.) With adjectives that normally follow the noun, the article can repeat: la voiture la plus rapide (the fastest car).
  • Irregular comparative/superlative forms (do NOT use plus + these words):
    bon (good) -> meilleur (better) -> le meilleur (the best)
    mauvais (bad) -> pire OR plus mauvais (worse) -> le pire (the worst)
    bien (well, adverb) -> mieux (better) -> le mieux (the best) Note the important distinction: "meilleur" is an ADJECTIVE (agrees with a noun), "mieux" is an ADVERB (describes a verb): Ce gâteau est meilleur. (This cake is better.) vs. Elle chante mieux que moi. (She sings better than me.)

Examples

Paris est plus grand que Lyon, mais moins grand que Tokyo.

Paris is bigger than Lyon, but smaller than Tokyo.

C'est le meilleur restaurant de la ville.

It's the best restaurant in town.

Elle parle français mieux que moi.

She speaks French better than I do.

J'ai autant de travail que toi cette semaine.

I have as much work as you this week.

Notes

  • Common learner mistake: saying "plus bon" instead of "meilleur," or confusing meilleur (adjective) with mieux (adverb) — "Il est mieux" actually means "He is better [health-wise]," a special idiomatic use, not a general grammatical substitute for meilleur.

Practice
