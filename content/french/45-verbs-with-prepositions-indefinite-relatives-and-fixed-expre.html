Verbs with Prepositions, Indefinite Relatives, and Fixed Expressions

A grab-bag "advanced toolkit" lesson: verbs whose French preposition doesn't match the English one, the "-ever" words (whatever, whoever...), and two useful idiomatic structures.

Grammar

  • Some French verbs take a DIFFERENT preposition than their English equivalent, or need none at all where English needs one — these must be memorized individually as you learn each verb:
    écouter (to listen TO) — no preposition: J'écoute la radio.
    regarder (to watch/look AT) — no preposition: Je regarde la télé.
    chercher (to look FOR) — no preposition: Je cherche mes clés.
    attendre (to wait FOR) — no preposition: J'attends le bus.
    payer (to pay FOR) — no preposition: J'ai payé le repas.
    demander (to ask FOR) — no preposition for the thing asked for: Je demande l'addition.
    téléphoner À quelqu'un (to call someone) — needs à, unlike English "call someone" (no preposition): Je téléphone à ma mère.
    se souvenir DE (to remember) — needs de: Je me souviens de ce jour.
    entrer DANS (to enter) — needs dans, not just "into": Il entre dans la maison.
  • Indefinite relative expressions ("-ever" words), formed with "que" + subjunctive:
    quoi que + subjunctive - whatever (Quoi que tu fasses, je te soutiendrai. = Whatever you do, I'll support you.)
    qui que + subjunctive - whoever (Qui que ce soit, dis-lui d'entrer.)
    où que + subjunctive - wherever (Où que tu ailles, écris-moi.)
    quel(le) que soit + noun - whatever/whichever (Quelle que soit la raison... = Whatever the reason may be...)
  • "Avoir beau + infinitive" = "no matter how much/despite trying to..." (concessive): J'ai beau essayer, je n'y arrive pas. (No matter how hard I try, I can't manage it.)
  • "Quitte à + infinitive" = "even if it means..." (accepting a consequence): Je vais finir ce soir, quitte à me coucher tard. (I'll finish tonight, even if it means going to bed late.)

Examples

J'attends mon ami depuis une heure.

I've been waiting for my friend for an hour.

Elle a téléphoné à sa grand-mère hier soir.

She called her grandmother last night.

Quoi qu'il arrive, nous serons prêts.

Whatever happens, we'll be ready.

J'ai beau lui expliquer, il ne comprend pas.

No matter how much I explain to him, he doesn't understand.

Notes

  • Build a personal running list of "tricky preposition" verbs as you meet them in reading/listening — this is a vocabulary-memorization problem more than a grammar-rule problem.

Practice

Translate, watching the prepositions: "I'm looking for my phone." "She's listening to music." "We're waiting for the train."
