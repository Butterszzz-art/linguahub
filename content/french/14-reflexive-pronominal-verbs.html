Reflexive (Pronominal) Verbs

Reflexive verbs describe an action the subject does to itself (washing oneself, getting up) and are extremely common in French daily-routine vocabulary.

Grammar

  • A reflexive verb pairs a reflexive pronoun (matching the subject) with the verb: me/te/se/nous/vous/se, placed directly BEFORE the conjugated verb.
  • Conjugation otherwise follows the normal pattern for the verb's family (-er/-ir/-re): se laver (to wash oneself) conjugates like laver, just with the reflexive pronoun added.
  • Not all "reflexive-looking" verbs are truly reflexive in meaning — some are "reciprocal" (two people doing something to each other: se parler = to talk to each other) and some are simply pronominal by convention (s'en aller = to leave/go away — no real "self" involved).
  • In the negative, "ne" goes before the reflexive pronoun and "pas" after the verb: Je ne me lève pas tôt. (I don't get up early.)
  • In the imperative (commands), reflexive pronouns move AFTER the verb in the affirmative, connected with a hyphen, and te becomes toi: Lève-toi ! (Get up!) but in the negative they stay before: Ne te lève pas ! (Don't get up!)

Reference Table

je me lave         nous nous lavons
tu te laves         vous vous lavez
il/elle/on se lave   ils/elles se lavent

Vocabulary

Examples

Je me réveille à sept heures et je me lève tout de suite.

I wake up at 7:00 and I get up right away.

Elle s'habille rapidement.

She gets dressed quickly.

Nous nous couchons tard le week-end.

We go to bed late on weekends.

Dépêche-toi, on est en retard !

Hurry up, we're late! (informal command)

Notes

  • With body parts and reflexive verbs, French uses the definite article (le/la/les), not a possessive: "je me brosse LES dents" (not "mes dents") — the reflexive pronoun already shows whose teeth they are.
