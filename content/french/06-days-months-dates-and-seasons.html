Days, Months, Dates, and Seasons

Talking about dates, the calendar, and seasons — practical vocabulary tied to the numbers from Lesson 5.

Grammar

  • Days and months are NOT capitalized in French (unlike English): lundi, janvier — capitalize only at the start of a sentence.
  • To give the date: "le" + cardinal number + month (no "de" between number and month, except "premier"): le 3 mai (May 3rd); le premier mai (May 1st — this is the ONE ordinal used for dates).
  • "On [a day]" needs no preposition: Je travaille lundi. (I work on Monday.) Adding "le" before the day makes it a recurring/habitual event: Je travaille le lundi. (I work on Mondays / every Monday.)
  • Seasons use "en" except spring: en été, en automne, en hiver, BUT au printemps (in spring).
  • Years can be said two ways: "dix-neuf cent quatre-vingt-seize" or "mille neuf cent quatre-vingt-seize" for 1996 (both mean "nineteen ninety-six" style vs. "one thousand nine hundred..." style).

Reference Table

Days (les jours): lundi, mardi, mercredi, jeudi, vendredi, samedi, dimanche
Months (les mois): janvier, février, mars, avril, mai, juin, juillet, août,
  septembre, octobre, novembre, décembre
Seasons (les saisons): le printemps, l'été, l'automne, l'hiver

Examples

Nous sommes le 14 août.

It's August 14th. (lit. "we are the 14 August")

Mon anniversaire est le premier mai.

My birthday is May 1st.

En hiver, il neige souvent.

In winter, it often snows.

Je pars en vacances au printemps.

I'm going on vacation in spring.

Notes

  • To ask "what day is it," French uses "Quel jour sommes-nous ?" or "On est quel jour ?" — literally "what day are we."
