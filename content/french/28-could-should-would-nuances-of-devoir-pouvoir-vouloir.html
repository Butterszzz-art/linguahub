Could, Should, Would — Nuances of Devoir, Pouvoir, Vouloir

English "could/should/would" map onto French in several different ways depending on tense and meaning — this lesson untangles the most common sources of confusion for learners.

Grammar

  • "Could" can mean:
    - ability in the past (imparfait of pouvoir): Je pouvais nager pendant des heures. (I could swim for hours — general past ability.)
    - managed to / succeeded (passé composé of pouvoir): J'ai pu finir à temps. (I was able to / managed to finish in time.)
    - a polite request or hypothetical (conditional of pouvoir): Pourrais-tu m'aider ? (Could you help me?)
  • "Should" is almost always the conditional of devoir:
    present conditional (should, right now/going forward): Tu devrais étudier plus. (You should study more.)
    past conditional (should have, in the past — a missed obligation): Tu aurais dû étudier plus. (You should have studied more.)
  • "Would" can mean:
    - conditional mood generally (hypothetical result): Je voyagerais si j'avais de l'argent. (I would travel if I had money.)
    - past habitual action — this is actually the IMPARFAIT, not conditional, despite English using "would": Quand j'étais jeune, je voyageais souvent. (When I was young, I would [used to] travel often.)
    - a polite wish (conditional of vouloir): Je voudrais un thé. (I would like a tea.)

Reference Table

  nous voulons, vous voulez, ils veulent
  (irregular futur/conditional stem: voudr-)

Examples

Quand j'étais enfant, je pouvais courir très vite.

When I was a child, I could run very fast. (ability, imparfait)

J'ai enfin pu lui parler hier.

I finally managed to talk to him yesterday. (achieved once, passé composé)

Vous devriez consulter un médecin.

You should see a doctor. (advice, present conditional)

J'aurais dû te le dire plus tôt.

I should have told you sooner. (regret, past conditional)

Chaque été, nous allions à la plage.

Every summer, we would go [used to go] to the beach. (habitual "would" = imparfait, NOT conditional)

Notes

  • The single most common learner error: translating habitual past "would" with the conditional instead of the imparfait. Test: if "would" can be replaced with "used to," it's imparfait, not conditional.

Practice

Translate: "When I was young, I would visit my grandmother every Sunday." / "You should have called me." / "Could you pass the salt?"
