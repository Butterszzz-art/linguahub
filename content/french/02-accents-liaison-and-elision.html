Accents, Liaison, and Elision

French uses five accent marks that change pronunciation or meaning, plus two "linking" rules — liaison and elision — that make spoken French flow smoothly between words.

Grammar

  • The five accents:
    é accent aigu (acute) -> closed "ay" sound (café, étudiant)
    è accent grave (grave) -> open "eh" sound (père, très)
    à/ù accent grave (grave) -> no sound change, distinguishes words (a "has" vs. à "to/at"; ou "or" vs. où "where")
    ê accent circonflexe (circumflex) -> often marks a dropped historical "s" (hôpital = hospital, forêt = forest) and slightly opens the vowel
    ë/ï accent tréma (diaeresis) -> forces two vowels to be pronounced separately (Noël = "no-el", not "nwel")
    ç cédille (cedilla) -> forces a soft "s" sound on c before a, o, u (garçon, français)
  • Liaison: when a word ending in a normally-silent consonant is followed by a word starting with a vowel or mute h, that consonant IS pronounced, linking the two words. Example: vous_avez ("voo-zah-vay", not "voo ah-vay"); les_amis ("lay-zah-mee"); un petit_ami ("uh puh-tee-tah-mee"). Liaison is mandatory after: subject pronouns before verbs, articles before nouns, and short adjectives before nouns. It's forbidden after "et" (and).
  • Elision: when a short word (je, me, te, se, le, la, de, ne, que, ce, si+il) ends in a vowel and the next word starts with a vowel or mute h, the vowel is dropped and replaced with an apostrophe. je + aime -> j'aime; le + école is impossible because école is feminine (l'école); si + il -> s'il.

Examples

J'habite à Paris.

I live in Paris. (elision: je -> j')

Vous êtes français ?

Are you French? (liaison: vous_êtes)

Les hôtels sont chers.

The hotels are expensive. (liaison, mute h)

Le héros arrive.

The hero arrives. (NO liaison — aspirate h)

Notes

  • Most words starting with "h" in French have a MUTE h and behave like vowels for elision/liaison purposes. A small set (héros, haricot, honte, hall, etc.) have an ASPIRATE h, historically pronounced, which now blocks both elision and liaison even though the h itself stays silent. Dictionaries mark aspirate-h words with an asterisk.
  • Common learner mistake: adding liaison where it's forbidden (after "et") or forgetting it where it's mandatory (after plural articles: les_amis).

Practice

Read aloud: "Nous_avons deux_enfants et une amie." Notice liaison after "nous" and "deux" but not after "et".
