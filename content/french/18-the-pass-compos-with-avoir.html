The Passé Composé with Avoir

The passé composé is the everyday "I did / I have done" past tense in French — the one you'll use most in conversation. Most verbs form it with avoir as the helper.

Grammar

  • Formation: conjugated avoir (present tense) + past participle of the main verb.
  • Regular past participles:
    -er verbs -> -é (parler -> parlé)
    -ir verbs -> -i (finir -> fini)
    -re verbs -> -u (vendre -> vendu)
  • Negation wraps the helper verb: Je n'ai pas mangé. (I haven't eaten.)
  • Questions with inversion invert the helper and subject: As-tu mangé ? (Did you eat?)
  • The passé composé translates several English past forms: "I ate" / "I have eaten" / "I did eat" are all "j'ai mangé."

Reference Table

parler (to speak) — passé composé:

tu as parlé          vous avez parlé

Examples

J'ai mangé une pomme ce matin.

I ate an apple this morning.

Nous avons regardé un film hier soir.

We watched a movie last night.

Est-ce que tu as fini tes devoirs ?

Did you finish your homework?

Elle n'a pas encore répondu.

She hasn't answered yet.

Notes

  • Word order with negation and adverbs like "déjà"/"pas encore" typically slots them between the helper verb and the past participle: J'ai déjà mangé (I've already eaten).

Practice

Conjugate "finir" and "vendre" fully in the passé composé.
