Aller, Venir, Faire — and the Immediate Future & Past

Three more essential irregular verbs, plus two of the most useful shortcut constructions in French: the immediate ("near") future and immediate past.

Grammar

  • Aller (to go) is also used to form the futur proche (immediate future): aller (conjugated) + infinitive = "going to [do something]": Je vais manger. (I'm going to eat.) This is the everyday way to talk about the near future, more common in speech than the true future tense.
  • Venir (to come) is used to form the passé récent (immediate past): venir de (conjugated) + infinitive = "just [did something]": Je viens de manger. (I just ate.)
  • Faire (to do/make) appears in weather expressions (il fait beau/chaud/ froid = it's nice/hot/cold out) and countless idioms (faire les courses = to run errands, faire du sport = to play sports, faire la cuisine = to cook).
  • The causative "faire + infinitive" means to have/make someone do something, or to have something done: Je fais réparer ma voiture. (I'm having my car repaired — lit. "I make repair my car.")

Examples

Je vais partir dans dix minutes.

I'm going to leave in ten minutes.

Nous venons de finir le dîner.

We just finished dinner.

Il fait très chaud aujourd'hui.

It's very hot today.

Elle fait ses devoirs.

She's doing her homework.

Notes

  • "Venir de + infinitive" is one of the most useful shortcuts in French — it lets you express "just did X" without touching the past tense at all.

Practice

Rewrite these using futur proche and passé récent: "I am going to call you" / "We just arrived."
