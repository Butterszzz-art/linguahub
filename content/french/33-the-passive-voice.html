The Passive Voice

The passive voice shifts focus from the doer of an action to the receiver of the action ("the book was written by..." instead of "she wrote the book"). French can form it directly but often prefers alternatives.

Grammar

  • Formation: être (conjugated in whatever tense is needed) + past participle (agreeing with the subject) + optionally "par" (by) + the agent: Le livre a été écrit par Victor Hugo. (The book was written by Victor Hugo.)
  • The passive can be formed in any tense by simply changing the tense of être: Le livre est écrit (present passive) / était écrit (imparfait passive) / sera écrit (future passive), etc.
  • French uses the passive LESS often than English — it can sound heavy or overly formal. Common alternatives:
    1. Use "on" + active verb (very common, especially in speech): On parle français ici. (French is spoken here.) instead of "Le français est parlé ici."
    2. Use a reflexive/pronominal verb for general truths: Ce plat se mange froid. (This dish is eaten cold.) instead of "est mangé."
    3. Simply flip to the active voice if the agent is known and important: Victor Hugo a écrit ce livre. (Victor Hugo wrote this book.)
  • Only verbs that take a direct object (transitive verbs) can be made passive, and only the direct object can become the subject of the passive sentence — indirect objects cannot (unlike some English passives).

Reference Table

(pattern reference)
Active:  Marie écrit la lettre. (Marie writes the letter.)

Vocabulary

par
by (introduces the agent)
on
impersonal "one/people/they" (common passive alternative)
se + verb
reflexive-as-passive for general truths

Examples

La Tour Eiffel a été construite en 1889.

The Eiffel Tower was built in 1889.

Ce vin se boit frais.

This wine is drunk chilled. (reflexive, natural alternative to passive)

On m'a dit que le magasin était fermé.

I was told that the shop was closed. (on + active, very natural way to express an English passive)

Les billets seront vendus demain.

The tickets will be sold tomorrow.

Notes

  • When translating an English passive sentence into French, always ask first whether "on" or a reflexive verb would sound more natural before defaulting to the literal être + past participle construction.

Practice

Rewrite these passive English sentences using "on" in French: "French is spoken in many countries." "The window was broken." "Dinner is served at 8."
