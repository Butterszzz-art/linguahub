The Plus-que-parfait

The plus-que-parfait ("pluperfect") expresses an action that happened BEFORE another past action — the French equivalent of English "had done."

Grammar

  • Formation: imparfait of avoir or être (same helper-verb rule as passé composé) + past participle.
  • Used to show that one past event occurred earlier than another past event already being discussed (usually in imparfait or passé composé): Quand je suis arrivé, elle était déjà partie. (When I arrived, she had already left — her leaving happened before my arriving.)
  • Agreement rules for the past participle are identical to the passé composé (agree with subject for être verbs, agree with a preceding direct object for avoir verbs — see Lesson 19).
  • Often appears with "déjà" (already) or in reported/indirect speech referring back to an earlier past event.

Reference Table

parler (to speak) — plus-que-parfait:
j'avais parlé          nous avions parlé
tu avais parlé           vous aviez parlé
il/elle/on avait parlé    ils/elles avaient parlé

aller (to go) — plus-que-parfait:
j'étais allé(e)          nous étions allé(e)s
tu étais allé(e)           vous étiez allé(e)(s)
il était allé / elle était allée   ils étaient allés / elles étaient allées

Vocabulary

déjà
already
avant que
before
ne...jamais (+ plus-que-parfait)
had never

Examples

Il avait déjà mangé quand nous sommes arrivés.

He had already eaten when we arrived.

Je n'avais jamais visité Paris avant ce voyage.

I had never visited Paris before this trip.

Elle était déjà partie quand j'ai téléphoné.

She had already left when I called.

Nous avions fini nos devoirs avant le dîner.

We had finished our homework before dinner.

Notes

  • Think of the plus-que-parfait as "the past of the past" — it always sets up a relationship to another, more recent past event mentioned nearby.

Practice

Combine each pair of events using plus-que-parfait + passé composé: "I arrived at the station. The train had already left." / "She called me. I had already gone to bed."
