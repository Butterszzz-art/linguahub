-ER Verbs with Spelling and Stem Changes

A subset of -er verbs make small spelling adjustments to preserve their sound across the conjugation. These are still "regular" in logic, just with a predictable twist.

Grammar

  • Verbs ending in -cer: c becomes ç before -ons (to keep the soft "s" sound): commencer -> nous commençons.
  • Verbs ending in -ger: g gets an extra e before -ons (to keep the soft "zh" sound): manger -> nous mangeons.
  • Verbs ending in -e + consonant + er (like acheter, lever): the mute "e" becomes è in all forms EXCEPT nous/vous: j'achète, tu achètes, il achète, ils achètent, but nous achetons, vous achetez.
  • Verbs ending in -é + consonant + er (like préférer, espérer): é becomes è in the same pattern (except nous/vous): je préfère, but nous préférons.
  • Verbs ending in -eler/-eter: some double the consonant instead of adding an accent: appeler -> j'appelle, nous appelons; jeter -> je jette, nous jetons.
  • Verbs ending in -yer: y becomes i before a silent ending (everywhere except nous/vous): payer -> je paie, nous payons. (For -ayer verbs like payer, keeping the y — je paye — is also accepted.)

Examples

Nous commençons le cours à neuf heures.

We start class at 9:00.

Je préfère le café au thé.

I prefer coffee to tea.

Elle achète des légumes au marché.

She buys vegetables at the market.

Nous voyageons souvent en été.

We travel often in summer.

Notes

  • These changes exist purely to preserve pronunciation — say the verb out loud and the spelling logic usually becomes obvious.
