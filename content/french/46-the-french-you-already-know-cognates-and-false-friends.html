The French You Already Know — Cognates and False Friends

English borrowed enormously from French after the Norman Conquest, so you already recognize thousands of French words. This lesson teaches you to spot true cognates quickly and avoid the "false friends" that trip up beginners.

Grammar

  • True cognates (bons alliés — "good allies"): words that look alike and mean the same thing: nation, important, hôpital, restaurant, hôtel, possible, nécessaire. Many English words ending in -tion, -ent, -ant, -ble, -ique map directly to French -tion, -ent, -ant, -ble, -ique.
  • Partial cognates ("kissing cousins"): recognizably related but not identical in form or exact meaning: éducation (education), université (university), différent (different).
  • False friends (faux amis): look similar but mean something different — a major source of embarrassing mistakes:
    assister à = to attend (NOT "to assist" — that's aider)
    attendre = to wait for (NOT "to attend" — that's assister à)
    la librairie = the bookstore (NOT "library" — that's la bibliothèque)
    la lecture = reading (NOT "lecture" — that's la conférence)
    sensible = sensitive (NOT "sensible" — that's raisonnable/sensé)
    demander = to ask (NOT "to demand" — that's exiger)
    rester = to stay (NOT "to rest" — that's se reposer)
    actuellement = currently/now (NOT "actually" — that's en fait)
    blesser = to injure/hurt (NOT "to bless" — that's bénir)
    une femme = a woman (not related to English "femme" as in "femme fatale" in meaning-shift terms, but note it also means "wife")
  • Borrowed English words in French (anglicismes): le week-end, le shopping, le sport, un e-mail, cool, le parking — common in casual speech, though official/formal French often prefers a French alternative (le courriel for e-mail, in Québec especially).

Examples

Je vais assister à la réunion demain.

I'm going to attend the meeting tomorrow.

J'ai acheté ce livre à la librairie.

I bought this book at the bookstore.

Elle est très sensible aux critiques.

She's very sensitive to criticism.

Actuellement, je travaille à Paris.

Currently, I work in Paris. (NOT "actually" — a classic false-friend trap)

Notes

  • When you meet a new French word that looks EXACTLY like an English word, pause and double-check its meaning before assuming — false friends are one of the most common sources of miscommunication for intermediate learners specifically (beginners are more cautious; intermediate learners often over-trust resemblance).

Practice

Translate correctly, avoiding the false-friend trap: "I'm going to attend the concert." "She works at the library." "He's a sensible person" (reasonable, not sensitive).
