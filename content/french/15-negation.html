Negation

The basic negative structure and its variations — how to say "not," "never," "nothing," "no longer," and more.

Grammar

  • Basic negation wraps the conjugated verb in ne...pas: Je ne parle pas espagnol. (I don't speak Spanish.) "Ne" becomes "n'" before a vowel/mute h.
  • With reflexive verbs, "ne" goes before the reflexive pronoun: Je ne me lève pas. (I don't get up.)
  • With compound tenses (passé composé, Part 3), ne...pas wraps the helper verb, not the past participle: Je n'ai pas mangé. (I haven't eaten.)
  • Other negative expressions follow the same "ne...X" pattern, with X replacing "pas":
    ne...jamais - never
    ne...rien - nothing
    ne...personne - nobody/no one
    ne...plus - no longer/no more
    ne...ni...ni - neither...nor
    ne...que - only (restrictive, not a true negation)
    ne...aucun(e) - none/not any
  • After a negation, indefinite/partitive articles (un/une/des/du/de la) usually shrink to "de" (see Lesson 4): J'ai des amis -> Je n'ai pas d'amis.
  • "Personne" and "rien" can also start a sentence as the subject, still paired with "ne": Personne ne sait. (No one knows.) Rien n'est facile. (Nothing is easy.)

Examples

Je ne mange jamais de viande.

I never eat meat.

Il n'y a rien dans le frigo.

There's nothing in the fridge.

Nous n'avons plus de pain.

We don't have any more bread.

Elle ne parle à personne.

She doesn't talk to anyone.

Je n'ai qu'un frère.

I only have one brother.

Notes

  • In casual spoken French, "ne" is very often dropped entirely (Je sais pas instead of Je ne sais pas), but you should always include it in writing and formal speech.

Practice

Negate these sentences using the indicated expression: "Je bois du café" (ne...jamais); "Il y a quelqu'un" (ne...personne); "J'ai des devoirs" (ne...plus).
