Être and Avoir (To Be and To Have)

Être and avoir are the two most important irregular verbs in French — they appear constantly on their own AND as helper verbs for compound tenses (Part 3), so full memorization now pays off for the rest of the course.

Grammar

  • Être (to be) and avoir (to have) are both completely irregular and must be memorized outright.
  • Avoir is used in many idiomatic expressions where English uses "to be": avoir faim (to be hungry), avoir soif (to be thirsty), avoir chaud/froid (to be hot/cold), avoir peur (to be afraid), avoir raison/tort (to be right/wrong), avoir X ans (to be X years old), avoir besoin de (to need), avoir envie de (to feel like / want).
  • "Il y a" (there is / there are) is built from avoir and doesn't change for number: Il y a un chat. / Il y a deux chats.

Reference Table

être (to be): je suis, tu es, il/elle/on est,
  nous sommes, vous êtes, ils/elles sont
avoir (to have): j'ai, tu as, il/elle/on a,
  nous avons, vous avez, ils/elles ont

Examples

Je suis étudiant et j'ai vingt ans.

I am a student and I am 20 years old.

Nous avons faim, allons manger !

We're hungry, let's go eat!

Il y a beaucoup de monde ici.

There are a lot of people here.

Vous êtes en retard.

You are late.

Notes

  • These two verbs are the base for the passé composé (Part 3) — memorizing them solidly now will make the past tense much easier later.

Practice

Translate using avoir-expressions: "I'm cold," "She's 30 years old," "We need a car," "They're afraid of the dog."
