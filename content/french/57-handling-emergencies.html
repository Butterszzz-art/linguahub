Handling Emergencies

Critical survival vocabulary for medical emergencies, lost items, and contacting emergency services — the kind of phrases you hope not to need but should know cold.

Grammar

  • "Au secours !" (Help!) is the general emergency call for immediate danger; "J'ai besoin d'aide" (I need help) is used for less urgent requests.
  • Describing symptoms uses "avoir mal à + definite article + body part": J'ai mal à la tête. (I have a headache.) J'ai mal au ventre. (My stomach hurts.)
  • Emergency numbers in France: 15 (SAMU, medical), 17 (police), 18 (fire/pompiers), 112 (general EU emergency number, works from any phone).

Examples

Au secours, appelez une ambulance !

Help, call an ambulance!

J'ai mal au ventre depuis ce matin.

My stomach has hurt since this morning.

On m'a volé mon sac ; je dois aller au commissariat.

My bag was stolen; I need to go to the police station.

Où est la pharmacie la plus proche ?

Where is the nearest pharmacy?

Notes

  • If you're in genuine danger in France, dial 112 — it connects to emergency services from any phone, including without a French SIM card, and operators can often assist in English.

Practice

Memorize how to say: "Help, I need a doctor," "I lost my passport," and "Where is the nearest hospital?"
