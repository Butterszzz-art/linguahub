The Passé Simple

The passé simple is a literary/formal past tense — you'll almost never use it in speech, but you need to RECOGNIZE it to read novels, newspapers, historical writing, and formal narration. It covers the same ground as the passé composé but in a more formal written register.

Grammar

  • Formation for regular verbs — drop the infinitive ending and add:
    -er verbs: -ai, -as, -a, -âmes, -âtes, -èrent (parler -> il parla)
    -ir/-re verbs: -is, -is, -it, -îmes, -îtes, -irent (finir -> il finit; vendre -> il vendit)
  • Many irregular verbs have their own passé simple stem, often related to the past participle: avoir -> il eut; être -> il fut; faire -> il fit; venir -> il vint; voir -> il vit; prendre -> il prit.
  • In modern usage, the passé simple survives almost exclusively in the 3rd person (il/elle/on and ils/elles) — the 1st/2nd person forms are rare even in literature, replaced by passé composé in nearly all contexts.
  • Recognition tip: if you see an unfamiliar verb form ending in -a, -it, -ut, -int, or -èrent/-irent/-urent in a story or historical text, it's very likely a passé simple form of a verb you already know in another tense.

Reference Table

parler (to speak) — passé simple (3rd person, most useful to recognize):
il/elle/on parla        ils/elles parlèrent
finir (to finish) — passé simple:
il/elle/on finit         ils/elles finirent
être (to be) — passé simple (irregular):
il/elle/on fut            ils/elles furent
avoir (to have) — passé simple (irregular):
il/elle/on eut            ils/elles eurent

Vocabulary

il était une fois
once upon a time
soudain
suddenly
alors
then/so
(the passé simple is common in fairy tales and historical narration)

Examples

Il était une fois une princesse qui vivait dans un château. Un jour, elle rencontra un dragon.

Once upon a time there was a princess who lived in a castle. One day, she met a dragon. ("rencontra" = passé simple of rencontrer)

Napoléon naquit en 1769 et mourut en 1821.

Napoleon was born in 1769 and died in 1821. (naquit/mourut = passé simple, common in history texts)

Notes

  • For self-study purposes, focus on RECOGNIZING the 3rd-person singular and plural forms in reading — you do not need to actively produce this tense in conversation or everyday writing.

Practice

In a French news article, fairy tale, or history text, find 3 passé simple verbs and identify their infinitive and their passé composé equivalent.
