Direct Object Pronouns

Direct object pronouns replace a noun that directly receives the action of the verb (no preposition involved) — "me, you, him, her, it, us, them" in English. In French, they move BEFORE the conjugated verb, a major structural difference from English.

Grammar

  • Direct object pronouns: me/m' (me), te/t' (you, informal), le/l' (him/ it, masc.), la/l' (her/it, fem.), nous (us), vous (you), les (them).
  • Placement: directly before the conjugated verb (not after, as in English): Je le vois. (I see it/him.) In the negative: Je ne le vois pas. (I don't see it/him.)
  • With an infinitive construction, the pronoun goes before the infinitive it belongs to, not the conjugated verb: Je vais le voir. (I'm going to see it/him.)
  • In the passé composé, the pronoun goes before the helper verb, and the past participle AGREES in gender/number with a preceding direct object pronoun: Je l'ai vue. (I saw her — "vue" agrees with "la/l'" = her, feminine.) J'ai vu Marie -> Je l'ai vue.
  • In the affirmative imperative, the pronoun follows the verb (hyphenated, and "me/te" become "moi/toi"): Regarde-le ! (Look at it/him!) In the negative imperative, normal order returns: Ne le regarde pas !

Examples

Tu connais Marc ? Oui, je le connais bien.

Do you know Marc? Yes, I know him well.

J'ai acheté ces chaussures parce que je les aimais.

I bought these shoes because I liked them.

Elle a écrit la lettre et l'a envoyée hier.

She wrote the letter and sent it yesterday. (agreement: envoyée, feminine, agrees with "la lettre")

Aide-moi, s'il te plaît !

Help me, please!

Notes

  • The past-participle agreement rule (only with a PRECEDING direct object) trips up even advanced learners — it applies with direct object pronouns and with relative pronoun "que," but never with indirect object pronouns (see Lesson 38).
