-IR and -RE Verbs in the Present Tense

After -er verbs, French has two more regular families: -ir verbs (like finir) and -re verbs (like vendre), each with their own predictable ending pattern.

Grammar

  • Regular -ir verbs: remove -ir from the infinitive, add:
    -is, -is, -it, -issons, -issez, -issent Note the -iss- that appears in the plural forms only.
  • Regular -re verbs: remove -re from the infinitive, add:
    -s, -s, - (nothing), -ons, -ez, -ent The il/elle/on form has NO added ending — you just use the bare stem.
  • Not all -ir verbs follow this pattern (many common ones like partir, sortir, venir are irregular — covered later), but the -iss- pattern covers a large regular group (finir, choisir, réussir, grandir...).

Reference Table

finir (to finish): je finis, tu finis, il finit,
  nous finissons, vous finissez, ils finissent
vendre (to sell): je vends, tu vends, il vend,
  nous vendons, vous vendez, ils vendent

Examples

Je finis mes devoirs à six heures.

I finish my homework at 6:00.

Elle choisit une robe rouge.

She chooses a red dress.

Nous attendons le bus.

We're waiting for the bus.

Ils vendent leur voiture.

They're selling their car.

Notes

  • "Attendre" means "to wait for" — no separate preposition needed, unlike English "wait FOR": J'attends Marie (I'm waiting for Marie), not "j'attends pour Marie."
