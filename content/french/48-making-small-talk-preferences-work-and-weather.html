Making Small Talk — Preferences, Work, and Weather

The everyday chit-chat toolkit: asking about likes/dislikes, talking about your job, and — a French conversational staple — discussing the weather.

Grammar

  • "Aimer" (to like/love), "adorer" (to adore), "détester" (to hate), "préférer" (to prefer) are all followed directly by a noun (with a definite article for general preferences) or an infinitive: J'aime le chocolat. J'aime lire.
  • Talking about your job: "Je suis + [profession, NO article]": Je suis professeur. (I am a teacher.) — unlike English, no "a/an" before an unmodified profession. If the profession is modified by an adjective, the article returns: Je suis un bon professeur. (I am a good teacher.)
  • Weather expressions mostly use "il fait" + adjective, or specific verbs: Il fait beau/mauvais/chaud/froid/frais. (It's nice/bad/hot/cold/cool.) Il pleut. (It's raining.) Il neige. (It's snowing.) Il y a du vent/du soleil/des nuages. (It's windy/sunny/cloudy.)

Examples

Qu'est-ce que vous faites comme travail ? Je suis ingénieur.

What do you do for work? I'm an engineer.

J'adore la randonnée, mais je déteste le sport en salle.

I love hiking, but I hate working out at the gym.

Il fait un temps magnifique aujourd'hui, n'est-ce pas ?

The weather is gorgeous today, isn't it?

On reste en contact !

Let's keep in touch!

Notes

  • Talking about the weather is a genuinely common French social ritual (much like in English) — a safe, friendly way to start or fill a conversation with someone you don't know well.

Practice

Write a short small-talk exchange: greeting, asking about work, sharing a hobby preference, commenting on the weather.
