The Imperative Mood

The imperative is used for commands, requests, and suggestions — it exists only for tu, nous, and vous (no subject pronoun is stated).

Grammar

  • Formation: take the present-tense tu/nous/vous form of the verb and drop the subject pronoun. For -er verbs (and verbs like ouvrir that conjugate like -er verbs), also drop the final -s from the tu form:
    parler: (tu) Parle ! / (nous) Parlons ! / (vous) Parlez !
    finir: (tu) Finis ! / (nous) Finissons ! / (vous) Finissez !
    vendre: (tu) Vends ! / (nous) Vendons ! / (vous) Vendez !
  • Être, avoir, and savoir have irregular imperative forms (borrowed from the subjunctive stem): Sois sage ! (Be good!) / Ayons du courage ! (Let's have courage!) / Sachez que... (Know that...).
  • With reflexive verbs (Lesson 14): in the AFFIRMATIVE, the reflexive pronoun moves after the verb (hyphenated), and "te" becomes "toi": Lève-toi ! (Get up!) Asseyez-vous ! (Sit down!) In the NEGATIVE, normal word order is kept: Ne te lève pas ! (Don't get up!)
  • With object pronouns, the same "flip" rule applies: in the affirmative, pronouns follow the verb, connected with hyphens (Donne-le-moi ! = Give it to me!); in the negative, they stay in normal position before the verb (Ne me le donne pas ! = Don't give it to me!).
  • To soften a command into a suggestion, French often uses the conditional instead of a true imperative: Tu devrais te reposer. (You should rest.) is gentler than Repose-toi ! (Rest!)

Reference Table

parler (to speak) — imperative:
(tu) Parle !     (nous) Parlons !     (vous) Parlez !
se lever (to get up) — imperative:
(tu) Lève-toi !   (nous) Levons-nous !   (vous) Levez-vous !
  negative: Ne te lève pas ! / Ne nous levons pas ! / Ne vous levez pas !

Vocabulary

Attention !
Watch out!/Be careful!
Dépêche-toi ! / Dépêchez-vous !

Écoute(z) !
Listen!
N'oublie(z) pas de...

Examples

Ferme la porte, s'il te plaît.

Close the door, please.

Asseyez-vous et détendez-vous.

Sit down and relax.

Ne t'inquiète pas, tout ira bien.

Don't worry, everything will be fine.

Allons-y !

Let's go!

Donnez-le-moi tout de suite.

Give it to me right away.

Notes

  • "Allons-y !" (Let's go!) and "Vas-y !" / "Allez-y !" (Go on!/Go for it!) are extremely common everyday expressions built on the imperative of aller + the pronoun "y."
  • Remember: aller's tu-form imperative keeps the -s only when followed by "y" for pronunciation reasons: "Va !" but "Vas-y !"
