Dining Out and Grocery Shopping

Ordering at a restaurant and shopping for food at a market — two of the most immediately useful practical skills for any French learner.

Grammar

  • "Je voudrais" (I would like — conditional of vouloir, Lesson 26) is the standard polite way to order: Je voudrais un café, s'il vous plaît.
  • Partitive articles (Lesson 4) are essential for ordering/buying food in quantity: du pain, de la viande, des légumes.
  • "Prendre" is commonly used at restaurants for "to have/order": Je vais prendre le poisson. (I'll have the fish.)
  • Asking for the check: "L'addition, s'il vous plaît."

Examples

Je voudrais réserver une table pour deux personnes.

I would like to reserve a table for two.

Qu'est-ce que vous me recommandez comme plat principal ?

What do you recommend as a main course?

Je vais prendre une salade et de l'eau minérale.

I'll have a salad and some mineral water.

Un kilo de pommes et une baguette, s'il vous plaît.

A kilo of apples and a baguette, please.

Notes

  • In France, "le menu" refers to a fixed-price set meal, NOT the full list of options — the full list is "la carte." Asking for "le menu" gets you the day's set offer, not the whole menu in the English sense.

Practice

Write a short restaurant dialogue: greeting, asking for a recommendation, ordering a starter and a main course, and asking for the check.
