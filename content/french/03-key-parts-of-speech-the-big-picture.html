Key Parts of Speech — The Big Picture

Before diving into verb tenses and pronoun rules, it helps to see the whole map: what a noun, article, pronoun, adjective, and adverb do in a French sentence, and how French sentence order compares to English.

Grammar

  • Nouns (les noms) always have a gender (masculine or feminine) in French, even for objects with no natural gender (la table = the table, f.; le livre = the book, m.). Gender is not predictable from meaning and must largely be memorized with the noun.
  • Articles (les articles) agree in gender and number with the noun they introduce: le/la/les (the), un/une/des (a/an/some), du/de la/des (some, partitive). Covered in depth in Lesson 4 and Part 6.
  • Subject pronouns (je, tu, il/elle/on, nous, vous, ils/elles) replace a noun as the subject of a verb. Note "tu" (informal "you", one person) vs. "vous" (formal "you" singular, OR plural "you" — always).
  • Adjectives (les adjectifs) agree in gender and number with the noun they describe, and in French MOST adjectives come AFTER the noun (une voiture rouge = a red car), unlike English. A short list of common adjectives (beauty, age, goodness, size — remembered by the acronym BAGS) come BEFORE the noun (une belle voiture = a beautiful car).
  • Adverbs (les adverbes) modify verbs, adjectives, or other adverbs and are usually invariable (they don't change form). Many are formed by adding -ment to the feminine form of an adjective, similar to English "-ly" (lente -> lentement = slowly).
  • Basic word order: Subject + Verb + Object, same as English: Je mange une pomme. (I eat an apple.) The big structural differences come with object pronouns (which move BEFORE the verb — see Part 6) and adjective placement (see above).

Examples

Le petit chat noir dort.

The little black cat is sleeping. (petit = BAGS adjective, before noun; noir = color, after noun)

Elle parle lentement.

She speaks slowly. (adverb from adjective "lente")

Nous aimons la musique française.

We love French music.

Notes

  • "On" technically means "one/people in general" but in everyday spoken French it's used constantly to mean "we" (with a 3rd-person singular verb): On mange ? = Shall we eat?
  • Because most adjectives follow the noun, and a handful precede it, memorize the BAGS list early: Beauty (beau, joli), Age (jeune, vieux, nouveau), Goodness (bon, mauvais), Size (grand, petit, gros).

Practice

Identify the part of speech of each word in: "Ma petite sœur parle rapidement." (ma=possessive adj., petite=adjective, sœur=noun, parle=verb, rapidement=adverb)
