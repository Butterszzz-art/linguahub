Articles and Nouns In Depth

Building on Lesson 4, this lesson covers noun gender/plural patterns more thoroughly and the special rules for geographical names — a notoriously tricky corner of French grammar.

Grammar

  • Gender patterns (useful but not 100% reliable):
    Masculine endings: -age, -al, -ail, -eau, -isme, -ment, -oir
    Feminine endings: -ade, -ance/-ence, -ée, -elle, -ette, -euse, -ie, -ion/-sion/-tion, -té, -ure
    Exceptions exist for almost every rule (le musée, la page) — always
    learn gender with the noun.
  • Some nouns change MEANING with gender: le livre (the book) vs. la livre (the pound — weight or currency); le poste (job position) vs. la poste (the post office); le tour (a turn/trip) vs. la tour (a tower).
  • Plural formation beyond the basic -s (Lesson 4):
    -eau/-eu -> -eaux/-eux (le bateau -> les bateaux; le jeu -> les jeux)
    -al -> -aux (le cheval -> les chevaux) — with exceptions (le bal -> les bals)
    -s/-x/-z endings don't change (le prix -> les prix)
    A handful are fully irregular: l'œil -> les yeux (eye -> eyes), madame -> mesdames
  • Gender of countries: countries ending in -e are usually feminine (la France, la Chine — with exceptions like le Mexique); most others are masculine (le Japon, le Canada, le Portugal); a few are plural (les États-Unis, les Pays-Bas).
  • Prepositions with geographical names:
    to/in + feminine country or country starting with a vowel -> EN: en France, en Iran
    to/in + masculine country -> AU: au Japon, au Canada
    to/in + plural country -> AUX: aux États-Unis
    to/in + city -> À (no article): à Paris, à Tokyo
    from + feminine country -> DE/D': de France
    from + masculine country -> DU: du Japon
    from + plural country -> DES: des États-Unis

Examples

Elle habite en France mais elle vient du Canada.

She lives in France but she's from Canada.

Nous allons au Japon cet été, puis aux Philippines.

We're going to Japan this summer, then to the Philippines.

J'ai perdu mes clés — je ne sais pas où elles sont.

I lost my keys — I don't know where they are. (les clés, always feminine plural)

Il pèse une livre de plus qu'avant.

He weighs one pound more than before. (la livre = pound, weight — contrast with le livre = book)

Notes

  • When a new noun's gender isn't obvious from the ending, just memorize "le/la + noun" as a single unit from the start — retrofitting gender later is much harder.
