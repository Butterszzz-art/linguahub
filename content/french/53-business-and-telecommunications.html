Business and Telecommunications

Phone, email, and office vocabulary for professional and practical communication.

Grammar

  • On the phone, French uses specific set phrases rather than direct translations: "Allô ?" (Hello? — phone-specific, not used face-to-face) "C'est de la part de qui ?" (Who's calling? lit. "It's on behalf of whom?") "Ne quittez pas." (Please hold, lit. "Don't leave.")
  • "Je voudrais laisser un message" (I'd like to leave a message) uses the conditional for politeness (Lesson 26), as in many service contexts.

Examples

Allô, bonjour, je voudrais parler à Madame Dubois, s'il vous plaît.

Hello, I'd like to speak with Ms. Dubois, please.

C'est de la part de qui ?

Who's calling?

Elle est en réunion, voulez-vous laisser un message ?

She's in a meeting, would you like to leave a message?

Je vous envoie les documents par e-mail cet après-midi.

I'll send you the documents by email this afternoon.

Notes

  • "Allô" is used ONLY to answer/begin a phone call — never as a general greeting in person, which would sound very odd.

Practice

Write a short phone dialogue: answering, asking to speak with someone, being told they're unavailable, and leaving a message.
