The Present Participle and the Gerund

French has an -ant form (like English "-ing") used in two related but distinct ways: as a present participle (adjectival/verbal) and as a gerund introduced by "en" (showing simultaneity or manner).

Grammar

  • Formation: take the nous form of the present tense, drop -ons, add -ant: parlons -> parlant; finissons -> finissant; faisons -> faisant. Three irregular exceptions: être -> étant, avoir -> ayant, savoir -> sachant.
  • The present participle (participe présent) can function like an adjective (agreeing in gender/number) or describe an action happening alongside the main verb without "en": Les enfants, courant dans le jardin, riaient fort. (The children, running in the garden, were laughing loudly.)
  • The gerund (le gérondif) is "en" + present participle, and expresses:
    - simultaneity ("while doing"): Elle chante en conduisant. (She sings while driving.)
    - manner ("by doing"): Il a appris le français en regardant des films. (He learned French by watching movies.)
    - The gerund's subject must be the SAME as the main clause's subject.
  • Important contrast with English: French normally does NOT use an -ant form after most conjugated verbs the way English uses "-ing" after verbs like "like/start/stop" — French uses the infinitive instead (J'aime lire, not "j'aime lisant" — see Lesson 31).

Reference Table

(formation reference)
parler -> parlant   finir -> finissant   vendre -> vendant
être -> étant   avoir -> ayant   savoir -> sachant

Vocabulary

en + [participe présent]
while/by (doing)
tout en + [participe présent]
all while (doing) — emphasizes simultaneity/contrast

Examples

Elle regarde la télé en mangeant.

She watches TV while eating.

Il est devenu riche en travaillant dur.

He became rich by working hard.

Tout en sachant la vérité, il n'a rien dit.

While knowing the truth [even though he knew], he said nothing.

Ayant fini son travail, elle est sortie se promener.

Having finished her work, she went out for a walk. (present participle, no "en")

Notes

  • Don't confuse the gerund (en + -ant, showing HOW/WHEN) with simply using the infinitive after most verbs — when in doubt about a verb + verb construction, the infinitive (Lesson 31) is far more often correct than the gerund.

Practice

Write three sentences describing how you do something using "en + gérondif" ("I learn French by...", "I relax by...", etc.).
