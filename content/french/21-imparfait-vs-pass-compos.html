Imparfait vs. Passé Composé

This is the single trickiest contrast for English speakers learning French past tenses — both translate to English simple past, but they express fundamentally different things.

Grammar

  • Passé composé = a completed, bounded action or event — something that happened and finished, often with a clear beginning/end or a specific number of occurrences: J'ai mangé à midi. (I ate at noon — one specific, completed event.)
  • Imparfait = an ongoing state, background description, habitual action, or a scene being "set" — no clear boundary on when it started or stopped: Je mangeais quand tu as appelé. (I was eating [ongoing] when you called [completed interruption].)
  • A useful test: if you can insert "was/were -ing" or "used to" in English and it still makes sense, it's imparfait. If the action is a single completed event you could put on a timeline with a clear end, it's passé composé.
  • The same verb can appear in either tense with a real meaning shift:
    Il faisait beau. (It was nice out — ongoing description.)
    Il a fait beau toute la journée. (It was nice ALL DAY — a bounded, completed period, viewed as one whole block of time.)
  • Certain verbs (connaître, pouvoir, savoir, vouloir) shift MEANING depending on which past tense is used:
    je connaissais (imparfait) = I knew (a person, over time) vs. j'ai connu (passé composé) = I met (for the first time)
    je pouvais (imparfait) = I was able to (in general/had the ability) vs. j'ai pu (passé composé) = I managed to / succeeded in (one specific instance)
    je savais (imparfait) = I knew (a fact, ongoing) vs. j'ai su (passé composé) = I found out / learned (at a specific moment)
    je voulais (imparfait) = I wanted (ongoing desire) vs. j'ai voulu (passé composé) = I decided to / tried to (and often failed); je n'ai pas voulu = I refused

Examples

J'étais à la maison quand le téléphone a sonné.

I was at home when the phone rang. (imparfait = background state; passé composé = the event)

Nous parlions français ensemble quand nous étions enfants.

We used to speak French together when we were children. (both imparfait — habitual background)

Elle a couru pour attraper le bus.

She ran to catch the bus. (single completed action)

Je ne savais pas que tu étais marié. J'ai su hier.

I didn't know you were married. I found out yesterday. (meaning shift: savais=knew, ongoing; ai su=found out, punctual)

Notes

  • Don't overthink single-word translation — think in terms of "is this a photograph (imparfait, a state/scene) or a video clip with a clear start and end (passé composé, an event)?"

Practice

Write a 4–5 sentence short story about last weekend, deliberately mixing imparfait (for background/description) and passé composé (for the actions that happened).
