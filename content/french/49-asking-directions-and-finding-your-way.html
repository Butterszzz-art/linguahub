Asking Directions and Finding Your Way

Essential survival vocabulary for navigating a French-speaking city — asking where something is and understanding the directions you're given.

Grammar

  • "Où" + est/se trouve + [place] is the standard way to ask where something is: Où est la gare ? / Où se trouve la pharmacie ? (Both mean "Where is the [place]?" — "se trouver" literally means "to find itself/be located.")
  • Direction/command vocabulary usually appears in the imperative (Lesson 35): Tournez à droite. (Turn right.) Continuez tout droit. (Keep going straight.) Traversez la rue. (Cross the street.)
  • "Prendre" is used for taking a route/street/transport: Prenez la première rue à gauche. (Take the first street on the left.)

Examples

Excusez-moi, où est la station de métro la plus proche ?

Excuse me, where is the nearest metro station?

Continuez tout droit, puis tournez à gauche au deuxième feu.

Keep going straight, then turn left at the second light.

La banque est en face de la boulangerie.

The bank is across from the bakery.

C'est à cinq minutes à pied d'ici.

It's a five-minute walk from here.

Notes

  • "Se trouver" (to be located) is very commonly used in place of "être" when talking about the location of a fixed place — Où se trouve la poste ? sounds just as natural as Où est la poste ?
