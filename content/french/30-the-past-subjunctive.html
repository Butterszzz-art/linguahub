The Past Subjunctive

The past subjunctive expresses the same subjective ideas as the present subjunctive (wish, doubt, emotion, necessity) but about an action that happened BEFORE the time of the main verb.

Grammar

  • Formation: present subjunctive of avoir or être (same helper-verb choice as usual) + past participle.
  • Used with the same trigger expressions as the present subjunctive (Lesson 29), but when the action in the "que" clause is already completed relative to the main clause: Je suis content(e) que tu sois venu(e). (I'm glad you came — the coming happened before the being glad.)
  • Agreement rules for the past participle follow the passé composé pattern.

Reference Table

parler (to speak) — past subjunctive:
que j'aie parlé          que nous ayons parlé
que tu aies parlé          que vous ayez parlé
qu'il/elle/on ait parlé     qu'ils/elles aient parlé

aller (to go) — past subjunctive:
que je sois allé(e)          que nous soyons allé(e)s
que tu sois allé(e)           que vous soyez allé(e)(s)
qu'il soit allé / qu'elle soit allée   qu'ils soient allés / qu'elles soient
  allées

Vocabulary

(same trigger expressions as Lesson 29 — il faut que, avoir peur que,
bien que, etc. — used here about a completed past action)

Examples

Je suis surpris(e) qu'elle ait déjà fini.

I'm surprised she's already finished.

Bien que nous soyons arrivés en retard, le spectacle n'avait pas commencé.

Although we arrived late, the show hadn't started.

Il est possible qu'il ait oublié notre rendez-vous.

It's possible he forgot our appointment.

Notes

  • The past subjunctive is much less common than the present subjunctive in everyday speech — recognize it and use it when the timing genuinely matters, but don't stress over it in early practice.

Practice

Write two sentences using "je suis content(e) que" + past subjunctive about something that already happened.
