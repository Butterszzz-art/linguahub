Greetings, Introductions, and Polite Expressions

The essential social toolkit: how to greet people, introduce yourself, choose between formal and informal address, and use the core polite phrases that keep every interaction smooth.

Grammar

  • Formal vs. informal "you": use "tu" with family, friends, children, and peers your own age in casual settings. Use "vous" with strangers, older people, in professional settings, or anyone you're not on familiar terms with. When unsure, default to "vous" — it's never rude to be too formal, but it can be rude to be too familiar.
  • Greetings change with time of day: bonjour (hello/good day, used until evening), bonsoir (good evening), bonne nuit (good night — only when parting for the night/going to bed, not a greeting).
  • "Comment allez-vous ?" (formal) / "Comment vas-tu ?" or "Ça va ?" (informal) = "How are you?" Common replies: "Je vais bien, merci." (I'm doing well, thanks.) or simply "Ça va." (I'm okay / So-so.)
  • To introduce yourself: "Je m'appelle..." (My name is...) or "Je suis..." (I am...). To introduce someone else: "Je vous présente..." (formal) / "Je te présente..." (informal) (Let me introduce you to...).
  • "Enchanté(e)" (Pleased to meet you) — agrees in gender with the speaker (a man says "enchanté", a woman says "enchantée").

Examples

Bonjour, madame. Comment allez-vous ?

Hello, ma'am. How are you?

Salut ! Ça va ?

Hi! How's it going? (informal)

Je m'appelle Thomas, et vous ?

My name is Thomas, and you?

Enchantée de faire votre connaissance.

Pleased to make your acquaintance. (a woman speaking, formal register)

Notes

  • "Salut" works both as "hi" and "bye" but only in informal contexts — never use it with someone you'd address as "vous."
  • Address strangers as "monsieur"/"madame" even without a name — dropping it can sound abrupt in French, unlike English.

Practice

Write a short formal exchange (greeting, "how are you," introduce yourself) and then rewrite it informally, changing vous->tu and adjusting vocabulary.
