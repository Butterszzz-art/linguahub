Relative Pronouns

Relative pronouns connect a clause back to a noun already mentioned ("the book THAT I read," "the woman WHO called") — French has more of these than English, each with a specific grammatical role.

Grammar

  • QUI = subject of the relative clause (who/which/that, doing the action). Never elides, never changes for gender/number: La femme qui parle est ma tante. (The woman who is speaking is my aunt.)
  • QUE (QU') = direct object of the relative clause (whom/which/that, receiving the action). Elides before a vowel. Triggers past-participle agreement in compound tenses (Lesson 37): Le livre que j'ai lu était excellent. (The book that I read was excellent — "lu" would agree if feminine.)
  • OÙ = place or time (where/when): La ville où j'habite est petite. (The city where I live is small.) Le jour où je suis né... (The day when I was born...)
  • DONT = replaces "de + noun" (whose, of which, about which/whom) — used whenever the verb or expression in the relative clause normally takes "de": C'est le livre dont je parle. (It's the book I'm talking about — from "parler de.") La fille dont le père est médecin... (The girl whose father is a doctor... — "dont" replaces "le père DE la fille.")
  • LEQUEL/LAQUELLE/LESQUELS/LESQUELLES = used after a preposition (other than "de," which prefers dont) when referring to a THING (not usually a person): Le stylo avec lequel j'écris... (The pen [that] I write with...) Contracts with à/de: auquel, duquel, auxquels, etc. For PEOPLE after a preposition, "qui" is generally preferred instead: la personne à qui je parle (the person I'm speaking to).
  • CE QUI / CE QUE / CE DONT / CE À QUOI = "what" (the thing which/that), used when there's no specific noun antecedent, only an idea: Je ne sais pas ce qui se passe. (I don't know what's happening — ce qui = subject.) Dis-moi ce que tu veux. (Tell me what you want — ce que = direct object.) C'est ce dont j'ai besoin. (That's what I need — ce dont, from "avoir besoin de.")

Reference Table

qui (subject) - que (direct object) - où (place/time) - dont (de + noun)
lequel/laquelle/lesquels/lesquelles (object of a preposition, things)
ce qui / ce que / ce dont (what..., no specific antecedent)

Vocabulary

qui / que / où / dont
ce qui / ce que / ce dont
lequel / laquelle

Examples

J'ai un ami qui habite au Canada.

I have a friend who lives in Canada.

Le film que nous avons vu hier était nul.

The movie we saw yesterday was terrible.

C'est la maison où j'ai grandi.

This is the house where I grew up.

Voici le collègue dont je t'ai parlé.

Here's the colleague I told you about.

Je ne comprends pas ce qu'il veut.

I don't understand what he wants.

Notes

  • If you can restate the missing piece as "de + noun" in your head, use "dont" — this single test resolves most qui/que/dont confusion.

Practice

Combine each pair of sentences with a relative pronoun: "J'ai un livre. Le livre est intéressant." / "Je parle d'un problème. Le problème est sérieux."
