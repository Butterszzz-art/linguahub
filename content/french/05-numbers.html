Numbers

Numbers come up constantly — prices, ages, phone numbers, dates. French numbers have a few quirky patterns above 60 that are worth learning early.

Grammar

  • 0–20 must simply be memorized (see vocabulary below); they don't follow a clean pattern.
  • 21, 31, 41, 51, 61, 71 use "et" before "un": vingt et un (21), trente et un (31)... but NOT 81 or 91 (quatre-vingt-un, no "et").
  • 22–29, 32–39, etc. are formed with a hyphen: vingt-deux (22).
  • 70–79: France uses base-60 counting — soixante-dix (70, literally "sixty-ten"), soixante-onze (71), ... soixante-dix-neuf (79).
  • 80–89: quatre-vingts (80, literally "four-twenties"), quatre-vingt-un (81, no plural -s on "vingt" once followed by another number), ... quatre-vingt-dix-neuf (99, "four-twenty-nineteen"). (Note: Belgium and Switzerland use septante=70, huitante/octante=80, nonante=90 — simpler, but not standard in France.)
  • 100+ : cent (100), cent un (101, no "et"), deux cents (200, "cent" takes -s only when a round multiple with nothing after it — deux cents but deux cent un), mille (1,000, NEVER takes -s: deux mille), un million, un milliard.
  • Ordinal numbers ("first, second..."): add -ième to the cardinal number (minus a silent final -e): quatre -> quatrième. Irregular: premier/première (1st), and second/seconde is an alternative to deuxième (2nd).

Vocabulary

a price

the age
combien
how much/many
gratuit
free (of charge)

Examples

J'ai vingt-cinq ans.

I am 25 years old. (French uses "avoir X ans", lit. "to have X years", not "to be")

Ça coûte quatre-vingt-quinze euros.

That costs 95 euros.

Elle habite au premier étage.

She lives on the first floor (= 2nd floor US-style, since "rez-de-chaussée" is the ground floor).

C'est mon deuxième voyage en France.

This is my second trip to France.

Notes

  • Age, hunger, thirst, fear, and being right/wrong all use "avoir" (to have) in French where English uses "to be": j'ai faim (I'm hungry, lit. "I have hunger"), j'ai raison (I'm right).
  • Practice 70–99 out loud until the math becomes automatic — it's the #1 numbers stumbling block for English speakers.

Practice

Say aloud: 71, 82, 95, 99, 100, 201, 1 000 000.
