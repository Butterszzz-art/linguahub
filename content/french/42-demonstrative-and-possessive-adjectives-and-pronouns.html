Demonstrative and Possessive Adjectives and Pronouns

"This/that/these/those" (demonstrative) and "my/your/his/her/our/their" (possessive) — as both adjectives (before a noun) and standalone pronouns (replacing a noun).

Grammar

  • Demonstrative adjectives ("this/that"): ce (masc. sing.), cet (masc. sing. before a vowel/mute h), cette (fem. sing.), ces (plural, either gender). French doesn't distinguish "this" vs. "that" by default — add "-ci" (this, nearby) or "-là" (that, farther) after the noun for clarity/contrast: ce livre-ci (this book) vs. ce livre-là (that book).
  • Possessive adjectives ("my/your/his/her/our/their") agree with the NOUN they describe, not with the owner's gender:
    mon/ma/mes (my), ton/ta/tes (your, informal), son/sa/ses (his/her/ its — same form regardless of the OWNER's gender), notre/notre/nos (our), votre/votre/vos (your, formal/plural), leur/leur/leurs (their) Before a feminine noun starting with a vowel/mute h, use "mon/ton/son" instead of "ma/ta/sa" for pronunciation: mon amie (my friend, feminine).
  • Possessive pronouns ("mine/yours/his/hers/ours/theirs") replace a noun entirely and include a definite article that agrees with the noun replaced: le mien/la mienne/les miens/les miennes (mine), le tien/la tienne (yours), le sien/la sienne (his/hers), le/la nôtre, les nôtres (ours), le/la vôtre, les vôtres (yours), le/la leur, les leurs (theirs).
  • Demonstrative pronouns ("this one/that one/the one(s)"): celui (masc. sing.), celle (fem. sing.), ceux (masc. pl.), celles (fem. pl.) — almost always followed by -ci/-là, "de" + noun (showing possession), or a relative clause: celui-ci (this one), celle de Marie (Marie's, lit. "that of Marie"), ceux qui travaillent (those who work).
  • "Ceci/cela/ça" are neutral demonstrative pronouns referring to an idea or unspecified thing rather than a specific noun (ça = informal contraction of cela, extremely common in speech): Ça va ? (How's it going?) Cela m'étonne. (That surprises me.)

Examples

Cette robe-ci est plus jolie que cette robe-là.

This dress is prettier than that dress.

Son frère et sa sœur habitent à Paris.

His/her brother and sister live in Paris. (son/sa depend on "frère/sœur" gender, NOT the owner's)

Ce n'est pas ma voiture, c'est la sienne.

It's not my car, it's his/ hers.

Je préfère celui-ci à celui-là.

I prefer this one to that one.

Ça m'est égal.

It's all the same to me. / I don't mind.

Notes

  • The #1 mistake for English speakers: using "son/sa" based on the OWNER's gender the way English does with "his/her." French bases it entirely on the gender of the NOUN owned: "sa voiture" can mean "his car" OR "her car" — context tells you which.

Practice

Translate, paying attention to son/sa/ses: "His mother is a doctor. Her father is a teacher. Their house is big."
