Order of Multiple Object Pronouns & Disjunctive Pronouns

When a sentence has more than one object pronoun, they follow a strict order. Separately, "disjunctive" (stress) pronouns are used after prepositions, for emphasis, and in a few other special positions.

Grammar

  • When combining multiple object pronouns before the verb, they follow this fixed order:
    me/te/se/nous/vous -> le/la/les -> lui/leur -> y -> en Example: Il me le donne. (He gives it to me.) Elle nous en parle. (She talks to us about it.) Je le lui ai donné. (I gave it to him/her.)
  • In the AFFIRMATIVE imperative only, the order changes: verb + direct object + indirect object, all hyphenated, with "moi/toi" instead of me/te (except before "y/en," where moi/toi shorten back to m'/t'): Donne-le-moi ! (Give it to me!) Donnez-m'en ! (Give me some!)
  • Disjunctive (stress) pronouns: moi, toi, lui, elle, nous, vous, eux, elles. Used:
    1. After a preposition: avec moi (with me), pour toi (for you), chez eux (at their place)
    2. For emphasis, often at the start or end of a sentence: Moi, je n'aime pas ça. (ME, I don't like that.)
    3. After "c'est/ce sont": C'est moi. (It's me.) Ce sont eux. (It's them.)
    4. In comparisons: Elle est plus grande que moi. (She's taller than me.)
    5. Combined with "-même(s)" for "-self/-selves": moi-même (myself), eux-mêmes (themselves).

Vocabulary

avec / pour / chez / sans + [disjunctive pronoun]
c'est... / ce sont...
-même(s)
-self/-selves

Examples

Il nous les a envoyés hier.

He sent them to us yesterday. (nous before les)

Prête-le-moi, s'il te plaît.

Lend it to me, please.

Tu viens avec nous ce soir ?

Are you coming with us tonight?

C'est toi qui as raison, pas moi.

You're the one who's right, not me.

Je l'ai fait moi-même.

I did it myself.

Notes

  • Combining pronoun order feels mechanical at first — practice building short chains (le + lui, nous + en, etc.) out loud until the sequence becomes automatic.
