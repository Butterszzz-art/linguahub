The Infinitive Mood

The infinitive (the unconjugated "to do" form) is used far more often in French than in English — as a subject, after prepositions, and after many conjugated verbs, sometimes requiring a specific connecting preposition that must be memorized verb by verb.

Grammar

  • The infinitif présent is the dictionary form (parler, finir, vendre) and can function as a noun (Fumer est mauvais pour la santé. = Smoking is bad for your health. — no "-ing" gerund needed, unlike English).
  • The infinitif passé ("past infinitive," to have done) is formed with avoir or être (in the infinitive) + past participle, used after certain expressions (especially "après") to show a completed action before the main verb: Après avoir mangé, il est parti. (After eating [lit. "having eaten"], he left.) Après être arrivée, elle a téléphoné. (After arriving, she called.)
  • When one conjugated verb is followed by a second verb, the second verb is almost always in the infinitive — but the CONNECTING WORD varies by verb and must be memorized:
    No preposition needed (verb + infinitive directly): vouloir, pouvoir, devoir, aimer, préférer, savoir, espérer, aller... Je veux partir. (I want to leave.)
    Verb + À + infinitive: aider à, apprendre à, commencer à, continuer à, réussir à, s'habituer à... J'apprends à cuisiner. (I'm learning to cook.)
    Verb + DE + infinitive: décider de, essayer de, finir de, oublier de, arrêter de, avoir besoin de, avoir envie de, être content de... J'ai décidé de partir. (I decided to leave.)
  • After a preposition other than "de," French always uses the infinitive where English uses "-ing": avant de partir (before leaving), sans parler (without speaking), au lieu de dormir (instead of sleeping).

Examples

Avant de partir, vérifie que la porte est fermée.

Before leaving, check that the door is closed.

Après avoir fini le travail, nous sommes allés au cinéma.

After finishing the work, we went to the movies.

Elle a réussi à obtenir le poste.

She succeeded in getting the job.

J'ai oublié de fermer la fenêtre.

I forgot to close the window.

Notes

  • There's no shortcut for the à/de/no-preposition pattern — build a running list of verb + preposition combinations as you encounter them, and review it regularly.
