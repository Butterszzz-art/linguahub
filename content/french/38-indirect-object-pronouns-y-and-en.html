Indirect Object Pronouns, Y, and EN

Indirect object pronouns replace a noun introduced by "à" (usually a person), while "y" and "en" are two special invariable pronouns that replace "à/dans + place or thing" and "de + noun" respectively.

Grammar

  • Indirect object pronouns: me/m', te/t', lui (to him/her — same form for both genders), nous, vous, leur (to them — same for both genders). These replace "à + person": Je parle à Marie -> Je lui parle. (I speak to her.)
  • Placement rules mirror direct object pronouns (Lesson 37): before the conjugated verb, or before the infinitive they belong to. UNLIKE direct object pronouns, indirect object pronouns do NOT trigger past-participle agreement: Je lui ai parlé. (I spoke to him/her — "parlé" stays unchanged.)
  • "Y" replaces "à/dans/en/sur + a place or thing" (not a person): Tu vas à Paris ? Oui, j'y vais. (Are you going to Paris? Yes, I'm going there.) It also replaces "à + thing" after verbs like penser à, réfléchir à: Je pense à mon voyage -> J'y pense.
  • "En" replaces "de + a noun" (a quantity, a thing, or a place you're coming FROM): Tu as des frères ? Oui, j'en ai deux. (Do you have brothers? Yes, I have two [of them].) Tu viens de Paris ? Oui, j'en viens. (Are you coming from Paris? Yes, I'm coming from there.) "En" is also used with expressions of quantity (Combien de livres as-tu ? J'en ai dix.) and after "de"-verbs: avoir besoin de, avoir envie de, parler de.
  • Both y and en are placed in the same position as other object pronouns (before the conjugated verb / before the infinitive).

Examples

J'ai téléphoné à mes parents. -> Je leur ai téléphoné.

I called them (my parents). (indirect object, no agreement on "téléphoné")

Est-ce que tu vas au marché ? Oui, j'y vais maintenant.

Are you going to the market? Yes, I'm going there now.

Il a trois enfants ; elle en a deux.

He has three children; she has two [of them].

Tu as besoin d'aide ? Oui, j'en ai besoin.

Do you need help? Yes, I need some [of it].

Notes

  • "Manquer à" (to be missed by) reverses the English structure: "Tu me manques" literally means "you are lacking to me," but translates as "I miss you" — the person doing the missing is the INDIRECT object in French.
