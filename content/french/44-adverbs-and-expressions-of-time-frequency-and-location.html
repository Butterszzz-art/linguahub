Adverbs and Expressions of Time, Frequency, and Location

Beyond the -ment adverbs from Lesson 3, this lesson rounds out the essential toolkit for saying when, how often, and where something happens.

Grammar

  • Adverb formation recap: feminine form of the adjective + -ment (lente -> lentement). Adjectives ending in a vowel in the masculine use the masculine form directly + -ment (vrai -> vraiment). Adjectives ending in -ant/-ent change to -amment/-emment (constant -> constamment; évident -> évidemment).
  • Placement of adverbs: usually AFTER a conjugated verb in simple tenses (Il parle bien.), and typically BETWEEN the helper verb and past participle for short/common adverbs in compound tenses (Il a bien parlé.) — unlike English, which puts the adverb after the whole verb phrase.
  • Common time adverbs: maintenant (now), bientôt (soon), déjà (already), encore (still/again), toujours (always/still), enfin (finally), tout de suite (right away).
  • Frequency adverbs: souvent (often), parfois/quelquefois (sometimes), rarement (rarely), jamais (never, needs "ne"), toujours (always), une fois/deux fois (once/twice).
  • Location adverbs and prepositions: ici (here), là (there), là-bas (over there), devant/derrière (in front of/behind), à côté de (next to), entre (between), sous/sur (under/on), dans (in), loin de/près de (far from/near).

Examples

Elle a bien réussi son examen.

She did well on her exam. (adverb between helper and participle)

Nous allons rarement au cinéma.

We rarely go to the movies.

La pharmacie est juste à côté de la banque.

The pharmacy is right next to the bank.

Il habite encore chez ses parents.

He still lives with his parents.

Notes

  • "Encore" is doing double duty in French: it means both "still" (Il travaille encore = He's still working) and "again" (Recommence encore ! = Start again!) — context determines which.
