Devoir and Related Structures

Devoir (must/have to/should) is one of the most useful and most irregular verbs in French, plus two handy fixed expressions and a family of verbs with a distinctive ending pattern.

Grammar

  • Devoir + infinitive expresses obligation ("must/have to") or probability ("must be"), depending on context: Je dois partir. (I have to leave.) Il doit être fatigué. (He must be tired.) Full nuance of devoir across different tenses (should have, would have to) is covered in Part 4, Lesson 26.
  • "Il y a" (there is/are, from Lesson 12) is invariable and doesn't change for singular/plural.
  • "Il s'agit de" (it's a matter of / it's about) is an impersonal expression used to introduce a topic: Il s'agit d'un problème sérieux. (It's about a serious problem.) It's ALWAYS impersonal ("il"), never conjugated for a real subject.
  • Verbs ending in -eindre and -aindre (peindre = to paint, craindre = to fear) follow their own irregular pattern: drop -indre, add -s, -s, -t, -gnons, -gnez, -gnent (note the "gn" appears only in the plural, and the singular has no d): je peins, tu peins, il peint, nous peignons, vous peignez, ils peignent.

Reference Table

Examples

Tu dois étudier avant l'examen.

You must study before the exam.

Il y a un problème avec la voiture.

There's a problem with the car.

Ce livre s'agit de l'histoire de France. -- (INCORRECT — common mistake; correct form:) Il s'agit de l'histoire de France dans ce livre.

This book is about the history of France.

Elle peint un beau tableau.

She's painting a beautiful picture.

Il craint les araignées.

He's afraid of spiders.

Notes

  • Common mistake: "il s'agit de" can NEVER take a real subject like a book or movie title as "il" — the "il" is always the impersonal "it," and the real subject is expressed with "dans" (in) if needed, as shown above.
