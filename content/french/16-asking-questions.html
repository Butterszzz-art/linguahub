Asking Questions

Three ways to turn a statement into a yes/no question in French, from most casual to most formal, plus the core question words for open-ended questions.

Grammar

  • Method 1 — Intonation (most casual, common in speech): keep normal word order, just raise your pitch at the end: Tu parles français ? (You speak French?)
  • Method 2 — Est-ce que (neutral, works everywhere): add "est-ce que" to the front of a normal statement: Est-ce que tu parles français ?
  • Method 3 — Inversion (more formal/written): swap the subject pronoun and verb, joined by a hyphen: Parles-tu français ? With il/elle/on and a verb ending in a vowel, insert -t- for pronunciation: Parle-t-il français ?
  • Question words (interrogatives) go at the front of the sentence, usually followed by est-ce que or inversion:
    qui - who
    que/qu' - what (object)
    quoi - what (after a preposition, or standalone)
    où - where
    quand - when
    pourquoi - why
    comment - how
    combien (de) - how much/many
    quel(le)(s) - which/what (agrees with the noun)
  • "Quel" vs. "qui/que": use "quel" when directly modifying a noun (Quelle heure est-il ?), use qui/que when standing alone as a pronoun (Qu'est-ce que tu fais ?).

Examples

Où habites-tu ?

Where do you live?

Qu'est-ce que vous faites ce soir ?

What are you doing tonight?

Combien coûte ce livre ?

How much does this book cost?

Quelle heure est-il ?

What time is it?

Pourquoi est-ce que tu pleures ?

Why are you crying?

Notes

  • "Pourquoi" is answered with "parce que" (because): Pourquoi tu pars ? Parce que je suis fatigué. (Why are you leaving? Because I'm tired.)
