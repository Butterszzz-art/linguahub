LESSON 46: The French You Already Know — Cognates and False Friends
================================================================

OVERVIEW:
English borrowed enormously from French after the Norman Conquest, so you
already recognize thousands of French words. This lesson teaches you to
spot true cognates quickly and avoid the "false friends" that trip up
beginners.

GRAMMAR:
- True cognates (bons alliés — "good allies"): words that look alike and
  mean the same thing: nation, important, hôpital, restaurant, hôtel,
  possible, nécessaire. Many English words ending in -tion, -ent, -ant,
  -ble, -ique map directly to French -tion, -ent, -ant, -ble, -ique.
- Partial cognates ("kissing cousins"): recognizably related but not
  identical in form or exact meaning: éducation (education), université
  (university), différent (different).
- False friends (faux amis): look similar but mean something different —
  a major source of embarrassing mistakes:
    assister à = to attend (NOT "to assist" — that's aider)
    attendre = to wait for (NOT "to attend" — that's assister à)
    la librairie = the bookstore (NOT "library" — that's la bibliothèque)
    la lecture = reading (NOT "lecture" — that's la conférence)
    sensible = sensitive (NOT "sensible" — that's raisonnable/sensé)
    demander = to ask (NOT "to demand" — that's exiger)
    rester = to stay (NOT "to rest" — that's se reposer)
    actuellement = currently/now (NOT "actually" — that's en fait)
    blesser = to injure/hurt (NOT "to bless" — that's bénir)
    une femme = a woman (not related to English "femme" as in "femme
      fatale" in meaning-shift terms, but note it also means "wife")
- Borrowed English words in French (anglicismes): le week-end, le
  shopping, le sport, un e-mail, cool, le parking — common in casual
  speech, though official/formal French often prefers a French
  alternative (le courriel for e-mail, in Québec especially).

CONJUGATION TABLE:
(vocabulary lesson — not applicable)

VOCABULARY:
assister à - to attend            attendre - to wait for
la librairie - the bookstore       la bibliothèque - the library
sensible - sensitive                raisonnable - sensible/reasonable
rester - to stay                    se reposer - to rest
actuellement - currently             en fait - actually

EXAMPLES:
Je vais assister à la réunion demain. - I'm going to attend the meeting
  tomorrow.
J'ai acheté ce livre à la librairie. - I bought this book at the
  bookstore.
Elle est très sensible aux critiques. - She's very sensitive to criticism.
Actuellement, je travaille à Paris. - Currently, I work in Paris. (NOT
  "actually" — a classic false-friend trap)

NOTES:
- When you meet a new French word that looks EXACTLY like an English
  word, pause and double-check its meaning before assuming — false
  friends are one of the most common sources of miscommunication for
  intermediate learners specifically (beginners are more cautious;
  intermediate learners often over-trust resemblance).

PRACTICE:
Translate correctly, avoiding the false-friend trap: "I'm going to attend
the concert." "She works at the library." "He's a sensible person"
(reasonable, not sensitive).

LESSON 47: Home, Family, and Daily Routine Vocabulary
================================================================

OVERVIEW:
Core vocabulary for describing where you live, who's in your family, and
your daily schedule — pairs naturally with the reflexive verbs from
Lesson 14 and possessive adjectives from Lesson 42.

GRAMMAR:
- "Habiter" (to live/reside) takes no preposition before a city, but uses
  à/dans/en as covered in Lesson 36 for locations: J'habite à Paris dans
  un petit appartement.
- Rooms and house vocabulary combine naturally with "dans le/la" (in the):
  dans la cuisine, dans le salon.
- Possessive adjectives (Lesson 42) are essential for family vocabulary,
  since you're almost always talking about MY mother, HIS brother, etc.

CONJUGATION TABLE:
(vocabulary-focused lesson)

VOCABULARY:
la maison / l'appartement - the house / the apartment
le salon - the living room       la cuisine - the kitchen
la chambre - the bedroom          la salle de bains - the bathroom
le père / la mère - father / mother
le frère / la sœur - brother / sister
le mari / la femme - husband / wife
les grands-parents - grandparents
le fils / la fille - son / daughter
le petit-fils / la petite-fille - grandson / granddaughter

EXAMPLES:
Dans mon appartement, il y a deux chambres et une petite cuisine. - In my
  apartment, there are two bedrooms and a small kitchen.
Mon père travaille et ma mère reste à la maison. - My father works and my
  mother stays home.
Nous dînons ensemble en famille tous les soirs. - We have dinner together
  as a family every evening.
J'ai deux frères et une sœur. - I have two brothers and a sister.

NOTES:
- Combine this vocabulary with Lesson 14's reflexive verbs to describe a
  full daily routine: Je me réveille, je me lave dans la salle de bains,
  je prends le petit-déjeuner dans la cuisine...

PRACTICE:
Describe your home (rooms) and your immediate family in five sentences.

LESSON 48: Making Small Talk — Preferences, Work, and Weather
================================================================

OVERVIEW:
The everyday chit-chat toolkit: asking about likes/dislikes, talking about
your job, and — a French conversational staple — discussing the weather.

GRAMMAR:
- "Aimer" (to like/love), "adorer" (to adore), "détester" (to hate),
  "préférer" (to prefer) are all followed directly by a noun (with a
  definite article for general preferences) or an infinitive: J'aime le
  chocolat. J'aime lire.
- Talking about your job: "Je suis + [profession, NO article]": Je suis
  professeur. (I am a teacher.) — unlike English, no "a/an" before an
  unmodified profession. If the profession is modified by an adjective,
  the article returns: Je suis un bon professeur. (I am a good teacher.)
- Weather expressions mostly use "il fait" + adjective, or specific verbs:
  Il fait beau/mauvais/chaud/froid/frais. (It's nice/bad/hot/cold/cool.)
  Il pleut. (It's raining.) Il neige. (It's snowing.) Il y a du vent/du
  soleil/des nuages. (It's windy/sunny/cloudy.)

CONJUGATION TABLE:
(vocabulary/expression-focused lesson)

VOCABULARY:
Quels sont vos loisirs ? - What are your hobbies?
Qu'est-ce que vous faites dans la vie ? - What do you do for a living?
je suis + [profession] - I am a...
il fait beau/mauvais/chaud/froid - it's nice/bad/hot/cold (weather)
il pleut / il neige - it's raining / it's snowing
rester en contact - to keep in touch

EXAMPLES:
Qu'est-ce que vous faites comme travail ? Je suis ingénieur. - What do you
  do for work? I'm an engineer.
J'adore la randonnée, mais je déteste le sport en salle. - I love hiking,
  but I hate working out at the gym.
Il fait un temps magnifique aujourd'hui, n'est-ce pas ? - The weather is
  gorgeous today, isn't it?
On reste en contact ! - Let's keep in touch!

NOTES:
- Talking about the weather is a genuinely common French social ritual
  (much like in English) — a safe, friendly way to start or fill a
  conversation with someone you don't know well.

PRACTICE:
Write a short small-talk exchange: greeting, asking about work, sharing a
hobby preference, commenting on the weather.

LESSON 49: Asking Directions and Finding Your Way
================================================================

OVERVIEW:
Essential survival vocabulary for navigating a French-speaking city —
asking where something is and understanding the directions you're given.

GRAMMAR:
- "Où" + est/se trouve + [place] is the standard way to ask where
  something is: Où est la gare ? / Où se trouve la pharmacie ? (Both
  mean "Where is the [place]?" — "se trouver" literally means "to find
  itself/be located.")
- Direction/command vocabulary usually appears in the imperative (Lesson
  35): Tournez à droite. (Turn right.) Continuez tout droit. (Keep going
  straight.) Traversez la rue. (Cross the street.)
- "Prendre" is used for taking a route/street/transport: Prenez la
  première rue à gauche. (Take the first street on the left.)

CONJUGATION TABLE:
(vocabulary-focused lesson)

VOCABULARY:
à droite / à gauche - to the right / to the left
tout droit - straight ahead
au coin de - at the corner of
en face de - across from / facing
près de / loin de - near / far from
le feu (rouge) - the traffic light
le carrefour - the intersection
c'est à côté de... - it's next to...

EXAMPLES:
Excusez-moi, où est la station de métro la plus proche ? - Excuse me,
  where is the nearest metro station?
Continuez tout droit, puis tournez à gauche au deuxième feu. - Keep going
  straight, then turn left at the second light.
La banque est en face de la boulangerie. - The bank is across from the
  bakery.
C'est à cinq minutes à pied d'ici. - It's a five-minute walk from here.

NOTES:
- "Se trouver" (to be located) is very commonly used in place of "être"
  when talking about the location of a fixed place — Où se trouve la
  poste ? sounds just as natural as Où est la poste ?

PRACTICE:
Write directions from your home to the nearest grocery store, using at
least four direction words/phrases from the vocabulary list.

LESSON 50: Dining Out and Grocery Shopping
================================================================

OVERVIEW:
Ordering at a restaurant and shopping for food at a market — two of the
most immediately useful practical skills for any French learner.

GRAMMAR:
- "Je voudrais" (I would like — conditional of vouloir, Lesson 26) is the
  standard polite way to order: Je voudrais un café, s'il vous plaît.
- Partitive articles (Lesson 4) are essential for ordering/buying food in
  quantity: du pain, de la viande, des légumes.
- "Prendre" is commonly used at restaurants for "to have/order": Je vais
  prendre le poisson. (I'll have the fish.)
- Asking for the check: "L'addition, s'il vous plaît."

CONJUGATION TABLE:
(vocabulary-focused lesson)

VOCABULARY:
le petit-déjeuner / le déjeuner / le dîner - breakfast / lunch / dinner
la carte / le menu - the menu / the fixed-price menu
l'entrée (f.) / le plat principal / le dessert - starter / main course /
  dessert
l'addition (f.) - the check/bill
le marché - the market
les fruits et légumes - fruits and vegetables
la boulangerie / la boucherie / l'épicerie - bakery / butcher shop /
  grocery store

EXAMPLES:
Je voudrais réserver une table pour deux personnes. - I would like to
  reserve a table for two.
Qu'est-ce que vous me recommandez comme plat principal ? - What do you
  recommend as a main course?
Je vais prendre une salade et de l'eau minérale. - I'll have a salad and
  some mineral water.
Un kilo de pommes et une baguette, s'il vous plaît. - A kilo of apples and
  a baguette, please.

NOTES:
- In France, "le menu" refers to a fixed-price set meal, NOT the full list
  of options — the full list is "la carte." Asking for "le menu" gets you
  the day's set offer, not the whole menu in the English sense.

PRACTICE:
Write a short restaurant dialogue: greeting, asking for a recommendation,
ordering a starter and a main course, and asking for the check.

LESSON 51: Shopping
================================================================

OVERVIEW:
Vocabulary and phrases for clothes shopping, sizes, prices, and payment.

GRAMMAR:
- "Est-ce que je peux essayer...?" (Can I try on...?) uses pouvoir
  (Lesson 28) + infinitive.
- Sizes and colors (Lesson 40) combine with clothing nouns: une chemise
  bleue en taille moyenne (a blue shirt in medium size).
- "Ça coûte combien ?" / "Combien ça coûte ?" both ask the price
  informally; "Quel est le prix ?" is more formal.

CONJUGATION TABLE:
(vocabulary-focused lesson)

VOCABULARY:
un vêtement - a piece of clothing
la taille - the size (clothing)     la pointure - the size (shoes)
essayer - to try on                  la cabine d'essayage - the fitting room
cher / bon marché - expensive / cheap
en solde - on sale
la caisse - the checkout/register
payer en espèces / par carte - to pay in cash / by card

EXAMPLES:
Je cherche une robe pour une occasion spéciale. - I'm looking for a dress
  for a special occasion.
Est-ce que je peux essayer cette veste en taille 40 ? - Can I try on this
  jacket in size 40?
C'est un peu trop cher pour moi. Vous avez quelque chose de moins cher ?
  - That's a bit too expensive for me. Do you have something less
  expensive?
Je vais payer par carte, merci. - I'll pay by card, thanks.

NOTES:
- European clothing and shoe sizes differ from US sizing — if shopping in
  France, check a conversion chart rather than assuming your US number
  translates directly.

PRACTICE:
Write a shopping dialogue: asking to try something on, asking the price,
and deciding whether to buy it.

LESSON 52: Going Out on the Town
================================================================

OVERVIEW:
Vocabulary for making plans, extending and accepting/declining invitations,
and enjoying evening entertainment.

GRAMMAR:
- Extending an invitation: "Ça te dit de...?" (informal, "Does it appeal
  to you to...?") or "Voudrais-tu / Voudriez-vous...?" (Would you like
  to...?) + infinitive.
- Accepting: "Avec plaisir !" (With pleasure!) / "Bonne idée !" (Good
  idea!) Declining politely: "Je suis désolé(e), mais je ne peux pas..."
  (I'm sorry, but I can't...) + infinitive.
- "On se retrouve..." (Let's meet up..., lit. "we find each other again")
  is a natural way to arrange a meeting point/time.

CONJUGATION TABLE:
(vocabulary-focused lesson)

VOCABULARY:
sortir - to go out            une soirée - an evening/party
le cinéma / le théâtre / le concert - the movies / theater / concert
une boîte de nuit - a nightclub
réserver des places - to book seats/tickets
Ça te dit de... ? - Do you feel like...?
on se retrouve à... - let's meet at...

EXAMPLES:
Ça te dit d'aller au cinéma ce soir ? - Do you feel like going to the
  movies tonight?
Avec plaisir, à quelle heure ? - With pleasure, what time?
On se retrouve devant le théâtre à vingt heures. - Let's meet in front of
  the theater at 8 PM.
Je suis désolé, je ne peux pas venir, j'ai déjà des projets. - I'm sorry,
  I can't come, I already have plans.

NOTES:
- Combine this lesson with Lesson 24's futur simple and Lesson 13's
  futur proche to talk about upcoming plans naturally.

PRACTICE:
Write an invitation exchange: propose an outing, have the other person
accept, and agree on a time/place to meet.

LESSON 53: Business and Telecommunications
================================================================

OVERVIEW:
Phone, email, and office vocabulary for professional and practical
communication.

GRAMMAR:
- On the phone, French uses specific set phrases rather than direct
  translations: "Allô ?" (Hello? — phone-specific, not used face-to-face)
  "C'est de la part de qui ?" (Who's calling? lit. "It's on behalf of
  whom?") "Ne quittez pas." (Please hold, lit. "Don't leave.")
- "Je voudrais laisser un message" (I'd like to leave a message) uses the
  conditional for politeness (Lesson 26), as in many service contexts.

CONJUGATION TABLE:
(vocabulary-focused lesson)

VOCABULARY:
le bureau - the office            une réunion - a meeting
un rendez-vous - an appointment    envoyer un e-mail - to send an email
composer le numéro - to dial the number
laisser un message - to leave a message
rappeler - to call back
le portable - the cell phone

EXAMPLES:
Allô, bonjour, je voudrais parler à Madame Dubois, s'il vous plaît. -
  Hello, I'd like to speak with Ms. Dubois, please.
C'est de la part de qui ? - Who's calling?
Elle est en réunion, voulez-vous laisser un message ? - She's in a
  meeting, would you like to leave a message?
Je vous envoie les documents par e-mail cet après-midi. - I'll send you
  the documents by email this afternoon.

NOTES:
- "Allô" is used ONLY to answer/begin a phone call — never as a general
  greeting in person, which would sound very odd.

PRACTICE:
Write a short phone dialogue: answering, asking to speak with someone,
being told they're unavailable, and leaving a message.

LESSON 54: Recreation and the Outdoors
================================================================

OVERVIEW:
Vocabulary for sports, hobbies, and nature/outdoor activities.

GRAMMAR:
- "Faire de + partitive article" is the standard pattern for sports and
  activities: faire du sport, faire de la natation, faire de l'escalade
  (see Lesson 13's faire idioms).
- "Jouer à + definite article" is used for games/sports played with rules
  (especially ball sports): jouer au football, jouer aux cartes. (Note:
  jouer à vs. faire de — sports with a ball/game structure often use
  jouer à, while more general physical activities use faire de; usage
  varies and both are worth learning per-activity.)
- "Jouer de + definite article" is used for musical instruments: jouer du
  piano, jouer de la guitare.

CONJUGATION TABLE:
(vocabulary-focused lesson)

VOCABULARY:
faire du sport - to play sports/exercise
faire de la randonnée - to go hiking
faire du vélo - to go cycling
jouer au football/tennis - to play soccer/tennis
jouer de la guitare/du piano - to play guitar/piano
la montagne / la plage / la forêt - the mountains / the beach / the forest
nager / se baigner - to swim / to bathe (swim casually)

EXAMPLES:
Le week-end, je fais souvent de la randonnée en montagne. - On weekends, I
  often go hiking in the mountains.
Mon fils joue au football tous les samedis. - My son plays soccer every
  Saturday.
Elle joue du piano depuis dix ans. - She's been playing piano for ten
  years.
Nous aimons nous baigner dans le lac en été. - We like to swim in the
  lake in summer.

NOTES:
- The jouer à / jouer de / faire de distinction is a genuinely useful
  vocabulary pattern to internalize early — sports/games take à, music
  takes de, general physical activity takes de.

PRACTICE:
List five activities you enjoy, using the correct pattern (faire de,
jouer à, or jouer de) for each.

LESSON 55: Planning a Trip and Money Matters
================================================================

OVERVIEW:
Vocabulary for booking travel, budgeting, and handling money — banks,
currency exchange, and everyday financial phrases.

GRAMMAR:
- "Il faut que + subjunctive" (Lesson 29) is common for trip-planning
  necessities: Il faut que je réserve mon billet. (I need to book my
  ticket.)
- Currency amounts follow the noun: cinquante euros (fifty euros) —
  Lesson 5's numbers apply directly here.

CONJUGATION TABLE:
(vocabulary-focused lesson)

VOCABULARY:
réserver un billet - to book a ticket
le passeport / le visa - the passport / the visa
faire les valises - to pack (suitcases)
la banque / le distributeur - the bank / the ATM
changer de l'argent - to exchange money
le taux de change - the exchange rate
retirer de l'argent - to withdraw money

EXAMPLES:
J'ai réservé mon billet d'avion pour le quinze juin. - I booked my plane

NOTES:
- Many ATMs and exchange counters in France use the term "distributeur"
  or "DAB" (distributeur automatique de billets) rather than a direct
  translation of "ATM."

LESSON 57: Handling Emergencies
================================================================

OVERVIEW:
Critical survival vocabulary for medical emergencies, lost items, and
contacting emergency services — the kind of phrases you hope not to need
but should know cold.

GRAMMAR:
- "Au secours !" (Help!) is the general emergency call for immediate
  danger; "J'ai besoin d'aide" (I need help) is used for less urgent
  requests.
- Describing symptoms uses "avoir mal à + definite article + body part":
  J'ai mal à la tête. (I have a headache.) J'ai mal au ventre. (My
  stomach hurts.)
- Emergency numbers in France: 15 (SAMU, medical), 17 (police), 18
  (fire/pompiers), 112 (general EU emergency number, works from any
  phone).

CONJUGATION TABLE:
(vocabulary-focused lesson)

VOCABULARY:
Au secours ! - Help!
J'ai eu un accident. - I've had an accident.
J'ai perdu mon passeport / mon portefeuille. - I lost my passport/wallet.
avoir mal à... - to have pain in...
appeler une ambulance - to call an ambulance
le commissariat de police - the police station
l'hôpital (m.) / la pharmacie - the hospital / the pharmacy

EXAMPLES:
Au secours, appelez une ambulance ! - Help, call an ambulance!
J'ai mal au ventre depuis ce matin. - My stomach has hurt since this
  morning.
On m'a volé mon sac ; je dois aller au commissariat. - My bag was stolen;
  I need to go to the police station.
Où est la pharmacie la plus proche ? - Where is the nearest pharmacy?

NOTES:
- If you're in genuine danger in France, dial 112 — it connects to
  emergency services from any phone, including without a French SIM card,
  and operators can often assist in English.

PRACTICE:
Memorize how to say: "Help, I need a doctor," "I lost my passport," and
"Where is the nearest hospital?"

LESSON 58: Idioms, Expressions, and Sounding Like a Native
================================================================

OVERVIEW:
A closing set of favorite French expressions, common phrases that will
make you sound more natural, and a few things to avoid saying — rounding
off your practical toolkit.

GRAMMAR:
- Idiomatic expressions often don't translate literally — learn them as
  fixed chunks rather than word-by-word: coûter les yeux de la tête
  (to cost an arm and a leg, lit. "to cost the eyes from the head");
  poser un lapin à quelqu'un (to stand someone up, lit. "to put a rabbit
  on someone"); avoir le cafard (to feel down/blue, lit. "to have the
  cockroach").
- A few phrases to be careful with: "Je suis plein(e)" does NOT mean
  "I'm full" (after a meal) in standard French the way it might sound —
  it can sound like claiming to be pregnant/drunk depending on context;
  the safer phrase is "J'ai bien mangé" or "Je n'ai plus faim" (I'm not
  hungry anymore).
- Filler words/connectors that make speech sound more natural: "du coup"
  (so/as a result — extremely common in casual modern French), "en fait"
  (actually), "bref" (in short/anyway), "quoi" (informal sentence-ending
  filler, roughly "you know"), "voilà" (there you go / that's it).

CONJUGATION TABLE:
(expressions lesson)

VOCABULARY:
du coup - so/as a result
en fait - actually
bref - in short/anyway
ça marche - that works / sounds good (informal agreement)
c'est pas grave - it's no big deal
je n'en reviens pas - I can't get over it/I can't believe it
poser un lapin à quelqu'un - to stand someone up

EXAMPLES:
Il pleuvait, du coup on est restés à la maison. - It was raining, so we
  stayed home.
Ça marche, on se voit à midi ! - Sounds good, see you at noon!
Ce sac coûte les yeux de la tête ! - This bag costs an arm and a leg!
Bref, la soirée s'est bien passée. - Anyway, the evening went well.

NOTES:
- Sprinkling in a few of these connectors ("du coup," "en fait," "bref")
  after you've solidified core grammar is one of the fastest ways to make
  your spoken French sound noticeably more natural and less textbook-ish.

PRACTICE:
Write a short paragraph (5–6 sentences) about your day using at least
three connector words from this lesson (du coup, en fait, bref, voilà).
