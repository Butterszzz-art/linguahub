LESSON 36: Articles and Nouns In Depth
================================================================

OVERVIEW:
Building on Lesson 4, this lesson covers noun gender/plural patterns more
thoroughly and the special rules for geographical names — a notoriously
tricky corner of French grammar.

GRAMMAR:
- Gender patterns (useful but not 100% reliable):
    Masculine endings: -age, -al, -ail, -eau, -isme, -ment, -oir
    Feminine endings: -ade, -ance/-ence, -ée, -elle, -ette, -euse, -ie,
      -ion/-sion/-tion, -té, -ure
    Exceptions exist for almost every rule (le musée, la page) — always
    learn gender with the noun.
- Some nouns change MEANING with gender: le livre (the book) vs. la livre
  (the pound — weight or currency); le poste (job position) vs. la poste
  (the post office); le tour (a turn/trip) vs. la tour (a tower).
- Plural formation beyond the basic -s (Lesson 4):
    -eau/-eu -> -eaux/-eux (le bateau -> les bateaux; le jeu -> les jeux)
    -al -> -aux (le cheval -> les chevaux) — with exceptions (le bal ->
      les bals)
    -s/-x/-z endings don't change (le prix -> les prix)
    A handful are fully irregular: l'œil -> les yeux (eye -> eyes),
      madame -> mesdames
- Gender of countries: countries ending in -e are usually feminine (la
  France, la Chine — with exceptions like le Mexique); most others are
  masculine (le Japon, le Canada, le Portugal); a few are plural (les
  États-Unis, les Pays-Bas).
- Prepositions with geographical names:
    to/in + feminine country or country starting with a vowel -> EN:
      en France, en Iran
    to/in + masculine country -> AU: au Japon, au Canada
    to/in + plural country -> AUX: aux États-Unis
    to/in + city -> À (no article): à Paris, à Tokyo
    from + feminine country -> DE/D': de France
    from + masculine country -> DU: du Japon
    from + plural country -> DES: des États-Unis

CONJUGATION TABLE:
(reference tables above — not a verb conjugation)

VOCABULARY:
la France - France (f.)      le Japon - Japan (m.)
les États-Unis - the United States (pl.)
le Canada - Canada (m.)       la Chine - China (f.)
l'Italie (f.) - Italy          le Mexique - Mexico (m.)

EXAMPLES:
Elle habite en France mais elle vient du Canada. - She lives in France but
  she's from Canada.
Nous allons au Japon cet été, puis aux Philippines. - We're going to Japan
  this summer, then to the Philippines.
J'ai perdu mes clés — je ne sais pas où elles sont. - I lost my keys — I
  don't know where they are. (les clés, always feminine plural)
Il pèse une livre de plus qu'avant. - He weighs one pound more than
  before. (la livre = pound, weight — contrast with le livre = book)

NOTES:
- When a new noun's gender isn't obvious from the ending, just memorize
  "le/la + noun" as a single unit from the start — retrofitting gender
  later is much harder.

PRACTICE:
Fill in the correct preposition: Je vais ___ Portugal. Elle vient ___
Brésil. Nous voyageons ___ Argentine.

LESSON 37: Direct Object Pronouns
================================================================

OVERVIEW:
Direct object pronouns replace a noun that directly receives the action of
the verb (no preposition involved) — "me, you, him, her, it, us, them" in
English. In French, they move BEFORE the conjugated verb, a major
structural difference from English.

GRAMMAR:
- Direct object pronouns: me/m' (me), te/t' (you, informal), le/l' (him/
  it, masc.), la/l' (her/it, fem.), nous (us), vous (you), les (them).
- Placement: directly before the conjugated verb (not after, as in
  English): Je le vois. (I see it/him.) In the negative: Je ne le vois
  pas. (I don't see it/him.)
- With an infinitive construction, the pronoun goes before the infinitive
  it belongs to, not the conjugated verb: Je vais le voir. (I'm going to
  see it/him.)
- In the passé composé, the pronoun goes before the helper verb, and the
  past participle AGREES in gender/number with a preceding direct object
  pronoun: Je l'ai vue. (I saw her — "vue" agrees with "la/l'" = her,
  feminine.) J'ai vu Marie -> Je l'ai vue.
- In the affirmative imperative, the pronoun follows the verb (hyphenated,
  and "me/te" become "moi/toi"): Regarde-le ! (Look at it/him!) In the
  negative imperative, normal order returns: Ne le regarde pas !

CONJUGATION TABLE:
(pronoun table)
me/m' - te/t' - le/l', la/l' - nous - vous - les

VOCABULARY:
voir - to see          regarder - to watch/look at
connaître - to know (a person/place)
prendre - to take       aimer - to like/love

EXAMPLES:
Tu connais Marc ? Oui, je le connais bien. - Do you know Marc? Yes, I know
  him well.
J'ai acheté ces chaussures parce que je les aimais. - I bought these shoes
  because I liked them.
Elle a écrit la lettre et l'a envoyée hier. - She wrote the letter and
  sent it yesterday. (agreement: envoyée, feminine, agrees with "la
  lettre")
Aide-moi, s'il te plaît ! - Help me, please!

NOTES:
- The past-participle agreement rule (only with a PRECEDING direct
  object) trips up even advanced learners — it applies with direct object
  pronouns and with relative pronoun "que," but never with indirect
  object pronouns (see Lesson 38).

PRACTICE:
Replace the underlined noun with a direct object pronoun and rewrite the
sentence: "Je regarde la télévision." "Nous avons invité nos amis."

================================================================
LESSON 38: Indirect Object Pronouns, Y, and EN
================================================================

OVERVIEW:
Indirect object pronouns replace a noun introduced by "à" (usually a
person), while "y" and "en" are two special invariable pronouns that
replace "à/dans + place or thing" and "de + noun" respectively.

GRAMMAR:
- Indirect object pronouns: me/m', te/t', lui (to him/her — same form for
  both genders), nous, vous, leur (to them — same for both genders).
  These replace "à + person": Je parle à Marie -> Je lui parle. (I speak
  to her.)
- Placement rules mirror direct object pronouns (Lesson 37): before the
  conjugated verb, or before the infinitive they belong to. UNLIKE direct
  object pronouns, indirect object pronouns do NOT trigger past-participle
  agreement: Je lui ai parlé. (I spoke to him/her — "parlé" stays
  unchanged.)
- "Y" replaces "à/dans/en/sur + a place or thing" (not a person): Tu vas à
  Paris ? Oui, j'y vais. (Are you going to Paris? Yes, I'm going there.)
  It also replaces "à + thing" after verbs like penser à, réfléchir à:
  Je pense à mon voyage -> J'y pense.
- "En" replaces "de + a noun" (a quantity, a thing, or a place you're
  coming FROM): Tu as des frères ? Oui, j'en ai deux. (Do you have
  brothers? Yes, I have two [of them].) Tu viens de Paris ? Oui, j'en
  viens. (Are you coming from Paris? Yes, I'm coming from there.) "En" is
  also used with expressions of quantity (Combien de livres as-tu ? J'en
  ai dix.) and after "de"-verbs: avoir besoin de, avoir envie de, parler
  de.
- Both y and en are placed in the same position as other object pronouns
  (before the conjugated verb / before the infinitive).

CONJUGATION TABLE:
(pronoun table)
me/m' - te/t' - lui - nous - vous - leur      (indirect object)
y (there / about it/them, replacing à + thing)
en (some/of it/of them, replacing de + noun)

VOCABULARY:
parler à - to speak to        téléphoner à - to call (phone)
donner à - to give to          répondre à - to answer/respond to
penser à - to think about       avoir besoin de - to need
avoir envie de - to feel like/want

EXAMPLES:
J'ai téléphoné à mes parents. -> Je leur ai téléphoné. - I called them
  (my parents). (indirect object, no agreement on "téléphoné")
Est-ce que tu vas au marché ? Oui, j'y vais maintenant. - Are you going to
  the market? Yes, I'm going there now.
Il a trois enfants ; elle en a deux. - He has three children; she has two
  [of them].
Tu as besoin d'aide ? Oui, j'en ai besoin. - Do you need help? Yes, I need
  some [of it].

NOTES:
- "Manquer à" (to be missed by) reverses the English structure: "Tu me
  manques" literally means "you are lacking to me," but translates as
  "I miss you" — the person doing the missing is the INDIRECT object in
  French.

PRACTICE:
Replace the underlined phrase with lui/leur, y, or en as appropriate: "Je
pense à mes vacances." "Nous avons parlé à nos voisins." "Elle boit du
café tous les matins."

LESSON 39: Order of Multiple Object Pronouns & Disjunctive Pronouns
================================================================

OVERVIEW:
When a sentence has more than one object pronoun, they follow a strict
order. Separately, "disjunctive" (stress) pronouns are used after
prepositions, for emphasis, and in a few other special positions.

GRAMMAR:
- When combining multiple object pronouns before the verb, they follow
  this fixed order:
    me/te/se/nous/vous  ->  le/la/les  ->  lui/leur  ->  y  ->  en
  Example: Il me le donne. (He gives it to me.) Elle nous en parle. (She
  talks to us about it.) Je le lui ai donné. (I gave it to him/her.)
- In the AFFIRMATIVE imperative only, the order changes: verb + direct
  object + indirect object, all hyphenated, with "moi/toi" instead of
  me/te (except before "y/en," where moi/toi shorten back to m'/t'):
  Donne-le-moi ! (Give it to me!) Donnez-m'en ! (Give me some!)
- Disjunctive (stress) pronouns: moi, toi, lui, elle, nous, vous, eux,
  elles. Used:
    1. After a preposition: avec moi (with me), pour toi (for you), chez
       eux (at their place)
    2. For emphasis, often at the start or end of a sentence: Moi, je
       n'aime pas ça. (ME, I don't like that.)
    3. After "c'est/ce sont": C'est moi. (It's me.) Ce sont eux. (It's
       them.)
    4. In comparisons: Elle est plus grande que moi. (She's taller than
       me.)
    5. Combined with "-même(s)" for "-self/-selves": moi-même (myself),
       eux-mêmes (themselves).

CONJUGATION TABLE:
Pronoun order before the verb:
me/te/se/nous/vous -> le/la/les -> lui/leur -> y -> en

Disjunctive pronouns: moi, toi, lui, elle, nous, vous, eux, elles

VOCABULARY:
avec / pour / chez / sans + [disjunctive pronoun]
c'est... / ce sont...
-même(s) - -self/-selves

EXAMPLES:
Il nous les a envoyés hier. - He sent them to us yesterday. (nous before
  les)
Prête-le-moi, s'il te plaît. - Lend it to me, please.
Tu viens avec nous ce soir ? - Are you coming with us tonight?
C'est toi qui as raison, pas moi. - You're the one who's right, not me.
Je l'ai fait moi-même. - I did it myself.

NOTES:
- Combining pronoun order feels mechanical at first — practice building
  short chains (le + lui, nous + en, etc.) out loud until the sequence
  becomes automatic.

PRACTICE:
Combine into one sentence with two pronouns: "Je donne le cadeau à
Marie." -> "Je ___ ___ donne."

LESSON 40: Adjectives — Agreement, Placement, and Colors
================================================================

OVERVIEW:
French adjectives must match the noun they describe in gender and number,
and most (but not all) go after the noun — this lesson covers the rules
and the main exceptions.

GRAMMAR:
- Basic agreement: add -e for feminine (if the masculine doesn't already
  end in -e), add -s for plural (silent in speech): grand -> grande ->
  grands -> grandes.
- Irregular feminine patterns: -eux -> -euse (heureux -> heureuse); -f ->
  -ve (actif -> active); -on/-en -> double consonant + e (bon -> bonne,
  ancien -> ancienne); -c -> -che or -que (blanc -> blanche); a handful
  are fully irregular (beau -> belle, nouveau -> nouvelle, vieux ->
  vieille, long -> longue).
- Adjectives with a special form before a masculine noun starting with a
  vowel/mute h: beau -> bel (un bel homme), nouveau -> nouvel (un nouvel
  ami), vieux -> vieil (un vieil arbre) — this extra form exists purely
  for pronunciation.
- Placement: most adjectives follow the noun (une voiture rouge). The
  BAGS adjectives (Beauty, Age, Goodness, Size — beau, joli, jeune, vieux,
  nouveau, bon, mauvais, grand, petit, gros, and a few more like haut,
  long, meilleur) come BEFORE the noun.
- A few adjectives change MEANING depending on placement: un homme grand
  (a tall man) vs. un grand homme (a great man); ma propre chambre (my
  own room) vs. une chambre propre (a clean room); un ancien professeur
  (a former teacher) vs. un livre ancien (an old/ancient book).
- Colors: agree like regular adjectives (une robe bleue) EXCEPT compound
  colors and colors derived from nouns (fruits, objects), which stay
  INVARIABLE: des yeux bleu clair (light blue eyes — invariable), des
  robes orange (orange dresses — invariable, from the noun "orange").

CONJUGATION TABLE:
Regular agreement pattern:
masc. sing.   fem. sing.    masc. pl.    fem. pl.
grand          grande         grands        grandes
heureux        heureuse       heureux       heureuses

VOCABULARY:
grand(e) - big/tall            petit(e) - small
beau/belle - beautiful          joli(e) - pretty
bon(ne) / mauvais(e) - good/bad  nouveau/nouvelle - new
vieux/vieille - old              jeune - young

EXAMPLES:
C'est une belle maison ancienne. - It's a beautiful old house. (belle =
  BAGS, before; ancienne here means "old/historic," after the noun)
Il porte une chemise bleu marine. - He's wearing a navy blue shirt.
  (compound color, invariable)
Mon ancien collègue habite maintenant à Nice. - My former colleague now
  lives in Nice. (meaning-shift placement: "ancien" before = "former")
Elle a les cheveux longs et les yeux verts. - She has long hair and green
  eyes.

NOTES:
- When two adjectives (one BAGS, one not) both describe the same noun,
  each stays in its normal position: une petite voiture rouge (a little
  red car).

PRACTICE:
Make these agree correctly: un(e) (grand) maison, des (beau) fleurs, une
femme (heureux), des chaussures (marron - note: invariable, from the noun
"chestnut").

LESSON 41: Comparatives and Superlatives
================================================================

OVERVIEW:
How to say "more/less/as...as" (comparative) and "the most/least"
(superlative) in French, including several irregular forms that don't
follow the standard pattern.

GRAMMAR:
- Comparative with adjectives/adverbs:
    plus...que    - more...than       (plus grand que = taller than)
    moins...que   - less...than        (moins cher que = less expensive
      than)
    aussi...que   - as...as             (aussi rapide que = as fast as)
- Comparative with nouns (quantity): plus de / moins de / autant de +
  noun + que: Elle a plus d'argent que moi. (She has more money than me.)
- Comparative with verbs (how much an action happens): verb + plus/moins/
  autant + que: Il travaille plus que moi. (He works more than I do.)
- Superlative: le/la/les + plus/moins + adjective (+ de, if naming the
  group): C'est la plus grande ville de France. (It's the biggest city in
  France — note "de," not "dans," after a superlative naming a group.)
  With adjectives that normally follow the noun, the article can repeat:
  la voiture la plus rapide (the fastest car).
- Irregular comparative/superlative forms (do NOT use plus + these words):
    bon (good) -> meilleur (better) -> le meilleur (the best)
    mauvais (bad) -> pire OR plus mauvais (worse) -> le pire (the worst)
    bien (well, adverb) -> mieux (better) -> le mieux (the best)
  Note the important distinction: "meilleur" is an ADJECTIVE (agrees with
  a noun), "mieux" is an ADVERB (describes a verb): Ce gâteau est
  meilleur. (This cake is better.) vs. Elle chante mieux que moi. (She
  sings better than me.)

CONJUGATION TABLE:
plus/moins/aussi + adjective + que
le/la/les plus/moins + adjective (+ de + group)
bon -> meilleur -> le meilleur
bien -> mieux -> le mieux

VOCABULARY:
plus...que - more...than
moins...que - less...than
aussi...que - as...as
le/la plus / le/la moins - the most / the least
meilleur(e) - better (adj.)     mieux - better (adv.)

EXAMPLES:
Paris est plus grand que Lyon, mais moins grand que Tokyo. - Paris is
  bigger than Lyon, but smaller than Tokyo.
C'est le meilleur restaurant de la ville. - It's the best restaurant in
  town.
Elle parle français mieux que moi. - She speaks French better than I do.
J'ai autant de travail que toi cette semaine. - I have as much work as
  you this week.

NOTES:
- Common learner mistake: saying "plus bon" instead of "meilleur," or
  confusing meilleur (adjective) with mieux (adverb) — "Il est mieux"
  actually means "He is better [health-wise]," a special idiomatic use,
  not a general grammatical substitute for meilleur.

PRACTICE:
Compare yourself to a sibling or friend using three different structures:
plus...que, aussi...que, and meilleur/mieux.

LESSON 42: Demonstrative and Possessive Adjectives and Pronouns
================================================================

OVERVIEW:
"This/that/these/those" (demonstrative) and "my/your/his/her/our/their"
(possessive) — as both adjectives (before a noun) and standalone pronouns
(replacing a noun).

GRAMMAR:
- Demonstrative adjectives ("this/that"): ce (masc. sing.), cet (masc.
  sing. before a vowel/mute h), cette (fem. sing.), ces (plural, either
  gender). French doesn't distinguish "this" vs. "that" by default — add
  "-ci" (this, nearby) or "-là" (that, farther) after the noun for
  clarity/contrast: ce livre-ci (this book) vs. ce livre-là (that book).
- Possessive adjectives ("my/your/his/her/our/their") agree with the
  NOUN they describe, not with the owner's gender:
    mon/ma/mes (my), ton/ta/tes (your, informal), son/sa/ses (his/her/
      its — same form regardless of the OWNER's gender), notre/notre/nos
      (our), votre/votre/vos (your, formal/plural), leur/leur/leurs
      (their)
  Before a feminine noun starting with a vowel/mute h, use "mon/ton/son"
  instead of "ma/ta/sa" for pronunciation: mon amie (my friend, feminine).
- Possessive pronouns ("mine/yours/his/hers/ours/theirs") replace a noun
  entirely and include a definite article that agrees with the noun
  replaced: le mien/la mienne/les miens/les miennes (mine), le tien/la
  tienne (yours), le sien/la sienne (his/hers), le/la nôtre, les nôtres
  (ours), le/la vôtre, les vôtres (yours), le/la leur, les leurs (theirs).
- Demonstrative pronouns ("this one/that one/the one(s)"): celui (masc.
  sing.), celle (fem. sing.), ceux (masc. pl.), celles (fem. pl.) — almost
  always followed by -ci/-là, "de" + noun (showing possession), or a
  relative clause: celui-ci (this one), celle de Marie (Marie's, lit.
  "that of Marie"), ceux qui travaillent (those who work).
- "Ceci/cela/ça" are neutral demonstrative pronouns referring to an idea
  or unspecified thing rather than a specific noun (ça = informal
  contraction of cela, extremely common in speech): Ça va ? (How's it
  going?) Cela m'étonne. (That surprises me.)

CONJUGATION TABLE:
Demonstrative adjectives: ce / cet / cette / ces
Possessive adjectives (my): mon / ma / mes
Possessive pronouns (mine): le mien / la mienne / les miens / les miennes
Demonstrative pronouns: celui / celle / ceux / celles

VOCABULARY:
ce/cet/cette/ces - this/that/these/those
mon/ma/mes, ton/ta/tes, son/sa/ses - my, your, his/her
le mien, le tien, le sien - mine, yours, his/hers
celui-ci/celui-là - this one/that one

EXAMPLES:
Cette robe-ci est plus jolie que cette robe-là. - This dress is prettier
  than that dress.
Son frère et sa sœur habitent à Paris. - His/her brother and sister live
  in Paris. (son/sa depend on "frère/sœur" gender, NOT the owner's)
Ce n'est pas ma voiture, c'est la sienne. - It's not my car, it's his/
  hers.
Je préfère celui-ci à celui-là. - I prefer this one to that one.
Ça m'est égal. - It's all the same to me. / I don't mind.

NOTES:
- The #1 mistake for English speakers: using "son/sa" based on the
  OWNER's gender the way English does with "his/her." French bases it
  entirely on the gender of the NOUN owned: "sa voiture" can mean "his
  car" OR "her car" — context tells you which.

PRACTICE:
Translate, paying attention to son/sa/ses: "His mother is a doctor. Her
father is a teacher. Their house is big."

LESSON 43: Relative Pronouns
================================================================

OVERVIEW:
Relative pronouns connect a clause back to a noun already mentioned ("the
book THAT I read," "the woman WHO called") — French has more of these than
English, each with a specific grammatical role.

GRAMMAR:
- QUI = subject of the relative clause (who/which/that, doing the action).
  Never elides, never changes for gender/number: La femme qui parle est
  ma tante. (The woman who is speaking is my aunt.)
- QUE (QU') = direct object of the relative clause (whom/which/that,
  receiving the action). Elides before a vowel. Triggers past-participle
  agreement in compound tenses (Lesson 37): Le livre que j'ai lu était
  excellent. (The book that I read was excellent — "lu" would agree if
  feminine.)
- OÙ = place or time (where/when): La ville où j'habite est petite. (The
  city where I live is small.) Le jour où je suis né... (The day when I
  was born...)
- DONT = replaces "de + noun" (whose, of which, about which/whom) —
  used whenever the verb or expression in the relative clause normally
  takes "de": C'est le livre dont je parle. (It's the book I'm talking
  about — from "parler de.") La fille dont le père est médecin... (The
  girl whose father is a doctor... — "dont" replaces "le père DE la
  fille.")
- LEQUEL/LAQUELLE/LESQUELS/LESQUELLES = used after a preposition (other
  than "de," which prefers dont) when referring to a THING (not usually a
  person): Le stylo avec lequel j'écris... (The pen [that] I write
  with...) Contracts with à/de: auquel, duquel, auxquels, etc. For
  PEOPLE after a preposition, "qui" is generally preferred instead:
  la personne à qui je parle (the person I'm speaking to).
- CE QUI / CE QUE / CE DONT / CE À QUOI = "what" (the thing which/that),
  used when there's no specific noun antecedent, only an idea: Je ne sais
  pas ce qui se passe. (I don't know what's happening — ce qui = subject.)
  Dis-moi ce que tu veux. (Tell me what you want — ce que = direct
  object.) C'est ce dont j'ai besoin. (That's what I need — ce dont,
  from "avoir besoin de.")

CONJUGATION TABLE:
qui (subject) - que (direct object) - où (place/time) - dont (de + noun)
lequel/laquelle/lesquels/lesquelles (object of a preposition, things)
ce qui / ce que / ce dont (what..., no specific antecedent)

VOCABULARY:
qui / que / où / dont
ce qui / ce que / ce dont
lequel / laquelle

EXAMPLES:
J'ai un ami qui habite au Canada. - I have a friend who lives in Canada.
Le film que nous avons vu hier était nul. - The movie we saw yesterday
  was terrible.
C'est la maison où j'ai grandi. - This is the house where I grew up.
Voici le collègue dont je t'ai parlé. - Here's the colleague I told you
  about.
Je ne comprends pas ce qu'il veut. - I don't understand what he wants.

NOTES:
- If you can restate the missing piece as "de + noun" in your head, use
  "dont" — this single test resolves most qui/que/dont confusion.

PRACTICE:
Combine each pair of sentences with a relative pronoun: "J'ai un livre.
Le livre est intéressant." / "Je parle d'un problème. Le problème est
sérieux."

LESSON 44: Adverbs and Expressions of Time, Frequency, and Location
================================================================

OVERVIEW:
Beyond the -ment adverbs from Lesson 3, this lesson rounds out the
essential toolkit for saying when, how often, and where something happens.

GRAMMAR:
- Adverb formation recap: feminine form of the adjective + -ment (lente ->
  lentement). Adjectives ending in a vowel in the masculine use the
  masculine form directly + -ment (vrai -> vraiment). Adjectives ending in
  -ant/-ent change to -amment/-emment (constant -> constamment; évident ->
  évidemment).
- Placement of adverbs: usually AFTER a conjugated verb in simple tenses
  (Il parle bien.), and typically BETWEEN the helper verb and past
  participle for short/common adverbs in compound tenses (Il a bien
  parlé.) — unlike English, which puts the adverb after the whole verb
  phrase.
- Common time adverbs: maintenant (now), bientôt (soon), déjà (already),
  encore (still/again), toujours (always/still), enfin (finally), tout de
  suite (right away).
- Frequency adverbs: souvent (often), parfois/quelquefois (sometimes),
  rarement (rarely), jamais (never, needs "ne"), toujours (always), une
  fois/deux fois (once/twice).
- Location adverbs and prepositions: ici (here), là (there), là-bas (over
  there), devant/derrière (in front of/behind), à côté de (next to),
  entre (between), sous/sur (under/on), dans (in), loin de/près de
  (far from/near).

CONJUGATION TABLE:
Adverb formation: lent(e) -> lentement ; vrai -> vraiment ;
  constant -> constamment ; évident -> évidemment

VOCABULARY:
souvent / parfois / rarement / jamais - often/sometimes/rarely/never
déjà / encore / toujours - already/still-again/always-still
devant / derrière / à côté de - in front of/behind/next to
loin de / près de - far from/near

EXAMPLES:
Elle a bien réussi son examen. - She did well on her exam. (adverb between
  helper and participle)
Nous allons rarement au cinéma. - We rarely go to the movies.
La pharmacie est juste à côté de la banque. - The pharmacy is right next
  to the bank.
Il habite encore chez ses parents. - He still lives with his parents.

NOTES:
- "Encore" is doing double duty in French: it means both "still" (Il
  travaille encore = He's still working) and "again" (Recommence encore !
  = Start again!) — context determines which.

PRACTICE:
Describe how often you do five daily activities using souvent, parfois,
rarement, and jamais.

================================================================
LESSON 45: Verbs with Prepositions, Indefinite Relatives, and
Fixed Expressions
================================================================

OVERVIEW:
A grab-bag "advanced toolkit" lesson: verbs whose French preposition
doesn't match the English one, the "-ever" words (whatever, whoever...),
and two useful idiomatic structures.

GRAMMAR:
- Some French verbs take a DIFFERENT preposition than their English
  equivalent, or need none at all where English needs one — these must be
  memorized individually as you learn each verb:
    écouter (to listen TO) — no preposition: J'écoute la radio.
    regarder (to watch/look AT) — no preposition: Je regarde la télé.
    chercher (to look FOR) — no preposition: Je cherche mes clés.
    attendre (to wait FOR) — no preposition: J'attends le bus.
    payer (to pay FOR) — no preposition: J'ai payé le repas.
    demander (to ask FOR) — no preposition for the thing asked for:
      Je demande l'addition.
    téléphoner À quelqu'un (to call someone) — needs à, unlike English
      "call someone" (no preposition): Je téléphone à ma mère.
    se souvenir DE (to remember) — needs de: Je me souviens de ce jour.
    entrer DANS (to enter) — needs dans, not just "into": Il entre dans
      la maison.
- Indefinite relative expressions ("-ever" words), formed with "que" +
  subjunctive:
    quoi que + subjunctive - whatever (Quoi que tu fasses, je te
      soutiendrai. = Whatever you do, I'll support you.)
    qui que + subjunctive - whoever (Qui que ce soit, dis-lui d'entrer.)
    où que + subjunctive - wherever (Où que tu ailles, écris-moi.)
    quel(le) que soit + noun - whatever/whichever (Quelle que soit la
      raison... = Whatever the reason may be...)
- "Avoir beau + infinitive" = "no matter how much/despite trying to..."
  (concessive): J'ai beau essayer, je n'y arrive pas. (No matter how hard
  I try, I can't manage it.)
- "Quitte à + infinitive" = "even if it means..." (accepting a
  consequence): Je vais finir ce soir, quitte à me coucher tard. (I'll
  finish tonight, even if it means going to bed late.)

CONJUGATION TABLE:
(reference lists — not a single verb)

VOCABULARY:
écouter / regarder / chercher / attendre / payer (no preposition needed,
  unlike English)
téléphoner à / se souvenir de / entrer dans
quoi que / qui que / où que + subjunctive
avoir beau + infinitive

EXAMPLES:
J'attends mon ami depuis une heure. - I've been waiting for my friend for
  an hour.
Elle a téléphoné à sa grand-mère hier soir. - She called her grandmother
  last night.
Quoi qu'il arrive, nous serons prêts. - Whatever happens, we'll be ready.
J'ai beau lui expliquer, il ne comprend pas. - No matter how much I
  explain to him, he doesn't understand.

NOTES:
- Build a personal running list of "tricky preposition" verbs as you meet
  them in reading/listening — this is a vocabulary-memorization problem
  more than a grammar-rule problem.

PRACTICE:
Translate, watching the prepositions: "I'm looking for my phone." "She's
listening to music." "We're waiting for the train."
