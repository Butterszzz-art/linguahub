LESSON 18: The Passé Composé with Avoir
================================================================

OVERVIEW:
The passé composé is the everyday "I did / I have done" past tense in
French — the one you'll use most in conversation. Most verbs form it with
avoir as the helper.

GRAMMAR:
- Formation: conjugated avoir (present tense) + past participle of the
  main verb.
- Regular past participles:
    -er verbs -> -é   (parler -> parlé)
    -ir verbs -> -i    (finir -> fini)
    -re verbs -> -u    (vendre -> vendu)
- Negation wraps the helper verb: Je n'ai pas mangé. (I haven't eaten.)
- Questions with inversion invert the helper and subject: As-tu mangé ?
  (Did you eat?)
- The passé composé translates several English past forms: "I ate" / "I
  have eaten" / "I did eat" are all "j'ai mangé."

CONJUGATION TABLE:
parler (to speak) — passé composé:
j'ai parlé          nous avons parlé
tu as parlé          vous avez parlé
il/elle/on a parlé    ils/elles ont parlé

VOCABULARY:
hier - yesterday          la semaine dernière - last week
déjà - already             pas encore - not yet
ce matin - this morning     tout à l'heure - a while ago / earlier

EXAMPLES:
J'ai mangé une pomme ce matin. - I ate an apple this morning.
Nous avons regardé un film hier soir. - We watched a movie last night.
Est-ce que tu as fini tes devoirs ? - Did you finish your homework?
Elle n'a pas encore répondu. - She hasn't answered yet.

NOTES:
- Word order with negation and adverbs like "déjà"/"pas encore" typically
  slots them between the helper verb and the past participle: J'ai déjà
  mangé (I've already eaten).

PRACTICE:
Conjugate "finir" and "vendre" fully in the passé composé.

================================================================
LESSON 19: Irregular Past Participles & the Passé Composé with Être
================================================================

OVERVIEW:
Many common, high-frequency verbs have irregular past participles, and a
small but important group of verbs (mostly verbs of motion, plus all
reflexive verbs) use être instead of avoir as their helper — with an
agreement rule that doesn't apply to avoir verbs.

GRAMMAR:
- Common irregular past participles to memorize:
    être -> été       avoir -> eu         faire -> fait
    prendre -> pris    mettre -> mis       dire -> dit
    voir -> vu          savoir -> su        pouvoir -> pu
    vouloir -> voulu    devoir -> dû        venir -> venu
    écrire -> écrit      lire -> lu          ouvrir -> ouvert
- A specific set of (mostly) intransitive motion/change-of-state verbs use
  être as the helper instead of avoir. A common memory device is the
  house "DR. & MRS. VANDERTRAMP" (each letter starts one of these verbs):
    Devenir (become), Revenir (come back), Monter (go up), Rester (stay),
    Sortir (go out), Venir (come), Aller (go), Naître (be born),
    Descendre (go down), Entrer (enter), Rentrer (go back in/home),
    Tomber (fall), Retourner (return), Arriver (arrive),
    Mourir (die), Partir (leave)
- With être verbs, the past participle AGREES in gender/number with the
  SUBJECT (like an adjective): elle est allée (she went, feminine -e
  added), ils sont partis (they left, masculine plural -s added), elles
  sont arrivées (they arrived, feminine plural -es added).
- Reflexive verbs (Lesson 14) ALSO always use être in the passé composé,
  with the reflexive pronoun placed before the helper: elle s'est levée
  (she got up). The past participle usually agrees with the reflexive
  pronoun when it functions as a direct object (most common case), but
  does NOT agree if the reflexive pronoun is an indirect object (e.g.,
  se laver les mains -> elle s'est lavé les mains — no agreement, because
  "les mains" is the direct object, not "se").

CONJUGATION TABLE:
aller (to go) — passé composé:
je suis allé(e)          nous sommes allé(e)s
tu es allé(e)             vous êtes allé(e)(s)
il est allé / elle est allée   ils sont allés / elles sont allées

se lever (to get up) — passé composé:
je me suis levé(e)        nous nous sommes levé(e)s
tu t'es levé(e)            vous vous êtes levé(e)(s)
il s'est levé / elle s'est levée   ils se sont levés / elles se sont levées

VOCABULARY:
(the DR & MRS VANDERTRAMP verbs — see grammar section above)
naître - to be born      mourir - to die
monter - to go up         descendre - to go down
rester - to stay           tomber - to fall

EXAMPLES:
Elle est née en 1995 à Marseille. - She was born in 1995 in Marseille.
Nous sommes rentrés tard hier soir. - We got home late last night.
Ils se sont rencontrés à l'université. - They met each other at university.
J'ai déjà vu ce film. - I've already seen this movie. (avoir + irregular
  participle "vu")

NOTES:
- Some VANDERTRAMP verbs (monter, descendre, sortir, rentrer, retourner)
  can also be used transitively (with a direct object) — when they take a
  direct object, they switch to avoir: Elle a monté les valises. (She
  brought the suitcases up.) vs. Elle est montée. (She went up.)

PRACTICE:
Conjugate "partir" and "se laver" fully in the passé composé, marking the
agreement on the past participle for each subject.

LESSON 20: The Imparfait
================================================================

OVERVIEW:
The imparfait ("imperfect") is French's other main past tense — used for
descriptions, background, habits, and ongoing states in the past, as
opposed to the completed actions of the passé composé.

GRAMMAR:
- Formation: take the nous form of the present tense, drop -ons, and add:
    -ais, -ais, -ait, -ions, -iez, -aient
  (This works for every verb except être, whose stem is irregular: ét-.)
- Uses of the imparfait:
    1. Descriptions in the past (people, places, weather, states of mind):
       Il faisait beau. (The weather was nice.)
    2. Habitual/repeated past actions ("used to," "would [routinely]"):
       Quand j'étais petit, je jouais dehors tous les jours. (When I was
       little, I used to play outside every day.)
    3. An ongoing action interrupted by another action (often paired with
       passé composé for the interrupting event): Je dormais quand le
       téléphone a sonné. (I was sleeping when the phone rang.)
    4. Polite requests/suggestions: Je voulais vous demander... (I wanted
       to ask you...)

CONJUGATION TABLE:
parler (to speak) — imparfait:
je parlais          nous parlions
tu parlais           vous parliez
il/elle/on parlait    ils/elles parlaient

être (to be) — imparfait (irregular stem ét-):
j'étais    tu étais    il était    nous étions    vous étiez    ils étaient

VOCABULARY:
autrefois - in the past / long ago
tous les jours/ans - every day/year
souvent - often
d'habitude - usually
pendant que - while

EXAMPLES:
Quand j'étais enfant, j'habitais à la campagne. - When I was a child, I
  lived in the countryside.
Il pleuvait quand nous sommes sortis. - It was raining when we went out.
Nous allions à la plage tous les étés. - We used to go to the beach every
  summer.
Elle était fatiguée après le travail. - She was tired after work.

NOTES:
- Weather, age, time, and emotional/physical states are almost always in
  the imparfait when narrating the past, because they describe a
  background condition rather than a completed event.

PRACTICE:
Describe your childhood home and a typical day from your childhood using
five imparfait sentences.

================================================================
LESSON 21: Imparfait vs. Passé Composé
================================================================

OVERVIEW:
This is the single trickiest contrast for English speakers learning French
past tenses — both translate to English simple past, but they express
fundamentally different things.

GRAMMAR:
- Passé composé = a completed, bounded action or event — something that
  happened and finished, often with a clear beginning/end or a specific
  number of occurrences: J'ai mangé à midi. (I ate at noon — one specific,
  completed event.)
- Imparfait = an ongoing state, background description, habitual action,
  or a scene being "set" — no clear boundary on when it started or
  stopped: Je mangeais quand tu as appelé. (I was eating [ongoing] when
  you called [completed interruption].)
- A useful test: if you can insert "was/were -ing" or "used to" in English
  and it still makes sense, it's imparfait. If the action is a single
  completed event you could put on a timeline with a clear end, it's
  passé composé.
- The same verb can appear in either tense with a real meaning shift:
    Il faisait beau. (It was nice out — ongoing description.)
    Il a fait beau toute la journée. (It was nice ALL DAY — a bounded,
      completed period, viewed as one whole block of time.)
- Certain verbs (connaître, pouvoir, savoir, vouloir) shift MEANING
  depending on which past tense is used:
    je connaissais (imparfait) = I knew (a person, over time) vs.
      j'ai connu (passé composé) = I met (for the first time)
    je pouvais (imparfait) = I was able to (in general/had the ability) vs.
      j'ai pu (passé composé) = I managed to / succeeded in (one specific
      instance)
    je savais (imparfait) = I knew (a fact, ongoing) vs.
      j'ai su (passé composé) = I found out / learned (at a specific moment)
    je voulais (imparfait) = I wanted (ongoing desire) vs.
      j'ai voulu (passé composé) = I decided to / tried to (and often
      failed); je n'ai pas voulu = I refused

CONJUGATION TABLE:
(contrast reference — see Lessons 18–20 for full conjugations)

VOCABULARY:
soudain / tout à coup - suddenly
un jour - one day
pendant que - while
à ce moment-là - at that moment

EXAMPLES:
J'étais à la maison quand le téléphone a sonné. - I was at home when the
  phone rang. (imparfait = background state; passé composé = the event)
Nous parlions français ensemble quand nous étions enfants. - We used to
  speak French together when we were children. (both imparfait — habitual
  background)
Elle a couru pour attraper le bus. - She ran to catch the bus. (single
  completed action)
Je ne savais pas que tu étais marié. J'ai su hier. - I didn't know you
  were married. I found out yesterday. (meaning shift: savais=knew,
  ongoing; ai su=found out, punctual)

NOTES:
- Don't overthink single-word translation — think in terms of "is this a
  photograph (imparfait, a state/scene) or a video clip with a clear start
  and end (passé composé, an event)?"

PRACTICE:
Write a 4–5 sentence short story about last weekend, deliberately mixing
imparfait (for background/description) and passé composé (for the actions
that happened).

LESSON 22: The Plus-que-parfait
================================================================

OVERVIEW:
The plus-que-parfait ("pluperfect") expresses an action that happened
BEFORE another past action — the French equivalent of English "had done."

GRAMMAR:
- Formation: imparfait of avoir or être (same helper-verb rule as passé
  composé) + past participle.
- Used to show that one past event occurred earlier than another past
  event already being discussed (usually in imparfait or passé composé):
  Quand je suis arrivé, elle était déjà partie. (When I arrived, she had
  already left — her leaving happened before my arriving.)
- Agreement rules for the past participle are identical to the passé
  composé (agree with subject for être verbs, agree with a preceding
  direct object for avoir verbs — see Lesson 19).
- Often appears with "déjà" (already) or in reported/indirect speech
  referring back to an earlier past event.

CONJUGATION TABLE:
parler (to speak) — plus-que-parfait:
j'avais parlé          nous avions parlé
tu avais parlé           vous aviez parlé
il/elle/on avait parlé    ils/elles avaient parlé

aller (to go) — plus-que-parfait:
j'étais allé(e)          nous étions allé(e)s
tu étais allé(e)           vous étiez allé(e)(s)
il était allé / elle était allée   ils étaient allés / elles étaient allées

VOCABULARY:
déjà - already
avant que - before
ne...jamais (+ plus-que-parfait) - had never

EXAMPLES:
Il avait déjà mangé quand nous sommes arrivés. - He had already eaten when
  we arrived.
Je n'avais jamais visité Paris avant ce voyage. - I had never visited
  Paris before this trip.
Elle était déjà partie quand j'ai téléphoné. - She had already left when
  I called.
Nous avions fini nos devoirs avant le dîner. - We had finished our
  homework before dinner.

NOTES:
- Think of the plus-que-parfait as "the past of the past" — it always sets
  up a relationship to another, more recent past event mentioned nearby.

PRACTICE:
Combine each pair of events using plus-que-parfait + passé composé: "I
arrived at the station. The train had already left." / "She called me. I
had already gone to bed."

LESSON 23: The Passé Simple
================================================================

OVERVIEW:
The passé simple is a literary/formal past tense — you'll almost never use
it in speech, but you need to RECOGNIZE it to read novels, newspapers,
historical writing, and formal narration. It covers the same ground as the
passé composé but in a more formal written register.

GRAMMAR:
- Formation for regular verbs — drop the infinitive ending and add:
    -er verbs: -ai, -as, -a, -âmes, -âtes, -èrent (parler -> il parla)
    -ir/-re verbs: -is, -is, -it, -îmes, -îtes, -irent (finir -> il finit;
      vendre -> il vendit)
- Many irregular verbs have their own passé simple stem, often related to
  the past participle: avoir -> il eut; être -> il fut; faire -> il fit;
  venir -> il vint; voir -> il vit; prendre -> il prit.
- In modern usage, the passé simple survives almost exclusively in the
  3rd person (il/elle/on and ils/elles) — the 1st/2nd person forms are
  rare even in literature, replaced by passé composé in nearly all
  contexts.
- Recognition tip: if you see an unfamiliar verb form ending in -a, -it,
  -ut, -int, or -èrent/-irent/-urent in a story or historical text, it's
  very likely a passé simple form of a verb you already know in another
  tense.

CONJUGATION TABLE:
parler (to speak) — passé simple (3rd person, most useful to recognize):
il/elle/on parla        ils/elles parlèrent
finir (to finish) — passé simple:
il/elle/on finit         ils/elles finirent
être (to be) — passé simple (irregular):
il/elle/on fut            ils/elles furent
avoir (to have) — passé simple (irregular):
il/elle/on eut            ils/elles eurent

VOCABULARY:
il était une fois - once upon a time
soudain - suddenly
alors - then/so
(the passé simple is common in fairy tales and historical narration)

EXAMPLES:
Il était une fois une princesse qui vivait dans un château. Un jour, elle
  rencontra un dragon. - Once upon a time there was a princess who lived
  in a castle. One day, she met a dragon. ("rencontra" = passé simple of
  rencontrer)
Napoléon naquit en 1769 et mourut en 1821. - Napoleon was born in 1769 and
  died in 1821. (naquit/mourut = passé simple, common in history texts)

NOTES:
- For self-study purposes, focus on RECOGNIZING the 3rd-person singular
  and plural forms in reading — you do not need to actively produce this
  tense in conversation or everyday writing.

PRACTICE:
In a French news article, fairy tale, or history text, find 3 passé simple
verbs and identify their infinitive and their passé composé equivalent.
