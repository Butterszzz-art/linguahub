LESSON 9: Regular -ER Verbs in the Present Tense
================================================================

OVERVIEW:
About 90% of French verbs end in -er and follow one predictable pattern.
Master this conjugation and you can immediately use hundreds of verbs.

GRAMMAR:
- Take the infinitive (parler = to speak), remove -er to get the stem
  (parl-), and add these endings:
    je -> -e        nous -> -ons
    tu -> -es        vous -> -ez
    il/elle/on -> -e   ils/elles -> -ent
- The endings -e, -es, -e, -ent are all SILENT in speech — je parle, tu
  parles, il parle, and ils parlent all sound identical when spoken aloud.
  Only nous (-ons) and vous (-ez) sound distinct.
- French present tense covers what English expresses with THREE forms:
  "I speak" / "I am speaking" / "I do speak" are all simply "je parle."

CONJUGATION TABLE:
parler (to speak):
je parle          nous parlons
tu parles          vous parlez
il/elle/on parle    ils/elles parlent

VOCABULARY:
parler - to speak          aimer - to like/love
habiter - to live (reside)  travailler - to work
manger* - to eat (*keeps the "e" before -ons: mangeons, see Lesson 10)
regarder - to watch/look at  écouter - to listen to
chercher - to look for       donner - to give
jouer - to play               étudier - to study

EXAMPLES:
Je parle français et anglais. - I speak French and English.
Nous habitons à Lyon. - We live in Lyon.
Elle travaille dans un bureau. - She works in an office.
Vous étudiez le français ? - Are you studying French?

NOTES:
- Because so many verbs are -er verbs, this pattern is the foundation for
  almost everything that follows — drill it until it's automatic.

PRACTICE:
Conjugate "aimer" (to like) for all six subject pronouns, then write one
sentence with each conjugated form.

================================================================
LESSON 10: -ER Verbs with Spelling and Stem Changes
================================================================

OVERVIEW:
A subset of -er verbs make small spelling adjustments to preserve their
sound across the conjugation. These are still "regular" in logic, just with
a predictable twist.

GRAMMAR:
- Verbs ending in -cer: c becomes ç before -ons (to keep the soft "s" sound):
  commencer -> nous commençons.
- Verbs ending in -ger: g gets an extra e before -ons (to keep the soft
  "zh" sound): manger -> nous mangeons.
- Verbs ending in -e + consonant + er (like acheter, lever): the mute "e"
  becomes è in all forms EXCEPT nous/vous: j'achète, tu achètes, il achète,
  ils achètent, but nous achetons, vous achetez.
- Verbs ending in -é + consonant + er (like préférer, espérer): é becomes è
  in the same pattern (except nous/vous): je préfère, but nous préférons.
- Verbs ending in -eler/-eter: some double the consonant instead of adding
  an accent: appeler -> j'appelle, nous appelons; jeter -> je jette, nous
  jetons.
- Verbs ending in -yer: y becomes i before a silent ending (everywhere
  except nous/vous): payer -> je paie, nous payons. (For -ayer verbs like
  payer, keeping the y — je paye — is also accepted.)

CONJUGATION TABLE:
commencer (to begin): je commence, tu commences, il commence,
  nous commençons, vous commencez, ils commencent
manger (to eat): je mange, tu manges, il mange,
  nous mangeons, vous mangez, ils mangent
acheter (to buy): j'achète, tu achètes, il achète,
  nous achetons, vous achetez, ils achètent
préférer (to prefer): je préfère, tu préfères, il préfère,
  nous préférons, vous préférez, ils préfèrent

VOCABULARY:
commencer - to begin       manger - to eat
acheter - to buy            préférer - to prefer
appeler - to call            jeter - to throw (away)
payer - to pay               nettoyer - to clean
voyager - to travel          nager - to swim

EXAMPLES:
Nous commençons le cours à neuf heures. - We start class at 9:00.
Je préfère le café au thé. - I prefer coffee to tea.
Elle achète des légumes au marché. - She buys vegetables at the market.
Nous voyageons souvent en été. - We travel often in summer.

NOTES:
- These changes exist purely to preserve pronunciation — say the verb out
  loud and the spelling logic usually becomes obvious.

PRACTICE:
Conjugate "voyager" and "appeler" fully; circle the forms that differ from
the "regular" -er pattern.

================================================================
LESSON 11: -IR and -RE Verbs in the Present Tense
================================================================

OVERVIEW:
After -er verbs, French has two more regular families: -ir verbs (like
finir) and -re verbs (like vendre), each with their own predictable ending
pattern.

GRAMMAR:
- Regular -ir verbs: remove -ir from the infinitive, add:
    -is, -is, -it, -issons, -issez, -issent
  Note the -iss- that appears in the plural forms only.
- Regular -re verbs: remove -re from the infinitive, add:
    -s, -s, - (nothing), -ons, -ez, -ent
  The il/elle/on form has NO added ending — you just use the bare stem.
- Not all -ir verbs follow this pattern (many common ones like partir,
  sortir, venir are irregular — covered later), but the -iss- pattern
  covers a large regular group (finir, choisir, réussir, grandir...).

CONJUGATION TABLE:
finir (to finish): je finis, tu finis, il finit,
  nous finissons, vous finissez, ils finissent
vendre (to sell): je vends, tu vends, il vend,
  nous vendons, vous vendez, ils vendent

VOCABULARY:
finir - to finish        choisir - to choose
réussir - to succeed      grandir - to grow (up)
vendre - to sell          attendre - to wait (for)
répondre - to answer/reply  perdre - to lose
descendre - to go down     entendre - to hear

EXAMPLES:
Je finis mes devoirs à six heures. - I finish my homework at 6:00.
Elle choisit une robe rouge. - She chooses a red dress.
Nous attendons le bus. - We're waiting for the bus.
Ils vendent leur voiture. - They're selling their car.

NOTES:
- "Attendre" means "to wait for" — no separate preposition needed, unlike
  English "wait FOR": J'attends Marie (I'm waiting for Marie), not
  "j'attends pour Marie."

PRACTICE:
Conjugate "choisir" and "répondre" fully; write one example sentence for
each subject pronoun form.

================================================================
LESSON 12: Être and Avoir (To Be and To Have)
================================================================

OVERVIEW:
Être and avoir are the two most important irregular verbs in French — they
appear constantly on their own AND as helper verbs for compound tenses
(Part 3), so full memorization now pays off for the rest of the course.

GRAMMAR:
- Être (to be) and avoir (to have) are both completely irregular and must
  be memorized outright.
- Avoir is used in many idiomatic expressions where English uses "to be":
  avoir faim (to be hungry), avoir soif (to be thirsty), avoir chaud/froid
  (to be hot/cold), avoir peur (to be afraid), avoir raison/tort (to be
  right/wrong), avoir X ans (to be X years old), avoir besoin de (to need),
  avoir envie de (to feel like / want).
- "Il y a" (there is / there are) is built from avoir and doesn't change
  for number: Il y a un chat. / Il y a deux chats.

CONJUGATION TABLE:
être (to be): je suis, tu es, il/elle/on est,
  nous sommes, vous êtes, ils/elles sont
avoir (to have): j'ai, tu as, il/elle/on a,
  nous avons, vous avez, ils/elles ont

VOCABULARY:
être - to be                avoir - to have
avoir faim/soif - to be hungry/thirsty
avoir chaud/froid - to be hot/cold
avoir peur (de) - to be afraid (of)
avoir raison/tort - to be right/wrong
avoir besoin de - to need
il y a - there is/are

EXAMPLES:
Je suis étudiant et j'ai vingt ans. - I am a student and I am 20 years old.
Nous avons faim, allons manger ! - We're hungry, let's go eat!
Il y a beaucoup de monde ici. - There are a lot of people here.
Vous êtes en retard. - You are late.

NOTES:
- These two verbs are the base for the passé composé (Part 3) — memorizing
  them solidly now will make the past tense much easier later.

PRACTICE:
Translate using avoir-expressions: "I'm cold," "She's 30 years old,"
"We need a car," "They're afraid of the dog."

================================================================
LESSON 13: Aller, Venir, Faire — and the Immediate Future & Past
================================================================

OVERVIEW:
Three more essential irregular verbs, plus two of the most useful shortcut
constructions in French: the immediate ("near") future and immediate past.

GRAMMAR:
- Aller (to go) is also used to form the futur proche (immediate future):
  aller (conjugated) + infinitive = "going to [do something]":
  Je vais manger. (I'm going to eat.) This is the everyday way to talk
  about the near future, more common in speech than the true future tense.
- Venir (to come) is used to form the passé récent (immediate past):
  venir de (conjugated) + infinitive = "just [did something]":
  Je viens de manger. (I just ate.)
- Faire (to do/make) appears in weather expressions (il fait beau/chaud/
  froid = it's nice/hot/cold out) and countless idioms (faire les courses
  = to run errands, faire du sport = to play sports, faire la cuisine =
  to cook).
- The causative "faire + infinitive" means to have/make someone do
  something, or to have something done: Je fais réparer ma voiture. (I'm
  having my car repaired — lit. "I make repair my car.")

CONJUGATION TABLE:
aller (to go): je vais, tu vas, il va,
  nous allons, vous allez, ils vont
venir (to come): je viens, tu viens, il vient,
  nous venons, vous venez, ils viennent
faire (to do/make): je fais, tu fais, il fait,
  nous faisons, vous faites, ils font

VOCABULARY:
aller - to go              venir - to come
faire - to do/make          venir de + infinitif - to have just done something
aller + infinitif - to be going to do something
faire les courses - to run errands
faire du sport - to play sports/exercise
faire la cuisine - to cook

EXAMPLES:
Je vais partir dans dix minutes. - I'm going to leave in ten minutes.
Nous venons de finir le dîner. - We just finished dinner.
Il fait très chaud aujourd'hui. - It's very hot today.
Elle fait ses devoirs. - She's doing her homework.

NOTES:
- "Venir de + infinitive" is one of the most useful shortcuts in French —
  it lets you express "just did X" without touching the past tense at all.

PRACTICE:
Rewrite these using futur proche and passé récent: "I am going to call you"
/ "We just arrived."

================================================================
LESSON 14: Reflexive (Pronominal) Verbs
================================================================

OVERVIEW:
Reflexive verbs describe an action the subject does to itself (washing
oneself, getting up) and are extremely common in French daily-routine
vocabulary.

GRAMMAR:
- A reflexive verb pairs a reflexive pronoun (matching the subject) with
  the verb: me/te/se/nous/vous/se, placed directly BEFORE the conjugated
  verb.
- Conjugation otherwise follows the normal pattern for the verb's family
  (-er/-ir/-re): se laver (to wash oneself) conjugates like laver, just
  with the reflexive pronoun added.
- Not all "reflexive-looking" verbs are truly reflexive in meaning — some
  are "reciprocal" (two people doing something to each other: se parler =
  to talk to each other) and some are simply pronominal by convention
  (s'en aller = to leave/go away — no real "self" involved).
- In the negative, "ne" goes before the reflexive pronoun and "pas" after
  the verb: Je ne me lève pas tôt. (I don't get up early.)
- In the imperative (commands), reflexive pronouns move AFTER the verb in
  the affirmative, connected with a hyphen, and te becomes toi:
  Lève-toi ! (Get up!) but in the negative they stay before: Ne te lève
  pas ! (Don't get up!)

CONJUGATION TABLE:
se laver (to wash oneself):
je me lave         nous nous lavons
tu te laves         vous vous lavez
il/elle/on se lave   ils/elles se lavent

VOCABULARY:
se lever - to get up          se coucher - to go to bed
se laver - to wash oneself     s'habiller - to get dressed
se réveiller - to wake up      se dépêcher - to hurry
se reposer - to rest            s'appeler - to be named/called
se brosser (les dents) - to brush (one's teeth)

EXAMPLES:
Je me réveille à sept heures et je me lève tout de suite. - I wake up at
  7:00 and I get up right away.
Elle s'habille rapidement. - She gets dressed quickly.
Nous nous couchons tard le week-end. - We go to bed late on weekends.
Dépêche-toi, on est en retard ! - Hurry up, we're late! (informal command)

NOTES:
- With body parts and reflexive verbs, French uses the definite article
  (le/la/les), not a possessive: "je me brosse LES dents" (not "mes
  dents") — the reflexive pronoun already shows whose teeth they are.

PRACTICE:
Describe your morning routine in five sentences using at least four
reflexive verbs from the vocabulary list.

LESSON 15: Negation
================================================================

OVERVIEW:
The basic negative structure and its variations — how to say "not,"
"never," "nothing," "no longer," and more.

GRAMMAR:
- Basic negation wraps the conjugated verb in ne...pas: Je ne parle pas
  espagnol. (I don't speak Spanish.) "Ne" becomes "n'" before a vowel/mute h.
- With reflexive verbs, "ne" goes before the reflexive pronoun: Je ne me
  lève pas. (I don't get up.)
- With compound tenses (passé composé, Part 3), ne...pas wraps the
  helper verb, not the past participle: Je n'ai pas mangé. (I haven't
  eaten.)
- Other negative expressions follow the same "ne...X" pattern, with X
  replacing "pas":
    ne...jamais    - never
    ne...rien      - nothing
    ne...personne  - nobody/no one
    ne...plus      - no longer/no more
    ne...ni...ni   - neither...nor
    ne...que       - only (restrictive, not a true negation)
    ne...aucun(e)  - none/not any
- After a negation, indefinite/partitive articles (un/une/des/du/de la)
  usually shrink to "de" (see Lesson 4): J'ai des amis -> Je n'ai pas
  d'amis.
- "Personne" and "rien" can also start a sentence as the subject, still
  paired with "ne": Personne ne sait. (No one knows.) Rien n'est facile.
  (Nothing is easy.)

CONJUGATION TABLE:
(pattern table, not a verb)
ne + [pronoun +] verb + pas/jamais/rien/plus/personne...

VOCABULARY:
ne...pas - not
ne...jamais - never
ne...rien - nothing
ne...personne - no one
ne...plus - no longer
ne...que - only
ne...ni...ni - neither...nor

EXAMPLES:
Je ne mange jamais de viande. - I never eat meat.
Il n'y a rien dans le frigo. - There's nothing in the fridge.
Nous n'avons plus de pain. - We don't have any more bread.
Elle ne parle à personne. - She doesn't talk to anyone.
Je n'ai qu'un frère. - I only have one brother.

NOTES:
- In casual spoken French, "ne" is very often dropped entirely (Je sais
  pas instead of Je ne sais pas), but you should always include it in
  writing and formal speech.

PRACTICE:
Negate these sentences using the indicated expression: "Je bois du café"
(ne...jamais); "Il y a quelqu'un" (ne...personne); "J'ai des devoirs"
(ne...plus).

LESSON 16: Asking Questions
================================================================

OVERVIEW:
Three ways to turn a statement into a yes/no question in French, from most
casual to most formal, plus the core question words for open-ended
questions.

GRAMMAR:
- Method 1 — Intonation (most casual, common in speech): keep normal word
  order, just raise your pitch at the end: Tu parles français ? (You speak
  French?)
- Method 2 — Est-ce que (neutral, works everywhere): add "est-ce que" to
  the front of a normal statement: Est-ce que tu parles français ?
- Method 3 — Inversion (more formal/written): swap the subject pronoun and
  verb, joined by a hyphen: Parles-tu français ? With il/elle/on and a
  verb ending in a vowel, insert -t- for pronunciation: Parle-t-il
  français ?
- Question words (interrogatives) go at the front of the sentence, usually
  followed by est-ce que or inversion:
    qui       - who
    que/qu'   - what (object)
    quoi      - what (after a preposition, or standalone)
    où        - where
    quand     - when
    pourquoi  - why
    comment   - how
    combien (de) - how much/many
    quel(le)(s)  - which/what (agrees with the noun)
- "Quel" vs. "qui/que": use "quel" when directly modifying a noun (Quelle
  heure est-il ?), use qui/que when standing alone as a pronoun (Qu'est-ce
  que tu fais ?).

CONJUGATION TABLE:
(pattern reference, not a verb)
Statement:        Tu aimes le café.
Intonation:        Tu aimes le café ?
Est-ce que:         Est-ce que tu aimes le café ?
Inversion:          Aimes-tu le café ?

VOCABULARY:
qui - who              que/qu'est-ce que - what
où - where               quand - when
pourquoi - why           comment - how
combien - how much/many   quel(le) - which/what

EXAMPLES:
Où habites-tu ? - Where do you live?
Qu'est-ce que vous faites ce soir ? - What are you doing tonight?
Combien coûte ce livre ? - How much does this book cost?
Quelle heure est-il ? - What time is it?
Pourquoi est-ce que tu pleures ? - Why are you crying?

NOTES:
- "Pourquoi" is answered with "parce que" (because): Pourquoi tu pars ?
  Parce que je suis fatigué. (Why are you leaving? Because I'm tired.)

PRACTICE:
Turn "Vous habitez à Paris" into a question all three ways (intonation,
est-ce que, inversion).

LESSON 17: Devoir and Related Structures
================================================================

OVERVIEW:
Devoir (must/have to/should) is one of the most useful and most irregular
verbs in French, plus two handy fixed expressions and a family of verbs
with a distinctive ending pattern.

GRAMMAR:
- Devoir + infinitive expresses obligation ("must/have to") or probability
  ("must be"), depending on context: Je dois partir. (I have to leave.)
  Il doit être fatigué. (He must be tired.) Full nuance of devoir across
  different tenses (should have, would have to) is covered in Part 4,
  Lesson 26.
- "Il y a" (there is/are, from Lesson 12) is invariable and doesn't change
  for singular/plural.
- "Il s'agit de" (it's a matter of / it's about) is an impersonal
  expression used to introduce a topic: Il s'agit d'un problème sérieux.
  (It's about a serious problem.) It's ALWAYS impersonal ("il"), never
  conjugated for a real subject.
- Verbs ending in -eindre and -aindre (peindre = to paint, craindre = to
  fear) follow their own irregular pattern: drop -indre, add -s, -s, -t,
  -gnons, -gnez, -gnent (note the "gn" appears only in the plural, and the
  singular has no d): je peins, tu peins, il peint, nous peignons, vous
  peignez, ils peignent.

CONJUGATION TABLE:
devoir (must/to have to): je dois, tu dois, il doit,
  nous devons, vous devez, ils doivent
peindre (to paint): je peins, tu peins, il peint,
  nous peignons, vous peignez, ils peignent

VOCABULARY:
devoir - must/to have to     il y a - there is/are
il s'agit de - it's about/a matter of
peindre - to paint            craindre - to fear
éteindre - to turn off/extinguish  atteindre - to reach/attain

EXAMPLES:
Tu dois étudier avant l'examen. - You must study before the exam.
Il y a un problème avec la voiture. - There's a problem with the car.
Ce livre s'agit de l'histoire de France. -- (INCORRECT — common mistake;
  correct form:) Il s'agit de l'histoire de France dans ce livre. - This
  book is about the history of France.
Elle peint un beau tableau. - She's painting a beautiful picture.
Il craint les araignées. - He's afraid of spiders.

NOTES:
- Common mistake: "il s'agit de" can NEVER take a real subject like a book
  or movie title as "il" — the "il" is always the impersonal "it," and the
  real subject is expressed with "dans" (in) if needed, as shown above.

PRACTICE:
Write three sentences using devoir to express obligations you have this
week.
