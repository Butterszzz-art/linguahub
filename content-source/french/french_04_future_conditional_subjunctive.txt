LESSON 24: The Futur Simple
================================================================

OVERVIEW:
The futur simple is the true "will do" future tense — more formal/written
than the futur proche (aller + infinitive) from Lesson 13, but essential
for plans, predictions, and promises.

GRAMMAR:
- Formation: infinitive (for -er and -ir verbs) or infinitive minus final
  -e (for -re verbs) + these endings, which are the SAME for every verb in
  French: -ai, -as, -a, -ons, -ez, -ont. (Notice these look like the
  present tense of avoir — a helpful memory trick.)
    parler -> je parlerai
    finir -> je finirai
    vendre -> je vendrai (drop the final -e first)
- Many common verbs have an irregular STEM (but still use the same regular
  endings): être -> ser-, avoir -> aur-, aller -> ir-, faire -> fer-,
  venir -> viendr-, voir -> verr-, savoir -> saur-, pouvoir -> pourr-,
  vouloir -> voudr-, devoir -> devr-, falloir -> il faudra (impersonal).
- Use futur simple for: predictions, promises, and formal plans, and
  ALWAYS after "quand/lorsque/dès que/aussitôt que" (when/as soon as) when
  the main clause is also about the future — unlike English, which uses
  present tense after "when" for future events: Quand j'arriverai, je
  t'appellerai. (When I arrive [lit. "will arrive"], I'll call you.)

CONJUGATION TABLE:
parler (to speak) — futur simple:
je parlerai         nous parlerons
tu parleras          vous parlerez
il/elle/on parlera    ils/elles parleront

être / avoir / aller / faire — futur simple stems:
je serai / j'aurai / j'irai / je ferai

VOCABULARY:
demain - tomorrow
la semaine prochaine - next week
un jour - someday
dès que / aussitôt que - as soon as
quand / lorsque - when

EXAMPLES:
L'année prochaine, nous irons en France. - Next year, we'll go to France.
Je te téléphonerai dès que j'arriverai. - I'll call you as soon as I
  arrive. (both verbs in futur simple, unlike English)
Il fera beau demain. - It will be nice out tomorrow.
Elle sera médecin dans cinq ans. - She will be a doctor in five years.

NOTES:
- Common mistake: using present tense after "quand" for a future event, as
  English does. In French this MUST be futur simple on both sides:
  "Quand je serai grand..." (When I grow up..., lit. "will be").

PRACTICE:
Write five predictions about your life in ten years using futur simple.

LESSON 25: The Futur Antérieur
================================================================

OVERVIEW:
The futur antérieur ("future perfect") expresses an action that WILL HAVE
happened before another future action or a specific future point in time.

GRAMMAR:
- Formation: futur simple of avoir or être (same helper-verb choice as
  passé composé) + past participle.
- Used to show that one future event will be completed before another
  future event: Quand tu arriveras, j'aurai déjà préparé le dîner. (When
  you arrive, I will have already prepared dinner.)
- Very commonly follows "quand/lorsque/dès que/après que" when the action
  needs to be presented as completed before the other future action:
  Dès que j'aurai fini, je te le dirai. (As soon as I've finished, I'll
  tell you.)
- Agreement rules for the past participle follow the same pattern as
  passé composé (Lesson 18–19).

CONJUGATION TABLE:
parler (to speak) — futur antérieur:
j'aurai parlé          nous aurons parlé
tu auras parlé           vous aurez parlé
il/elle/on aura parlé    ils/elles auront parlé

VOCABULARY:
dès que / aussitôt que - as soon as
après que - after
avant + [future time] - before

EXAMPLES:
Nous aurons fini le projet avant vendredi. - We will have finished the
  project before Friday.
Quand elle aura vingt-cinq ans, elle aura déjà voyagé partout. - By the
  time she's 25, she'll already have traveled everywhere.
Dès que j'aurai reçu ta lettre, je répondrai. - As soon as I've received
  your letter, I'll reply.

NOTES:
- The futur antérieur and futur simple often appear paired in the same
  sentence, one showing what happens first (futur antérieur), the other
  showing what happens as a result (futur simple).

PRACTICE:
Write two sentences using "dès que + futur antérieur, [main clause in
futur simple]".

LESSON 26: The Present Conditional
================================================================

OVERVIEW:
The conditional expresses "would" in French — used for politeness, wishes,
hypotheticals, and speculation.

GRAMMAR:
- Formation: same stem as the futur simple (including all the irregular
  stems from Lesson 24) + the imparfait endings: -ais, -ais, -ait, -ions,
  -iez, -aient.
    parler -> je parlerais    être -> je serais    avoir -> j'aurais
- Uses of the present conditional:
    1. Politeness: Je voudrais un café, s'il vous plaît. (I would like a
       coffee, please.) — much softer/more polite than "je veux."
    2. Hypothetical results (paired with "si + imparfait" in the "if"
       clause): Si j'avais de l'argent, je voyagerais. (If I had money, I
       would travel.)
    3. Speculation / unconfirmed information (common in news reporting):
       Le président serait en visite à Paris. (The president is
       reportedly visiting Paris — not confirmed.)
    4. Giving advice: À ta place, je partirais tout de suite. (In your
       place, I would leave right away.)

CONJUGATION TABLE:
parler (to speak) — present conditional:
je parlerais          nous parlerions
tu parlerais           vous parleriez
il/elle/on parlerait    ils/elles parleraient

VOCABULARY:
si + imparfait, ...conditionnel - if... , ...would...
je voudrais - I would like
à ta/votre place - in your place
il/elle devrait - he/she should

EXAMPLES:
Si j'étais riche, j'achèterais une maison à la mer. - If I were rich, I
  would buy a house by the sea.
Pourriez-vous m'aider, s'il vous plaît ? - Could you help me, please?
  (polite request using conditional of pouvoir)
Nous aimerions visiter le musée demain. - We would like to visit the
  museum tomorrow.
À votre place, je ne dirais rien. - In your place, I wouldn't say
  anything.

NOTES:
- The "si-clause" pattern is strict: si + IMPARFAIT in the "if" part
  pairs with CONDITIONAL in the result part. Never use conditional inside
  the "si" clause itself.

PRACTICE:
Complete: "Si je gagnais à la loterie, je..." with three different
conditional endings.

LESSON 27: The Past Conditional
================================================================

OVERVIEW:
The past conditional expresses "would have" — used for hypothetical
situations in the past that didn't actually happen, and for regrets.

GRAMMAR:
- Formation: present conditional of avoir or être (same helper-verb choice
  as usual) + past participle.
- Used for: 
    1. Hypothetical past results, paired with "si + plus-que-parfait":
       Si j'avais su, je serais venu. (If I had known, I would have come.)
    2. Regret or reproach: J'aurais dû t'appeler. (I should have called
       you. — see Lesson 28 for devoir nuances)
    3. Unconfirmed past information (reported speech): L'accident aurait
       été causé par la pluie. (The accident was reportedly caused by the
       rain.)
- Agreement rules for the past participle follow the passé composé
  pattern.

CONJUGATION TABLE:
parler (to speak) — past conditional:
j'aurais parlé          nous aurions parlé
tu aurais parlé           vous auriez parlé
il/elle/on aurait parlé    ils/elles auraient parlé

VOCABULARY:
si + plus-que-parfait, ...conditionnel passé - if (had)..., would have...
j'aurais dû - I should have
j'aurais aimé / voulu - I would have liked

EXAMPLES:
Si j'avais eu le temps, je t'aurais aidé. - If I had had time, I would
  have helped you.
Elle aurait aimé venir, mais elle était malade. - She would have liked to
  come, but she was sick.
Vous n'auriez pas dû partir si tôt. - You shouldn't have left so early.
Nous serions arrivés à l'heure sans les embouteillages. - We would have
  arrived on time if not for the traffic.

NOTES:
- The three-tense "if-chain" is a common exam/conversation stumbling
  block — memorize it as a set: si + présent -> présent/futur/impératif;
  si + imparfait -> conditionnel présent; si + plus-que-parfait ->
  conditionnel passé.

PRACTICE:
Write two "if only" regret sentences using past conditional + plus-que-
parfait.

LESSON 28: Could, Should, Would — Nuances of Devoir, Pouvoir, Vouloir
================================================================

OVERVIEW:
English "could/should/would" map onto French in several different ways
depending on tense and meaning — this lesson untangles the most common
sources of confusion for learners.

GRAMMAR:
- "Could" can mean:
    - ability in the past (imparfait of pouvoir): Je pouvais nager
      pendant des heures. (I could swim for hours — general past ability.)
    - managed to / succeeded (passé composé of pouvoir): J'ai pu finir à
      temps. (I was able to / managed to finish in time.)
    - a polite request or hypothetical (conditional of pouvoir):
      Pourrais-tu m'aider ? (Could you help me?)
- "Should" is almost always the conditional of devoir:
    present conditional (should, right now/going forward): Tu devrais
      étudier plus. (You should study more.)
    past conditional (should have, in the past — a missed obligation):
      Tu aurais dû étudier plus. (You should have studied more.)
- "Would" can mean:
    - conditional mood generally (hypothetical result): Je voyagerais si
      j'avais de l'argent. (I would travel if I had money.)
    - past habitual action — this is actually the IMPARFAIT, not
      conditional, despite English using "would": Quand j'étais jeune, je
      voyageais souvent. (When I was young, I would [used to] travel
      often.)
    - a polite wish (conditional of vouloir): Je voudrais un thé. (I
      would like a tea.)

CONJUGATION TABLE:
pouvoir (can/to be able to): je peux, tu peux, il peut,
  nous pouvons, vous pouvez, ils peuvent
  (irregular futur/conditional stem: pourr-)
vouloir (to want): je veux, tu veux, il veut,
  nous voulons, vous voulez, ils veulent
  (irregular futur/conditional stem: voudr-)

VOCABULARY:
pouvoir - can / to be able to
vouloir - to want
devoir - must / to have to
(see Lesson 17 for devoir's present tense)

EXAMPLES:
Quand j'étais enfant, je pouvais courir très vite. - When I was a child, I
  could run very fast. (ability, imparfait)
J'ai enfin pu lui parler hier. - I finally managed to talk to him
  yesterday. (achieved once, passé composé)
Vous devriez consulter un médecin. - You should see a doctor. (advice,
  present conditional)
J'aurais dû te le dire plus tôt. - I should have told you sooner. (regret,
  past conditional)
Chaque été, nous allions à la plage. - Every summer, we would go [used to
  go] to the beach. (habitual "would" = imparfait, NOT conditional)

NOTES:
- The single most common learner error: translating habitual past "would"
  with the conditional instead of the imparfait. Test: if "would" can be
  replaced with "used to," it's imparfait, not conditional.

PRACTICE:
Translate: "When I was young, I would visit my grandmother every Sunday."
/ "You should have called me." / "Could you pass the salt?"

LESSON 29: The Present Subjunctive
================================================================

OVERVIEW:
The subjunctive mood expresses subjectivity — wishes, doubts, emotions,
necessity, and opinions — rather than objective fact. It's triggered by
specific expressions, almost always after "que."

GRAMMAR:
- Formation (regular pattern for most verbs): take the ils/elles form of
  the present tense, drop -ent, and add: -e, -es, -e, -ent for je/tu/il/
  ils; for nous/vous, use the IMPARFAIT nous/vous forms instead (-ions,
  -iez). Example with parler (ils parlent -> parl-): que je parle, que tu
  parles, qu'il parle, que nous parlions, que vous parliez, qu'ils
  parlent.
- Several verbs have irregular subjunctive stems: être (que je sois),
  avoir (que j'aie), aller (que j'aille, but que nous allions), faire (que
  je fasse), pouvoir (que je puisse), savoir (que je sache), vouloir (que
  je veuille, but que nous voulions), falloir (qu'il faille).
- The subjunctive is triggered after "que" by expressions of:
    - Will/wish: vouloir que, souhaiter que, préférer que
    - Emotion: être content(e)/triste/surpris(e) que, avoir peur que
    - Doubt/possibility: douter que, il est possible que, il se peut que
    - Necessity/obligation: il faut que, il est nécessaire que
    - Certain conjunctions: bien que (although), pour que (so that), avant
      que (before), à moins que (unless), jusqu'à ce que (until)
- NOT used after expressions of certainty (je pense que, je suis sûr(e)
  que, il est évident que) — those take the indicative (normal tenses),
  UNLESS the sentence is negative or a question, which can introduce doubt.
- Only used when the subject of the "que" clause is DIFFERENT from the
  main subject. If the subject is the same, use an infinitive instead:
  Je veux partir (I want to leave — same subject) vs. Je veux que tu
  partes (I want you to leave — different subjects, subjunctive).

CONJUGATION TABLE:
parler (to speak) — present subjunctive:
que je parle          que nous parlions
que tu parles          que vous parliez
qu'il/elle/on parle     qu'ils/elles parlent

être / avoir — present subjunctive (irregular):
que je sois, que tu sois, qu'il soit, que nous soyons, que vous soyez,
  qu'ils soient
que j'aie, que tu aies, qu'il ait, que nous ayons, que vous ayez,
  qu'ils aient

VOCABULARY:
il faut que - it's necessary that / one must
vouloir que - to want (someone to do something)
avoir peur que - to be afraid that
bien que - although
avant que - before
pour que - so that

EXAMPLES:
Il faut que tu finisses tes devoirs. - You need to finish your homework.
Je suis content(e) que vous soyez ici. - I'm happy that you're here.
Bien qu'il pleuve, nous sortons. - Although it's raining, we're going out.
Je ne pense pas qu'elle ait raison. - I don't think she's right. (negative
  opinion -> subjunctive)

NOTES:
- Don't try to translate the subjunctive word-for-word from English (which
  barely uses it) — instead, learn the TRIGGER expressions above by heart,
  since the subjunctive is grammatically automatic once you recognize the
  trigger.

PRACTICE:
Complete five sentences starting with "Il faut que..." about things you
need to do this week.

LESSON 30: The Past Subjunctive
================================================================

OVERVIEW:
The past subjunctive expresses the same subjective ideas as the present
subjunctive (wish, doubt, emotion, necessity) but about an action that
happened BEFORE the time of the main verb.

GRAMMAR:
- Formation: present subjunctive of avoir or être (same helper-verb choice
  as usual) + past participle.
- Used with the same trigger expressions as the present subjunctive
  (Lesson 29), but when the action in the "que" clause is already
  completed relative to the main clause: Je suis content(e) que tu sois
  venu(e). (I'm glad you came — the coming happened before the being
  glad.)
- Agreement rules for the past participle follow the passé composé
  pattern.

CONJUGATION TABLE:
parler (to speak) — past subjunctive:
que j'aie parlé          que nous ayons parlé
que tu aies parlé          que vous ayez parlé
qu'il/elle/on ait parlé     qu'ils/elles aient parlé

aller (to go) — past subjunctive:
que je sois allé(e)          que nous soyons allé(e)s
que tu sois allé(e)           que vous soyez allé(e)(s)
qu'il soit allé / qu'elle soit allée   qu'ils soient allés / qu'elles soient
  allées

VOCABULARY:
(same trigger expressions as Lesson 29 — il faut que, avoir peur que,
bien que, etc. — used here about a completed past action)

EXAMPLES:
Je suis surpris(e) qu'elle ait déjà fini. - I'm surprised she's already
  finished.
Bien que nous soyons arrivés en retard, le spectacle n'avait pas commencé.
  - Although we arrived late, the show hadn't started.
Il est possible qu'il ait oublié notre rendez-vous. - It's possible he
  forgot our appointment.

NOTES:
- The past subjunctive is much less common than the present subjunctive in
  everyday speech — recognize it and use it when the timing genuinely
  matters, but don't stress over it in early practice.

PRACTICE:
Write two sentences using "je suis content(e) que" + past subjunctive
about something that already happened.
