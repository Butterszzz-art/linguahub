LESSON 28: Phone Calls and Telecommunicating
================================================================

OVERVIEW:
Phone etiquette has its own script in every language. This lesson covers
how Italians answer and open a phone call, ask who's calling, and handle
messages — plus the vocabulary for phones and phone cards.

GRAMMAR:
- Italians answer the phone with Pronto? (literally "ready?") rather than
  a greeting — this is universal and worth internalizing as a fixed
  phrase.
- To identify yourself on the phone, Italian uses essere (Lesson 9), not a
  direct "this is": Sono Marco. (This is Marco. — lit. "I am Marco.")
- To ask who's calling: Chi parla? (Who's speaking?) or, more formally,
  Con chi parlo? (With whom am I speaking?)
- To ask for someone: C'è [name]? (Is [name] there?) To say someone just
  stepped out: È appena uscito/a. (He/she just went out.)
- Leaving or requesting a message: Posso lasciare un messaggio? (Can I
  leave a message?) Le posso lasciare un messaggio? (Can I leave him/her
  a message? — formal, using the indirect object pronoun le)
- Asking to call back: Ti posso richiamare più tardi? (informal, "Can I
  call you back later?") La posso richiamare fra mezz'ora? (formal, "Can I
  call you back in half an hour?")

VOCABULARY:
pronto? (PROHN-toh) - hello? (phone only)
il cellulare (eel chehl-loo-LAH-reh) - cell phone
il numero di telefono (eel NOO-meh-roh dee teh-LEH-foh-noh) - phone number
chiamare (kyah-MAH-reh) - to call
richiamare (ree-kyah-MAH-reh) - to call back
lasciare un messaggio (lah-SHAH-reh oon mehs-SAHJ-joh) - to leave a message
attenda in linea (aht-TEHN-dah een LEE-neh-ah) - please hold
la segreteria telefonica (lah seh-greh-teh-REE-ah teh-leh-FOH-nee-kah) -
  voicemail
è in riunione (eh een ryoo-NYOH-neh) - he/she is in a meeting
richiamo fra mezz'ora (ree-KYAH-moh frah mehd-DZOH-rah) - I'll call back
  in half an hour

EXAMPLES:
Pronto? Sono Giorgio, c'è Simona? - PROHN-toh? SOH-noh JOHR-joh, cheh
  see-MOH-nah - Hello? This is Giorgio, is Simona there?
Mi dispiace, è in riunione. Posso lasciare un messaggio? - mee
  dees-PYAH-cheh, eh een ryoo-NYOH-neh. POHS-soh lah-SHAH-reh oon
  mehs-SAHJ-joh - I'm sorry, he's in a meeting. Can I leave a message?
Ti posso richiamare più tardi? - tee POHS-soh ree-kyah-MAH-reh pyoo
  TAHR-dee - Can I call you back later?
Con chi parlo, scusi? - kohn kee PAHR-loh, SKOO-zee - With whom am I
  speaking, excuse me?

NOTES:
- Answering with "Pronto?" instead of a name or greeting is a fixed
  cultural convention — using buongiorno as your very first word on
  answering the phone will sound unusual to a native speaker.
- Sono + [your name] is the standard way to identify yourself, mirroring
  how essere is used for identity throughout the language (Sono
  americano, Sono studente, Sono Marco).

PRACTICE:
Write a short phone dialogue: answer the phone, identify yourself, ask for
someone who isn't available, and leave a message.

LESSON 29: Talking About Your Job and the Workplace
================================================================

OVERVIEW:
Talking about work is common small talk once introductions are out of the
way. This lesson covers professions, workplace vocabulary, and the verb
lavorare (to work) alongside fare (to do) for describing what someone does
for a living.

GRAMMAR:
- To ask what someone does for work: Che lavoro fai/fa? (What work do you
  do? inf./form.) or Che cosa fai nella vita? (What do you do [for a
  living]?)
- To answer, Italian typically uses fare + the profession (with an
  article) or essere + the profession (without an article) — both are
  correct, with essere being slightly more formal/simple:
    Faccio l'insegnante. / Sono insegnante. (I'm a teacher.)
    Fa il medico. / È medico. (He/she is a doctor.)
- Many profession nouns have distinct masculine/feminine forms, following
  the same patterns as other nouns (Lesson 4): l'avvocato/l'avvocatessa
  (lawyer), il dottore/la dottoressa (doctor), l'infermiere/l'infermiera
  (nurse); some are invariable across gender, changing only the article:
  il/la dentista, il/la giornalista.
- Lavorare (to work) is a regular -are verb (Lesson 8): lavoro, lavori,
  lavora, lavoriamo, lavorate, lavorano. It pairs with per (for) or in
  (at/in) to describe where: Lavoro per un'azienda italiana. (I work for
  an Italian company.) Lavoro in un ufficio. (I work in an office.)

VOCABULARY:
il lavoro (eel lah-VOH-roh) - work, job
lavorare (lah-voh-RAH-reh) - to work
l'ufficio (loof-FEE-choh) - the office
l'insegnante (leen-seh-NYAHN-teh) - teacher
il medico/la dottoressa (eel MEH-dee-koh) - doctor
l'avvocato (lahv-voh-KAH-toh) - lawyer
l'ingegnere (leen-jeh-NYEH-reh) - engineer
il/la commesso/a (eel/lah kohm-MEHS-soh) - shop clerk
l'appuntamento (lahp-poon-tah-MEHN-toh) - appointment
il computer (eel kohm-POO-tehr) - computer

EXAMPLES:
Che lavoro fai? Faccio l'insegnante. - keh lah-VOH-roh fahy? FAHCH-choh
  leen-seh-NYAHN-teh - What do you do for work? I'm a teacher.
Lavoro in un ufficio dal lunedì al venerdì. - lah-VOH-roh een oon
  oof-FEE-choh dahl loo-neh-DEE ahl veh-nehr-DEE - I work in an office
  Monday through Friday.
Vorrei prendere un appuntamento. - vohr-RAY PREHN-deh-reh oon
  ahp-poon-tah-MEHN-toh - I'd like to make an appointment.
Mia sorella è avvocato a Milano. - MEE-ah soh-REHL-lah eh ahv-voh-KAH-toh
  ah mee-LAH-noh - My sister is a lawyer in Milan.

NOTES:
- Whichever pattern you use (fare + article, or essere without one), stay
  consistent — mixing them mid-sentence ("Sono l'insegnante") is the kind
  of small error that's easy to avoid once you notice it.
- Some profession words remain the same for men and women, distinguished
  only by the article (il dentista / la dentista) — don't force a
  feminine ending onto every profession noun.

PRACTICE:
Describe your own job or a family member's job, including where they work
and what days/hours.

LESSON 30: The Imperfetto — the Other Past Tense
================================================================

OVERVIEW:
Alongside the passato prossimo (Lessons 26-27), Italian has a second past
tense, the imperfetto, used for descriptions, habits, and ongoing states in
the past rather than single completed actions. Choosing correctly between
the two is one of the more nuanced parts of intermediate Italian.

GRAMMAR:
- The imperfetto is a simple (one-word) tense, formed by dropping the
  infinitive ending and adding a consistent set of endings — it's actually
  more regular than the passato prossimo, with only a few common
  irregular stems.
- -ARE endings: -avo, -avi, -ava, -avamo, -avate, -avano (parlare ->
  parlavo, parlavi, parlava, parlavamo, parlavate, parlavano)
- -ERE endings: -evo, -evi, -eva, -evamo, -evate, -evano (avere -> avevo,
  avevi, aveva, avevamo, avevate, avevano)
- -IRE endings: -ivo, -ivi, -iva, -ivamo, -ivate, -ivano (dormire ->
  dormivo, dormivi, dormiva, dormivamo, dormivate, dormivano)
- Two important irregular imperfetto stems: essere -> ero, eri, era,
  eravamo, eravate, erano; fare -> facevo, facevi, faceva, facevamo,
  facevate, facevano (fare uses its old Latin stem "face-" rather than the
  modern infinitive stem).
- USE the imperfetto for: ongoing or repeated past actions ("I used to..."
  / "I was ...ing"), descriptions of people, places, weather, age, or
  time in the past, and background/setting for a story.
- USE the passato prossimo for: a specific, completed action or event —
  something that happened once, at a defined point.
- The classic contrast: Mentre studiavo (imperfetto — ongoing background
  action), ha chiamato Marco (passato prossimo — the single event that
  interrupted it). (While I was studying, Marco called.)

CONJUGATION TABLE:
Pronoun     parlare (-are)   avere (-ere)   dormire (-ire)   essere (irreg.)
io          parlavo          avevo          dormivo          ero
tu          parlavi          avevi          dormivi          eri
lui/lei     parlava          aveva          dormiva          era
noi         parlavamo        avevamo        dormivamo        eravamo
voi         parlavate        avevate        dormivate        eravate
loro        parlavano        avevano        dormivano        erano

VOCABULARY:
da bambino/a (dah bahm-BEE-noh) - as a child
ogni giorno (OH-nyee JOHR-noh) - every day
sempre (SEHM-preh) - always
mentre (MEHN-treh) - while
di solito (dee SOH-lee-toh) - usually
tutti i giorni (TOOT-tee ee JOHR-nee) - every day
una volta (OO-nah VOHL-tah) - once, at one time
c'era/c'erano (CHEH-rah/CHEH-rah-noh) - there was/there were
quando (KWAHN-doh) - when
mentre ero (MEHN-treh EH-roh) - while I was

EXAMPLES:
Da bambino giocavo sempre in giardino. - dah bahm-BEE-noh joh-KAH-voh
  SEHM-preh een jahr-DEE-noh - As a child I always used to play in the
  garden.
Faceva bel tempo ieri quando siamo usciti. - fah-CHEH-vah behl TEHM-poh
  YEH-ree KWAHN-doh SYAH-moh oo-SHEE-tee - The weather was nice yesterday
  when we went out.
Mentre guardavo la TV, ha suonato il telefono. - MEHN-treh gwahr-DAH-voh
  lah tee-VOO, ah swoh-NAH-toh eel teh-LEH-foh-noh - While I was watching
  TV, the phone rang.
C'erano molte persone alla festa. - CHEH-rah-noh MOHL-teh pehr-SOH-neh
  AHL-lah FEHS-tah - There were a lot of people at the party.

NOTES:
- A simple test: if you can insert "used to" or "was/were ...ing" in
  English without changing the meaning, reach for the imperfetto; if the
  action is a single, complete event, use the passato prossimo instead.
- The imperfetto is genuinely one of the more regular tenses in Italian —
  once you know the six endings per verb type, you can apply them almost
  universally except for essere and fare.

PRACTICE:
Describe what you used to do on weekends as a child (three sentences,
imperfetto), then write one sentence combining both tenses correctly
(an ongoing action interrupted by a completed one).

LESSON 31: Reflexive Verbs and Taking a Tour
================================================================

OVERVIEW:
Reflexive verbs describe actions the subject does to or for themselves —
waking up, getting dressed, feeling a certain way — and they're extremely
common in everyday Italian. This lesson covers their formation and applies
them to talking about sightseeing and daily routines.

GRAMMAR:
- A reflexive verb pairs a normal verb conjugation with a reflexive
  pronoun that matches the subject: mi, ti, si, ci, vi, si. The pronoun
  goes directly before the conjugated verb.
- You've already used one reflexive verb: chiamarsi (Lesson 11). The
  pattern is the same for all reflexive verbs — for example, alzarsi (to
  get (oneself) up):
    io mi alzo, tu ti alzi, lui/lei si alza, noi ci alziamo, voi vi
    alzate, loro si alzano
- Common everyday reflexive verbs: svegliarsi (to wake up), alzarsi (to
  get up), lavarsi (to wash oneself), vestirsi (to get dressed), sentirsi
  (to feel — used for physical/emotional states, Lesson 16's "handling
  emergencies" vocabulary reuses this exact verb), divertirsi (to have
  fun, enjoy oneself), riposarsi (to rest).
- In the passato prossimo, ALL reflexive verbs take essere as their
  helping verb and the past participle agrees with the subject (Lesson
  27's agreement rule): Mi sono svegliata alle sette. (I woke up at seven.
  — female speaker)
- Reflexive verbs are useful for describing a guided tour or excursion:
  fermarsi (to stop, pause), fare un giro (to take a ride/tour — not
  reflexive, but frequently paired with sightseeing), divertirsi (to enjoy
  oneself).

CONJUGATION TABLE:
Pronoun     alzarsi (present)   divertirsi (present)   sentirsi (present)
io          mi alzo             mi diverto              mi sento
tu          ti alzi             ti diverti              ti senti
lui/lei     si alza             si diverte              si sente
noi         ci alziamo          ci divertiamo           ci sentiamo
voi         vi alzate           vi divertite            vi sentite
loro        si alzano           si divertono            si sentono

VOCABULARY:
svegliarsi (zveh-LYAHR-see) - to wake up
alzarsi (ahl-TSAHR-see) - to get up
lavarsi (lah-VAHR-see) - to wash oneself
vestirsi (vehs-TEER-see) - to get dressed
divertirsi (dee-vehr-TEER-see) - to have fun, enjoy oneself
riposarsi (ree-poh-ZAHR-see) - to rest
fare un giro (FAH-reh oon JEE-roh) - to take a ride/tour
fermarsi (fehr-MAHR-see) - to stop
la guida (lah GWEE-dah) - the guide, guidebook
la visita guidata (lah VEE-zee-tah gwee-DAH-tah) - guided tour

EXAMPLES:
Mi sveglio sempre alle sei e mi alzo subito. - mee ZVEH-lyoh SEHM-preh
  AHL-leh sey eh mee AHL-tsoh SOO-bee-toh - I always wake up at six and get
  up right away.
Ci siamo divertiti molto durante la visita guidata. - chee SYAH-moh
  dee-vehr-TEE-tee MOHL-toh doo-RAHN-teh lah VEE-zee-tah gwee-DAH-tah - We
  had a lot of fun during the guided tour.
Vuoi fare un giro in centro? - VWOI FAH-reh oon JEE-roh een CHEHN-troh -
  Do you want to take a ride/walk downtown?
Ci fermiamo un momento a fare fotografie. - chee fehr-MYAH-moh oon
  moh-MEHN-toh ah FAH-reh foh-toh-grah-FEE-eh - Let's stop for a moment to
  take pictures.

NOTES:
- Don't drop the reflexive pronoun out of habit — "Alzo alle sette" is
  incomplete; the pronoun (mi) is a required part of the verb, not an
  optional add-on.
- Remember reflexive verbs always take essere in compound tenses, with
  full subject agreement on the participle — this connects directly back
  to Lesson 27.

PRACTICE:
Describe your typical morning routine using at least four reflexive verbs,
then narrate the same routine from yesterday using the passato prossimo.

LESSON 32: Sports and Hobbies
================================================================

OVERVIEW:
Talking about free time and hobbies uses a specific set of verbs that
don't always match English one-to-one — particularly the difference
between playing a sport and playing an instrument. This lesson sorts out
the vocabulary and structures for talking about what you enjoy doing.

GRAMMAR:
- Italian uses different verbs for different kinds of "playing":
    giocare a + [sport] -> to play a sport: gioco a calcio (I play
      soccer), gioco a tennis (I play tennis)
    suonare + [instrument] -> to play an instrument: suono il piano (I
      play piano), suono la chitarra (I play guitar)
  Never use suonare for a sport, or giocare for an instrument — this is a
  common false-friend-style trap for English speakers used to one verb
  ("play") for both.
- Fare is used for many other athletic/hobby activities, especially ones
  without a ball: faccio jogging, faccio yoga, fare nuoto (to swim/do
  swimming), fare una passeggiata (to take a walk, from Lesson 9's fare
  idioms).
- To express liking an activity, use piacere (to be pleasing to — Lesson
  33's future tense lesson will build on this construction further): Mi
  piace il calcio. (I like soccer — lit. "soccer is pleasing to me.") Mi
  piace nuotare. (I like swimming/to swim — with an infinitive following).
  For plural things you like: Mi piacciono gli sport. (I like sports.)

VOCABULARY:
giocare a (joh-KAH-reh ah) - to play (a sport)
suonare (swoh-NAH-reh) - to play (an instrument)
il calcio (eel KAHL-choh) - soccer
il tennis (eel TEHN-nees) - tennis
il nuoto (eel NWOH-toh) - swimming
la chitarra (lah kee-TAHR-rah) - guitar
il tempo libero (eel TEHM-poh LEE-beh-roh) - free time
lo sport (loh spohrt) - sport
mi piace/piacciono (mee PYAH-cheh/PYAHCH-choh-noh) - I like (sg./pl.)
l'hobby (LOHB-bee) - hobby

EXAMPLES:
Gioco a calcio ogni domenica con gli amici. - JOH-koh ah KAHL-choh OH-nyee
  doh-MEH-nee-kah kohn lyee ah-MEE-chee - I play soccer with friends every
  Sunday.
Mia sorella suona il violino molto bene. - MEE-ah soh-REHL-lah SWOH-nah
  eel vyoh-LEE-noh MOHL-toh BEH-neh - My sister plays the violin very
  well.
Mi piace fare jogging la mattina. - mee PYAH-cheh FAH-reh JOHG-ging lah
  maht-TEE-nah - I like to go jogging in the morning.
Che cosa fai nel tempo libero? - keh KOH-zah fahy nehl TEHM-poh
  LEE-beh-roh - What do you do in your free time?

NOTES:
- The giocare/suonare split is worth drilling deliberately — the error of
  saying "suono a calcio" or "gioco il piano" is common but easy to fix
  once you've practiced the pattern a few times.
- Piacere agrees with the THING liked, not with the person who likes it —
  this reverses the English structure and is worth extra attention when
  you formally study indirect object pronouns later.

PRACTICE:
List three sports you play, one instrument (real or imagined), and one
fare-based hobby, using the correct verb for each.

LESSON 33: Planning a Trip and the Future Tense
================================================================

OVERVIEW:
This lesson introduces the simple future tense — used for plans,
predictions, and promises — in the context of planning a trip: deciding
where and when to go, and booking travel.

GRAMMAR:
- The future tense (futuro semplice) is formed by dropping the final -e
  of the infinitive and adding a consistent set of endings. -ARE verbs
  additionally change their a to e before adding the ending.
- Endings for all three verb types: -ò, -ai, -à, -emo, -ete, -anno
    parlare -> parlerò, parlerai, parlerà, parleremo, parlerete,
      parleranno (note the -are -> -er- shift)
    prendere -> prenderò, prenderai, prenderà, prenderemo, prenderete,
      prenderanno
    partire -> partirò, partirai, partirà, partiremo, partirete,
      partiranno
- Several very common verbs have irregular future STEMS (the endings stay
  the same, only the stem changes): essere -> sarò, sarai, sarà, saremo,
  sarete, saranno; avere -> avrò, avrai, avrà, avremo, avrete, avranno;
  andare -> andrò, andrai, andrà, andremo, andrete, andranno; fare ->
  farò, farai, farà, faremo, farete, faranno; venire -> verrò, verrai,
  verrà, verremo, verrete, verranno.
- The future is also used, distinctively from English, to express
  probability or a guess about the present: Sarà tardi. (It's probably
  late. / It must be late.) Avrà trent'anni. (He's probably thirty.)
- Everyday Italian often uses the present tense for near-future plans
  instead of the future tense (Parto domani — I'm leaving tomorrow), the
  same way English can say "I'm leaving tomorrow." Reserve the true
  future for more distant plans, predictions, or formal contexts.

CONJUGATION TABLE:
Pronoun     parlare      prendere       partire       essere    avere
io          parlerò      prenderò       partirò       sarò      avrò
tu          parlerai     prenderai      partirai      sarai     avrai
lui/lei     parlerà      prenderà       partirà       sarà      avrà
noi         parleremo    prenderemo     partiremo     saremo    avremo
voi         parlerete    prenderete     partirete     sarete    avrete
loro        parleranno   prenderanno    partiranno    saranno   avranno

VOCABULARY:
il viaggio (eel VYAHJ-joh) - the trip
prenotare (preh-noh-TAH-reh) - to book, reserve
il volo (eel VOH-loh) - the flight
la vacanza (lah vah-KAHN-tsah) - the vacation
partire (pahr-TEE-reh) - to leave, depart
l'agenzia di viaggi (lah-jehn-TSEE-ah dee VYAHJ-jee) - travel agency
la meta (lah MEH-tah) - destination
tra... giorni (trah JOHR-nee) - in ... days
il prossimo anno (eel PROHS-see-moh AHN-noh) - next year
fra poco (frah POH-koh) - soon

EXAMPLES:
L'anno prossimo andremo in Sicilia. - LAHN-noh PROHS-see-moh ahn-DREH-moh
  een see-CHEE-lyah - Next year we'll go to Sicily.
Prenoterò il volo domani. - preh-noh-teh-ROH eel VOH-loh doh-MAH-nee -
  I'll book the flight tomorrow.
Che tempo farà in agosto? - keh TEHM-poh fah-RAH een ah-GOHS-toh - What
  will the weather be like in August?
Sarà una vacanza indimenticabile. - sah-RAH OO-nah vah-KAHN-tsah
  een-dee-mehn-tee-KAH-bee-leh - It will be an unforgettable vacation.

NOTES:
- Don't try to guess future stems from the infinitive for irregular verbs
  — essere, avere, andare, fare, and venire all need to be memorized
  directly, the same way their present-tense forms did.
- Watch for the "probability" use of the future — Sarà in ufficio (He's
  probably at the office) is not about a future event at all, and this
  nuance often surprises English speakers.

PRACTICE:
Write four sentences about a future trip: where you'll go, when you'll
leave, what you'll do there, and a guess (using the future of probability)
about the weather.

LESSON 34: Money, Banking, and Currency
================================================================

OVERVIEW:
Handling money — exchanging currency, using an ATM, understanding your
bank statement — requires its own practical vocabulary. This lesson covers
banking transactions and Italy's currency, the euro.

GRAMMAR:
- Common banking verbs: cambiare valuta (to exchange currency), prelevare
  (to withdraw), versare (to deposit), aprire un conto (to open an
  account), riscuotere un assegno (to cash a check). Most of these are
  regular -are or -ere verbs following the patterns from Lesson 8.
- To ask for something politely at a bank counter, the conditional vorrei
  (Lesson 19) works here too: Vorrei cambiare dei dollari in euro. (I'd
  like to change some dollars into euros.)
- Italy's currency is the euro (l'euro) — notably, the word is invariable:
  un euro, due euro (never "due euri"). Smaller units are i centesimi
  (cents).
- Useful questions: Quant'è il cambio? (What's the exchange rate?) Dov'è
  il bancomat più vicino? (Where's the nearest ATM?) Posso pagare con la
  carta di credito? (Can I pay by credit card?)

VOCABULARY:
la banca (lah BAHN-kah) - the bank
il bancomat (eel BAHN-koh-maht) - the ATM
cambiare (kahm-BYAH-reh) - to change, exchange
prelevare (preh-leh-VAH-reh) - to withdraw
i contanti (ee kohn-TAHN-tee) - cash
la carta di credito (lah KAHR-tah dee KREH-dee-toh) - credit card
il conto (eel KOHN-toh) - the account, the bill
l'euro (LEH-oo-roh) - the euro
gli spiccioli (lyee SPEECH-choh-lee) - small change
il cambio (eel KAHM-byoh) - the exchange rate

EXAMPLES:
Vorrei cambiare duecento dollari in euro. - vohr-RAY kahm-BYAH-reh
  doo-eh-CHEHN-toh DOHL-lah-ree een EH-oo-roh - I'd like to change two
  hundred dollars into euros.
Dov'è il bancomat più vicino? - doh-VEH eel BAHN-koh-maht pyoo
  vee-CHEE-noh - Where's the nearest ATM?
Posso pagare con la carta di credito? - POHS-soh pah-GAH-reh kohn lah
  KAHR-tah dee KREH-dee-toh - Can I pay with a credit card?
Mi dispiace, non ho spiccioli. - mee dees-PYAH-cheh, nohn oh
  SPEECH-choh-lee - I'm sorry, I don't have any change.

NOTES:
- Remember that "euro" never takes a plural -i or -s ending — cento euro,
  not "cento euri."
- Cash is still widely used and often preferred in parts of Italy, even
  though cards are accepted almost everywhere in cities — don't assume a
  card will always work, especially at smaller shops or markets.

PRACTICE:
Role-play exchanging $150 for euros at a bank counter, asking the exchange
rate and confirming the amount you'll receive.

LESSON 35: Getting Around — Airports, Customs, Car Rental, and Public Transportation
================================================================

OVERVIEW:
This lesson bundles the vocabulary you need to physically get from place
to place in Italy: through the airport, past customs, renting a car if
needed, and using trains, buses, and taxis.

GRAMMAR:
- Airport vocabulary and check-in phrases: fare il check-in (to check in),
  il volo (flight), l'imbarco (boarding), il bagaglio (luggage), la carta
  d'imbarco (boarding pass).
- Customs: la dogana (customs). Common question: Ha qualcosa da
  dichiarare? (Do you have anything to declare?)
- Renting a car uses noleggiare (to rent, for vehicles specifically —
  distinct from affittare, used for renting apartments/rooms): Vorrei
  noleggiare una macchina. (I'd like to rent a car.)
- Public transportation: il treno (train), l'autobus (bus), il tram
  (tram), il taxi (taxi), la fermata (the stop). Before boarding many
  Italian buses and trams, you must validate (obliterare/convalidare) your
  ticket in a small machine on board — an unvalidated ticket, even a
  purchased one, can result in a fine.
- To ask about schedules and platforms: A che ora parte il treno per
  Firenze? (What time does the train for Florence leave?) Da che binario?
  (From which platform?)

VOCABULARY:
il volo (eel VOH-loh) - the flight
la carta d'imbarco (lah KAHR-tah deem-BAHR-koh) - boarding pass
il bagaglio (eel bah-GAH-lyoh) - luggage
la dogana (lah doh-GAH-nah) - customs
noleggiare (noh-lehj-JAH-reh) - to rent (a vehicle)
il treno (eel TREH-noh) - the train
il binario (eel bee-NAH-ryoh) - the platform, track
l'autobus (LOU-toh-boos) - the bus
la fermata (lah fehr-MAH-tah) - the stop
convalidare/obliterare il biglietto (kohn-vah-lee-DAH-reh) - to validate
  the ticket

EXAMPLES:
A che ora parte il volo per Roma? - ah keh OH-rah PAHR-teh eel VOH-loh
  pehr ROH-mah - What time does the flight for Rome leave?
Ha qualcosa da dichiarare? No, niente. - ah kwahl-KOH-zah dah
  dee-kyah-RAH-reh? noh, NYEHN-teh - Do you have anything to declare? No,
  nothing.
Vorrei noleggiare una macchina per una settimana. - vohr-RAY
  noh-lehj-JAH-reh OO-nah MAHK-kee-nah pehr OO-nah seht-tee-MAH-nah - I'd
  like to rent a car for a week.
Non dimenticare di convalidare il biglietto! - nohn dee-mehn-tee-KAH-reh
  dee kohn-vah-lee-DAH-reh eel bee-LYEHT-toh - Don't forget to validate
  the ticket!

NOTES:
- Ticket validation is a real and commonly-missed step for visitors —
  buying a ticket alone is not enough on many regional trains, buses, and
  trams; look for the small validation machine and stamp your ticket
  before or immediately after boarding.
- Remember noleggiare (rent a vehicle) vs. affittare (rent a room/
  apartment) — they aren't interchangeable, unlike English "rent," which
  covers both.

PRACTICE:
Write a short dialogue at a train station: ask what time and from which
platform the train to Venice leaves, then buy and mention validating your
ticket.

================================================================
LESSON 36: Finding a Place to Stay and the Imperative Mood
================================================================

OVERVIEW:
This closing lesson of Part 4 covers booking and checking into
accommodation, and introduces the imperative mood — the form Italian uses
for commands, instructions, and requests, which you'll hear constantly
from hotel staff, tour guides, and recipes alike.

GRAMMAR:
- Lodging vocabulary: l'albergo/l'hotel (hotel), una camera singola/
  doppia (a single/double room), la prenotazione (reservation), la
  reception (front desk).
- The imperative is used to give commands, instructions, and (in its
  polite Lei form) formal requests. Its endings differ from the present
  tense mainly in the tu and Lei forms:
    -ARE verbs (parlare): (tu) parla!, (Lei) parli!, (noi) parliamo!,
      (voi) parlate!, (loro) parlino!
    -ERE verbs (prendere): (tu) prendi!, (Lei) prenda!, (noi) prendiamo!,
      (voi) prendete!, (loro) prendano!
    -IRE verbs (partire): (tu) parti!, (Lei) parta!, (noi) partiamo!,
      (voi) partite!, (loro) partano!
  Notice the tu and Lei forms are the ones that most sharply differ from
  the present tense — the -are tu form ends in -a (not -i as in the
  present), and the Lei forms across all conjugations borrow endings from
  the "opposite" verb family (-are verbs get -i, -ere/-ire verbs get -a).
- The negative tu imperative uses non + INFINITIVE, not the normal
  negative construction: Non parlare! (Don't speak!) Non aprire la
  porta! (Don't open the door!) All other persons simply add non before
  the normal imperative: Non parli! (Lei), Non parlate! (voi).
- Several verbs have irregular imperative tu forms worth knowing: andare
  -> va'/vai!, dare -> da'/dai!, dire -> di'!, fare -> fa'/fai!, stare ->
  sta'/stai! (all also have a regular alternate form).
- You'll hear the plural voi imperative constantly on signs and in group
  instructions (chiamate!, aspettate!), and the formal Lei imperative from
  hotel staff, waitstaff, and officials (Mi dia un documento, per favore
  — Give me an ID, please).

CONJUGATION TABLE:
Pronoun     parlare!      prendere!      partire!
(tu)        parla!        prendi!        parti!
(Lei)       parli!        prenda!        parta!
(noi)       parliamo!     prendiamo!     partiamo!
(voi)       parlate!      prendete!      partite!
(loro)      parlino!      prendano!      partano!

VOCABULARY:
l'albergo (lahl-BEHR-goh) - the hotel
la camera singola/doppia (lah KAH-meh-rah SEEN-goh-lah/DOHP-pyah) - single/
  double room
la prenotazione (lah preh-noh-tah-TSYOH-neh) - reservation
la reception (lah reh-SEHP-shohn) - front desk
il documento (eel doh-koo-MEHN-toh) - ID document
la chiave (lah KYAH-veh) - the key
compilare (kohm-pee-LAH-reh) - to fill out (a form)
firmare (feer-MAH-reh) - to sign
aspetti! (ahs-PEHT-tee) - wait! (formal command)
mi dia... (mee DEE-ah) - give me... (formal command, from dare)

EXAMPLES:
Vorrei una camera doppia per due notti. - vohr-RAY OO-nah KAH-meh-rah
  DOHP-pyah pehr DOO-eh NOHT-tee - I'd like a double room for two nights.
Mi dia un documento, per favore, e compili questo modulo. - mee DEE-ah
  oon doh-koo-MEHN-toh, pehr fah-VOH-reh, eh kohm-PEE-lee KWEHS-toh
  MOH-doo-loh - Give me an ID, please, and fill out this form.
Aspetti un momento, controllo la prenotazione. - ahs-PEHT-tee oon
  moh-MEHN-toh, kohn-TROHL-loh lah preh-noh-tah-TSYOH-neh - Wait a
  moment, I'll check the reservation.
Non dimenticare la chiave! - nohn dee-mehn-tee-KAH-reh lah KYAH-veh -
  Don't forget the key!

NOTES:
- Because Lei imperatives borrow the "opposite family" ending (-are verbs
  take -i, -ere/-ire verbs take -a), they can momentarily look like a
  different verb entirely — Parli! (Lei, from parlare) looks like a tu
  present-tense form of a different verb, so context matters.
- The negative tu command switching to the infinitive (Non parlare!, not
  "Non parli" for tu) is a genuinely irregular quirk worth memorizing as a
  fixed rule rather than reasoning through each time.

PRACTICE:
Give five commands you might hear or give during a hotel check-in, using a
mix of tu, Lei, and voi forms, including at least one negative command.
