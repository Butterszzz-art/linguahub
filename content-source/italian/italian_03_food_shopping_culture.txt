LESSON 19: Eating and Drinking, Italian Style
================================================================

OVERVIEW:
Food culture in Italy runs on its own rhythm and rituals — especially
around coffee. This lesson introduces the three daily meals, the drink
vocabulary you'll use constantly, and the unwritten rules around ordering
espresso like a local.

GRAMMAR:
- Italians structure the day around three main meals: la colazione
  (breakfast), il pranzo (lunch), and la cena (dinner), with uno spuntino
  (a snack) possible in between.
- The verb prendere (to take) does double duty for ordering food and
  drink, the way "to have" works in English: Prendo un caffè. (I'll have
  a coffee.) It conjugates as a regular -ere verb (Lesson 8).
- The conditional form vorrei (I would like) is the polite, standard way
  to order anything — much softer than voglio (I want): Vorrei un
  cappuccino, per favore. You'll study the conditional formally later;
  for now, treat vorrei as a fixed polite phrase.
- Quantities when ordering drinks use di (of): una bottiglia di... (a
  bottle of...), un bicchiere di... (a glass of...), mezzo litro di...
  (half a liter of...).

VOCABULARY:
la colazione (lah koh-lah-TSYOH-neh) - breakfast
il pranzo (eel PRAHN-tsoh) - lunch
la cena (lah CHEH-nah) - dinner
uno spuntino (OO-noh spoon-TEE-noh) - a snack
un caffè (oon kahf-FEH) - an espresso
un cappuccino (oon kahp-poot-CHEE-noh) - a cappuccino
un caffè macchiato (mahk-KYAH-toh) - espresso with a touch of milk
un caffelatte (kahf-feh-LAHT-teh) - espresso with plenty of milk
acqua minerale (AHK-kwah mee-neh-RAH-leh) - mineral water
vorrei... (vohr-RAY) - I would like...

EXAMPLES:
Prendo un caffè, grazie. - PREHN-doh oon kahf-FEH, GRAH-tsee-eh - I'll
  have an espresso, thanks.
Vorrei un cappuccino e una pasta. - vohr-RAY oon kahp-poot-CHEE-noh eh OO-
  nah PAHS-tah - I'd like a cappuccino and a pastry.
A che ora fai colazione? - ah keh OH-rah fahy koh-lah-TSYOH-neh - What
  time do you have breakfast?
Un bicchiere d'acqua naturale, per favore. - oon beek-KYEH-reh
  DAHK-kwah nah-too-RAH-leh, pehr fah-VOH-reh - A glass of still water,
  please.

NOTES:
- Ordering "un caffè" always gets you an espresso — Italy has no concept
  of the oversized "coffee to go" cup, and asking for "un caffè grande"
  will likely get you a puzzled look.
- Cappuccino is considered strictly a morning/breakfast drink; ordering
  one after roughly 11 a.m. — especially after a meal — marks you
  instantly as a tourist (more in Lesson 44).

PRACTICE:
Plan a full day of eating in Italian: name what you'd order for
colazione, pranzo, uno spuntino, and cena.

LESSON 20: Dining Out — Reservations, Ordering, and Paying
================================================================

OVERVIEW:
This lesson walks through a restaurant visit start to finish: reserving a
table, understanding the course structure of an Italian meal, and asking
for and paying the bill — including a few customs that differ sharply from
North America.

GRAMMAR:
- To reserve a table, use vorrei + prenotare (to reserve): Vorrei
  prenotare un tavolo per due, per favore. (I'd like to reserve a table
  for two, please.)
- A full Italian meal traditionally moves through courses in this order:
  antipasto (appetizer) -> primo (first course — pasta, risotto, or soup)
  -> secondo (second course — meat or fish) -> contorno (side dish/
  vegetable, ordered separately) -> dolce (dessert). You are never
  obligated to order every course.
- To ask for the bill, use portare (to bring) or fare (to make/do):
  Ci porta il conto, per favore? (Will you bring us the bill, please? —
  formal) or simply Il conto, per favore! (The bill, please!) — the server
  will not bring it unheeded; you must ask.
- Restaurant vocabulary you'll need: il cameriere/la cameriera (waiter/
  waitress), il tavolo (table), il menù (menu), il conto (the bill).

VOCABULARY:
prenotare (preh-noh-TAH-reh) - to reserve
il tavolo (eel TAH-voh-loh) - the table
per quante persone? (pehr KWAHN-teh pehr-SOH-neh) - for how many people?
l'antipasto (lahn-tee-PAHS-toh) - the appetizer
il primo (piatto) (eel PREE-moh PYAHT-toh) - the first course
il secondo (piatto) (eel seh-KOHN-doh) - the second course (meat/fish)
il contorno (eel kohn-TOHR-noh) - the side dish
il dolce (eel DOHL-cheh) - dessert
il conto (eel KOHN-toh) - the bill
il coperto (eel koh-PEHR-toh) - cover charge

EXAMPLES:
Vorrei prenotare un tavolo per quattro, per favore. - vohr-RAY
  preh-noh-TAH-reh oon TAH-voh-loh pehr KWAHT-troh, pehr fah-VOH-reh - I'd
  like to reserve a table for four, please.
Come primo prendo le lasagne, come secondo il pollo. - KOH-meh PREE-moh
  PREHN-doh leh lah-ZAH-nyeh, KOH-meh seh-KOHN-doh eel POHL-loh - For my
  first course I'll have lasagna, for my second the chicken.
Il conto, per favore! - eel KOHN-toh, pehr fah-VOH-reh - The bill, please!
Si paga anche il coperto. - see PAH-gah AHN-keh eel koh-PEHR-toh - There's
  also a cover charge.

NOTES:
- Tipping is not customary in Italy — the coperto (a small per-person cover
  charge) covers bread and table service instead, and it appears on nearly
  every restaurant bill; it isn't a mistake or a scam.
- Servers consider it rude to bring the bill before you ask for it — they
  won't rush you, so you need to actively request il conto when you're
  ready to leave.

PRACTICE:
Role-play a restaurant reservation call (name, party size, time), then
order a full meal naming one dish for each course.

LESSON 21: Breakfast, Lunch, and Grocery Shopping Vocabulary
================================================================

OVERVIEW:
Building on Lesson 19's meal structure, this lesson fills in concrete food
vocabulary — for breakfast pastries, classic pasta shapes, and shopping at
specialty food shops like the butcher, fishmonger, and bakery.

GRAMMAR:
- At specialty shops, quantities are usually asked for by weight: un etto
  (100 grams, Lesson 17), mezzo chilo (half a kilo), un chilo (a kilo). Ask
  with Vorrei + [quantity] + di + [item]: Vorrei mezzo chilo di prosciutto.
- Pasta comes in dry, factory-made forms (pasta secca — spaghetti, penne,
  fusilli, rigatoni) and fresh, often homemade forms (pasta fresca /
  pasta all'uovo — tagliatelle, fettuccine). "Al dente" (to the tooth)
  describes pasta cooked just firm enough to have some bite.
- Common specialty shops: la macelleria (the butcher's), la pescheria (the
  fishmonger's), la panetteria (the bakery), l'alimentari (the small
  general grocery). Many Italians still shop at these instead of — or in
  addition to — il supermercato.

VOCABULARY:
il pane (eel PAH-neh) - bread
la marmellata (lah mahr-mehl-LAH-tah) - jam
il prosciutto (eel proh-SHOOT-toh) - cured ham
il pollo (eel POHL-loh) - chicken
la carne (lah KAHR-neh) - meat
il pesce (eel PEH-sheh) - fish
gli spaghetti (lyee spah-GEHT-tee) - spaghetti
al dente (ahl DEHN-teh) - firm to the bite (pasta doneness)
la frutta (lah FROOT-tah) - fruit
la verdura (lah vehr-DOO-rah) - vegetables

EXAMPLES:
Vorrei un etto di prosciutto e mezzo chilo di pane. - vohr-RAY oon
  EHT-toh dee proh-SHOOT-toh eh MEHD-dzoh KEE-loh dee PAH-neh - I'd like
  100 grams of prosciutto and half a kilo of bread.
Preferisco la pasta fresca alla pasta secca. - preh-feh-REES-koh lah
  PAHS-tah FREHS-kah AHL-lah PAHS-tah SEHK-kah - I prefer fresh pasta to
  dried pasta.
La pasta dev'essere al dente. - lah PAHS-tah deh-VEHS-seh-reh ahl DEHN-teh
  - The pasta needs to be al dente.
Vado in macelleria a comprare il pollo. - VAH-doh een mah-chehl-leh-REE-
  ah ah kohm-PRAH-reh eel POHL-loh - I'm going to the butcher's to buy
  chicken.

NOTES:
- "Al dente" isn't just restaurant jargon — it's the standard, expected
  way pasta is cooked throughout Italy; soft, overcooked pasta is
  considered a mistake, not a preference.
- When shopping by weight, un etto (100g) is a genuinely common everyday
  unit at delis — don't default only to grams or kilos when ordering.

PRACTICE:
Write a short shopping list in Italian for a simple pasta dinner (pasta
type, sauce ingredients, a side, and dessert), specifying quantities.

LESSON 22: Fruits, Vegetables, and the Open-Air Market
================================================================

OVERVIEW:
Open-air markets (il mercato) remain a common way to buy fresh produce in
Italy. This lesson focuses on fruit and vegetable vocabulary and the
phrases you'll need to shop for them.

GRAMMAR:
- Most fruit and vegetable names follow the regular gender patterns from
  Lesson 4 (-a/-e feminine, -o/-i masculine), so you can predict most
  plurals once you know the singular and its gender.
- To ask if something is fresh or in season: È fresco/a? (Is it fresh?) È
  di stagione? (Is it in season?)
- Vendors will often ask Quanto ne vuole? (How much/many would you like?)
  — answer with a quantity + di: due chili di pomodori (two kilos of
  tomatoes), tre mele (three apples, using the item directly when it's a
  countable number rather than weight).

VOCABULARY:
la mela (lah MEH-lah) - apple
la banana (lah bah-NAH-nah) - banana
l'arancia (lah-RAHN-chah) - orange
la fragola (lah FRAH-goh-lah) - strawberry
il limone (eel lee-MOH-neh) - lemon
il pomodoro (eel poh-moh-DOH-roh) - tomato
la carota (lah kah-ROH-tah) - carrot
la cipolla (lah chee-POHL-lah) - onion
l'insalata (leen-sah-LAH-tah) - lettuce, salad
i funghi (ee FOON-gee) - mushrooms

EXAMPLES:
Vorrei due chili di pomodori maturi. - vohr-RAY DOO-eh KEE-lee dee
  poh-moh-DOH-ree mah-TOO-ree - I'd like two kilos of ripe tomatoes.
Sono fresche queste fragole? - SOH-noh FREHS-keh KWEHS-teh FRAH-goh-leh -
  Are these strawberries fresh?
Mi dà anche una cipolla e due carote? - mee dah AHN-keh OO-nah chee-POHL-
  lah eh DOO-eh kah-ROH-teh - Could you also give me an onion and two
  carrots?
Il mercato è aperto la mattina. - eel mehr-KAH-toh eh ah-PEHR-toh lah
  maht-TEE-nah - The market is open in the morning.

NOTES:
- Haggling is not customary at Italian food markets, unlike some shopping
  contexts abroad — prices are fixed, even in open-air settings.
- Many fruit and vegetable words are near-cognates or easy to remember
  once you see the pattern (banana, limone/lemon-like, tomato/pomodoro is
  a rarer exception worth memorizing directly).

PRACTICE:
Practice asking for five different fruits or vegetables by specific
quantity (some by weight, some by count).

LESSON 23: Clothes Shopping — Sizes, Colors, and Trying Things On
================================================================

OVERVIEW:
Italy is famous for fashion, and shopping for clothes involves its own
vocabulary for sizes, colors, fabrics, and the phrases used in a fitting
room. This lesson also reviews how color adjectives agree (or don't) with
the noun they describe.

GRAMMAR:
- Colors behave in three different ways for agreement, which is worth
  knowing at a glance:
    Fully agreeing (four endings, like Lesson 6's Type 1 adjectives):
      rosso/a/i/e, giallo/a/i/e, bianco/a/i/e, nero/a/i/e, grigio/a/i/e,
      azzurro/a/i/e
    Agreeing only in number (Type 2, -e/-i): verde/verdi
    Invariable (never change at all, regardless of gender or number):
      blu, rosa, marrone, viola, beige, arancione
- Clothing sizes (la taglia) and shoe sizes (il numero) are different
  words in Italian — don't mix them up: Che taglia porta? (What size do
  you wear? — clothes) vs. Che numero porta? (What size do you wear? —
  shoes).
- To ask to try something on: Posso provare...? (May I try on...?) In a
  dressing room, i camerini.
- Useful fit vocabulary: stretto/a (tight), largo/a (loose), lungo/a
  (long), corto/a (short) — all Type 1, four-ending adjectives.

VOCABULARY:
la camicia (lah kah-MEE-chah) - shirt
la maglia (lah MAH-lyah) - sweater
i pantaloni (ee pahn-tah-LOH-nee) - pants
la gonna (lah GOHN-nah) - skirt
il vestito (eel vehs-TEE-toh) - dress, suit
le scarpe (leh SKAHR-peh) - shoes
la taglia (lah TAH-lyah) - clothing size
il numero (eel NOO-meh-roh) - shoe size
stretto/a (STREHT-toh) - tight
largo/a (LAHR-goh) - loose

EXAMPLES:
Posso provare questa gonna blu? - POHS-soh proh-VAH-reh KWEHS-tah GOHN-nah
  bloo - May I try on this blue skirt?
Che taglia porta? La quarantadue. - keh TAH-lyah POHR-tah? lah
  kwah-rahn-tah-DOO-eh - What size do you wear? A 42.
È troppo stretta; ha una taglia più grande? - eh TROHP-poh STREHT-tah; ah
  OO-nah TAH-lyah pyoo GRAHN-deh - It's too tight; do you have a bigger
  size?
Cerco una giacca sportiva blu e pantaloni bianchi. - CHEHR-koh OO-nah
  JAHK-kah spohr-TEE-vah bloo eh pahn-tah-LOH-nee BYAHN-kee - I'm looking
  for a casual blue jacket and white pants.

NOTES:
- Italian clothing sizes don't map 1:1 to US/UK sizes (an Italian 42 is
  roughly a US 6 in women's dresses) — when shopping for real, check a
  conversion chart rather than assuming your home size.
- Remember the three-way agreement split for colors above: getting rosso
  right (agrees fully) and blu right (never changes) both matter for
  sounding natural.

PRACTICE:
Describe an outfit using at least four clothing items and four different
colors, making sure each color agrees correctly (or doesn't, if
invariable).

LESSON 24: Going Out — Movies, Theater, Museums, and Festivals
================================================================

OVERVIEW:
Italians enjoy an active cultural and social life — outdoor festivals,
local sagre celebrating regional food, film, and theater. This lesson
gives you the vocabulary and questions to plan an evening out.

GRAMMAR:
- To ask what's happening or what's playing: Cosa c'è da fare di sera?
  (What is there to do in the evenings?) Cosa danno? (What's playing? —
  literally "what are they showing," used for movies)
- To ask about tickets and timing: Dove si comprano i biglietti? (Where do
  you buy tickets?) A che ora comincia lo spettacolo? (What time does the
  show start?) Ci sono ancora posti? (Are there still seats available?)
- Suggesting an activity together uses the noi form of andare as a
  question, without needing a separate "let's" word: Andiamo al cinema?
  (Shall we go to the movies?) — the noi form alone carries the "let's"
  meaning.
- Common venues: il cinema (movie theater), il teatro (theater), il museo
  (museum), la sagra (a local food/harvest festival), il concerto
  (concert).

VOCABULARY:
lo spettacolo (loh speht-TAH-koh-loh) - the show
il biglietto (eel bee-LYEHT-toh) - the ticket
cominciare (koh-meen-CHAH-reh) - to start
finire (fee-NEE-reh) - to end, finish
il cinema (eel CHEE-neh-mah) - the movies, cinema
il teatro (eel teh-AH-troh) - the theater
il museo (eel moo-ZEH-oh) - the museum
la sagra (lah SAH-grah) - local festival (harvest/food/patron saint)
l'attore/l'attrice (laht-TOH-reh/laht-TREE-cheh) - actor/actress
divertente (dee-vehr-TEHN-teh) - fun, amusing

EXAMPLES:
Andiamo al cinema stasera? - ahn-DYAH-moh ahl CHEE-neh-mah stah-SEH-rah -
  Shall we go to the movies tonight?
A che ora comincia lo spettacolo? Alle nove. - ah keh OH-rah koh-MEEN-
  chah loh speht-TAH-koh-loh? AHL-leh NOH-veh - What time does the show
  start? At nine.
Ci sono ancora biglietti per il concerto? - chee SOH-noh AHN-koh-rah
  bee-LYEHT-tee pehr eel kohn-CHEHR-toh - Are there still tickets for the
  concert?
Il film è così divertente! - eel feelm eh koh-ZEE dee-vehr-TEHN-teh - The
  movie is so much fun!

NOTES:
- "Andiamo...?" as a question is a natural, common way to propose doing
  something together — you don't need a separate word for "let's," the
  noi verb form does that work.
- American films shown in Italy are usually dubbed (doppiati) rather than
  subtitled; asking Cosa danno in lingua originale? (What's playing in the
  original language?) helps if you want to watch something undubbed.

PRACTICE:
Plan an evening out: propose an activity with "Andiamo...?", ask what
time it starts, and ask if tickets are still available.

LESSON 25: Making, Accepting, and Declining Invitations
================================================================

OVERVIEW:
This lesson rounds out Part 3's social vocabulary with the phrases for
inviting someone to do something, and for accepting or gracefully
declining an invitation yourself.

GRAMMAR:
- To invite someone, you can use Ti va di + infinitive (informal, "Do you
  feel like...?") or Vuoi + infinitive (Do you want to...?): Ti va di
  uscire stasera? (Do you feel like going out tonight?)
- To accept enthusiastically: Volentieri! (Gladly!) Con piacere! (With
  pleasure!) Perché no? (Why not?)
- To decline politely, softening with mi dispiace (I'm sorry) is standard:
  Mi dispiace, non posso. (I'm sorry, I can't.) Purtroppo ho un altro
  impegno. (Unfortunately I have another commitment.)
- To suggest an alternative time: Possiamo rimandare a un'altra volta?
  (Can we postpone to another time?) or Che ne dici di domani? (What do
  you say to tomorrow?)

VOCABULARY:
ti va di...? (tee vah dee) - do you feel like...? (informal)
volentieri! (voh-lehn-TYEH-ree) - gladly!
con piacere (kohn pyah-CHEH-reh) - with pleasure
mi dispiace (mee dees-PYAH-cheh) - I'm sorry
purtroppo (poor-TROHP-poh) - unfortunately
un impegno (oon eem-PEH-nyoh) - a commitment, engagement
un'altra volta (oo-NAHL-trah VOHL-tah) - another time
che ne dici di...? (keh neh DEE-chee dee) - what do you say to...?
naturalmente (nah-too-rahl-MEHN-teh) - of course, naturally
magari! (mah-GAH-ree) - if only!, I'd love to!

EXAMPLES:
Ti va di andare al mare domenica? - tee vah dee ahn-DAH-reh ahl MAH-reh
  doh-MEH-nee-kah - Do you feel like going to the beach on Sunday?
Volentieri, a che ora? - voh-lehn-TYEH-ree, ah keh OH-rah - Gladly, what
  time?
Mi dispiace, purtroppo ho già un impegno. - mee dees-PYAH-cheh,
  poor-TROHP-poh oh jah oon eem-PEH-nyoh - I'm sorry, unfortunately I
  already have a commitment.
Che ne dici di rimandare a sabato? - keh neh DEE-chee dee ree-mahn-DAH-
  reh ah SAH-bah-toh - What do you say to postponing to Saturday?

NOTES:
- "Magari!" is a very common, enthusiastic one-word response — it means
  something closer to "I wish!" or "I'd love that!" rather than a literal
  "maybe."
- Softening a decline with mi dispiace + purtroppo is the polite Italian
  norm; a flat "no, I can't" without any cushioning can come across as
  abrupt.

PRACTICE:
Write a short dialogue: propose an outing, have the other person decline
once with a reason, then successfully agree on an alternative time.

LESSON 26: The Passato Prossimo — Formation with Avere
================================================================

OVERVIEW:
This is your first past tense, and the one you'll use most often to talk
about completed actions — "I ate," "we studied," "she called." It's a
compound tense built from two parts, and this lesson covers the larger
group of verbs that form it with avere.

GRAMMAR:
- The passato prossimo ("recent past") corresponds to both the English
  simple past (I spoke) and present perfect (I have spoken) — Italian
  doesn't distinguish between them the way English does.
- It's a compound tense with two parts: a helping verb (either avere or
  essere, conjugated in the present tense) + the past participle of the
  main verb.
- Most verbs use avere as their helping verb. Regular past participles are
  formed from the infinitive stem:
    -are verbs -> -ato: parlare -> parlato
    -ere verbs -> -uto: vivere -> vivuto (though many -ere verbs are
      irregular here — see below)
    -ire verbs -> -ito: partire -> partito, capire -> capito
- Full example with parlare: ho parlato, hai parlato, ha parlato, abbiamo
  parlato, avete parlato, hanno parlato. (I spoke/have spoken, you
  spoke..., etc.)
- Many common -ere verbs have IRREGULAR past participles that must be
  memorized individually. Some of the most frequent:
    fare (to do/make) -> fatto        Ho fatto una passeggiata.
    dire (to say/tell) -> detto        Ha detto la verità.
    leggere (to read) -> letto         Abbiamo letto il libro.
    scrivere (to write) -> scritto     Hanno scritto una lettera.
    prendere (to take) -> preso        Ho preso un caffè.
    mettere (to put) -> messo          Ha messo la giacca.
    vedere (to see) -> visto           Hai visto il film?
    aprire (to open) -> aperto         Ho aperto la porta.
- With avere-verbs, the past participle does NOT change to agree with the
  subject — it stays in its base -o form regardless of who's speaking
  (Maria ha parlato, just like Marco ha parlato).

CONJUGATION TABLE:
Pronoun     avere + participle example (parlare -> parlato)
io          ho parlato
tu          hai parlato
lui/lei     ha parlato
noi         abbiamo parlato
voi         avete parlato
loro        hanno parlato

VOCABULARY:
ieri (YEH-ree) - yesterday
la settimana scorsa (lah seht-tee-MAH-nah SKOHR-sah) - last week
già (jah) - already
non ... ancora (nohn AHN-koh-rah) - not ... yet
mai (my) - never, ever
sempre (SEHM-preh) - always
appena (ahp-PEH-nah) - just, just now
qualche volta (KWAHL-keh VOHL-tah) - sometimes
stamattina (stah-maht-TEE-nah) - this morning
stasera (stah-SEH-rah) - tonight, this evening

EXAMPLES:
Ieri ho mangiato un gelato buonissimo. - YEH-ree oh mahn-JAH-toh oon
  jeh-LAH-toh bwoh-NEES-see-moh - Yesterday I ate a delicious ice cream.
Hai già visto quel film? - AH-ee jah VEES-toh kwehl feelm - Have you
  already seen that film?
Non ho ancora finito i compiti. - nohn oh AHN-koh-rah fee-NEE-toh ee
  KOHM-pee-tee - I haven't finished my homework yet.
Abbiamo letto due libri questo mese. - ahb-BYAH-moh LEHT-toh DOO-eh
  LEE-bree KWEHS-toh MEH-zeh - We've read two books this month.

NOTES:
- Because irregular participles are so common among high-frequency verbs
  (fare, dire, leggere, scrivere, prendere, vedere), memorize them as
  vocabulary items in their own right rather than assuming a regular -uto
  ending.
- Time expressions like ieri, già, ancora, and mai are your strongest
  clue that a sentence needs the passato prossimo — notice them and let
  them cue the tense.

PRACTICE:
Put these into the passato prossimo: io (mangiare) la pizza; tu (leggere)
il giornale; noi (fare) una passeggiata; loro (vedere) un film.

LESSON 27: The Passato Prossimo — Formation with Essere and Agreement
================================================================

OVERVIEW:
A smaller but very frequent group of verbs — mostly verbs of motion and
state-change — form the passato prossimo with essere instead of avere.
This changes one important rule: with essere, the past participle DOES
agree with the subject, like an adjective.

GRAMMAR:
- Verbs that take essere as their helper are mostly verbs of motion,
  staying, or change of state. The most common ones to memorize: andare
  (to go), venire (to come), partire (to leave), arrivare (to arrive),
  entrare (to enter), uscire (to go out), tornare (to return), stare (to
  stay/be), nascere (to be born), morire (to die), diventare (to become),
  rimanere (to remain). Reflexive verbs (Lesson 31) also always take
  essere.
- With essere, the past participle behaves like an adjective and must
  agree in gender and number with the SUBJECT:
    Marco è andato al mare. (Marco went to the beach. — masc. sg.)
    Maria è andata al mare. (Maria went to the beach. — fem. sg.)
    I ragazzi sono andati al mare. (The boys/mixed group went. — masc.
      pl.)
    Le ragazze sono andate al mare. (The girls went. — fem. pl.)
- A handy way to remember which group a verb belongs to: many essere-verbs
  can be arranged into the classic "House of essere" mnemonic — verbs of
  movement in and out (entrare/uscire), up and down (salire/scendere),
  arriving and leaving (arrivare/partire), staying and becoming (stare/
  diventare/rimanere), being born and dying (nascere/morire), going and
  coming (andare/venire), being and seeming (essere/sembrare).
- Some irregular essere-verb participles: essere -> stato (yes, essere
  itself uses stato, which doubles as the participle for stare too);
  nascere -> nato; morire -> morto; venire -> venuto; rimanere -> rimasto.

venire -> venuto (veh-NOO-toh) - came, come
partire -> partito (pahr-TEE-toh) - left, departed

uscire -> uscito (oo-SHEE-toh) - went out

EXAMPLES:
Sono arrivato stamattina. - SOH-noh ahr-ree-VAH-toh stah-maht-TEE-nah - I
  arrived this morning. (male speaker)
A che ora sei tornata ieri sera? - ah keh OH-rah sey tohr-NAH-tah YEH-ree
  SEH-rah - What time did you get back last night? (asking a female)
Siamo usciti insieme sabato. - SYAH-moh oo-SHEE-tee een-SYEH-meh SAH-bah-
  toh - We went out together on Saturday. (mixed/male group)
Dove sei nata? Sono nata a Napoli. - DOH-veh sey NAH-tah? SOH-noh NAH-tah
  ah NAH-poh-lee - Where were you born? I was born in Naples. (female
  speaker)

NOTES:
- The single most useful shortcut: if a verb describes moving somewhere,
  staying somewhere, or changing state (arriving, leaving, being born,
  dying, becoming), it's very likely an essere-verb — check that instinct
  against the list above as you build vocabulary.
- Don't forget the agreement once you've picked essere — this is the most
  common error at this stage, since avere-verbs (Lesson 26) never
  changed the participle, and it's tempting to keep that habit here too.
